
**1. Internal Energy and Temperature**

- **Internal Energy:** An energy store consisting of the **total kinetic energy** (random motion of particles) and the **total potential energy** (between particles) in a system.
- **Temperature Relationship:** Heating increases the internal energy store.
    - If temperature increases, the **average kinetic energy** of the particles increases.
    - During a state change, the temperature remains constant while the **internal potential energy** changes.
- **SI Unit:** Joule (**J**).

**2. Heat Capacity and Specific Heat Capacity**
[[Physics formulas]]
- **Heat Capacity (**C**):** The change in internal energy per unit change in temperature.
    - **Formula:** 
$$
    C=\frac{Q}{Δθ}​
$$
    - **Units:** J/K or J/∘C,  Δθ = change in temperature, Q= Joules required, c =heat capacity, m =  mass
    - **Factors:** Depends on the **mass** and the **material** of the substance.
- **Specific Heat Capacity (**c**):** The change in internal energy per unit mass for each unit change in temperature.
    - **Formula:**
$$
    c=\frac{c}{m}=\frac{Q}{mΔθ}
$$
    - **Units:** J/(kg K) or J/(kg ∘C).
- **Key Relationship (Warming/Cooling):**
    - Bodies with **high specific heat capacity** warm up and cool down **slower** than those with lower capacity (e.g., water).
    - Water is an ideal coolant because it can absorb large amounts of thermal energy with only a small temperature increase.

**3. Phase Changes and Latent Heat**
[[Physics formulas]]
- **State Changes:** Processes like melting, solidification, boiling, and condensation occur at a **constant temperature**.
- **Latent Heat (**L**):** Energy absorbed or released to change the state of a substance without changing its temperature.
- **Specific Latent Heat (**l**):** Energy transferred **per unit mass** to change the state at a constant temperature.
    - **Fusion (**l**):** Solid ↔ Liquid. **Formula:** <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">L​=ml</mark> (melting or solidification)
    - **Vaporization (**l​**):** Liquid ↔ Gas. **Formula:** <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">L​=ml</mark>​
- **Molecular Behavior:** During boiling/melting, energy is used to **increase the average separation** of molecules (breaking/weakening bonds), not their speed.

**4. Boiling vs. Evaporation**

|Feature|**Boiling**|**Evaporation**|
|---|---|---|
|**Temperature**|Occurs at a particular temperature (boiling point).|Occurs at **any** temperature.|
|**Location**|Takes place **throughout** the liquid (bubbles form).|Occurs only at the **liquid surface**.|
|**Speed**|Relatively fast.|Relatively slow.|
|**Thermal Source**|External heat source required.|No external heat source required.|

- **Evaporation Cooling:** Faster (higher KE) molecules escape the surface; the remaining molecules have a **lower average kinetic energy**, causing the liquid temperature to decrease.
- **Factors Increasing Evaporation Rate:** Higher temperature, lower humidity, larger surface area, reduced atmospheric pressure, and increased air movement.

**5. Exam Calculation Formulas & Logic**

- **Thermal Energy from a Heater:** Q=P⋅t (Power × time).
- **Temperature Change:** Q=mcΔθ.
- **State Change:** Q=ml.
- **Mixed Systems:** Energy required to change temp + change state (e.g., warming water + boiling it).
- **Heating/Cooling Curves:** **Flat regions** on a temperature-time graph indicate a mixture of states (melting or boiling) where temperature is constant.
- **Experimental Error:** Calculated heat capacity may be higher than the actual value if heat is lost to the surroundings or the container. Stirring is necessary to ensure **uniform temperature**.
