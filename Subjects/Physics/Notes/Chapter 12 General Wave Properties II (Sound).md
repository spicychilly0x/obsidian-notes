
**1. Nature and Production of Sound**

- **Production:** Sound is a form of energy transmitted as a wave, originating from vibrating sources.
- **Medium Required:** Sound is a **mechanical wave** and **requires a medium** to travel. It cannot travel in a vacuum because there are no particles to transmit the vibration.
- **Longitudinal Nature:** Sound is a **longitudinal wave**. Medium particles vibrate **parallel** to the direction of wave travel.
    - _Comparison:_ Light waves are transverse waves and do not require a medium for transmission.
- **Speed in Different Media:** The speed of sound depends on the **properties of the medium**.
    - Sound travels **fastest in tightly packed solids** and **slowest in gases** where particle structures are in random motion.
    - _Key Speed Reference Values:_
        - **Glass/Solids:** $\approx 5000\text{ m/s}$
        - **Water/Liquids:** $\approx 1200\text{ m/s}$
        - **Air/Gases:** $\approx 330\text{ m/s}$

**2. Sound as a Pressure Wave**

- **The Mechanism (e.g., Tuning Fork):**
    - As a prong stretches **outward**, it compresses surrounding air molecules, producing a high-pressure region called a **compression**.
    - As the prong moves **inward**, it leaves a low-pressure region called a **rarefaction**.
    - Continuous vibration creates an alternating pattern of high- and low-pressure regions moving through the medium.
- **Exam Warning:** A pressure-time graph of a sound wave looks like a transverse wave with peaks and troughs. **Do not conclude that sound is a transverse wave.**

**3. Key Wave Terms**

- **Wavelength (**$\lambda$**):** The distance between two successive compressions or successive rarefactions.
    - The distance from a compression to the immediate next rarefaction is **half a wavelength (**$\frac{1}{2}\lambda$**)**.
- **Frequency (**$f$**):** The number of complete oscillations in 1 second. Unit is Hertz (Hz).
- **Intensity Unit:** The intensity (loudness) of sound is measured in **decibels (dB)**.

**4. Pitch vs. Frequency & Loudness vs. Amplitude**

- **Pitch & Frequency:** Pitch is directly related to frequency. High pitch = high frequency; low pitch = low frequency.
    - _Example:_ Soprano singers stretch their vocal cords so they vibrate more quickly to create higher frequency and higher pitch.
- **Stretched Strings (Factors affecting frequency):**
    1. **Length (**$L$**):** Frequency is inversely proportional to length ($f \propto \frac{1}{L}$).
    2. **Tension (**$T$**):** Frequency is directly proportional to the square root of tension ($f \propto \sqrt{T}$). Making a string more taut increases tension.
    3. **Linear Mass Density (**$m$**):** Frequency is inversely proportional to the square root of mass per unit length ($f \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}$). This factor changes when using different materials or thicknesses.
    4. _Relationship formula:_ $f = \frac{1}{2L}\sqrt{\frac{T}{m}}$
- **Loudness & Amplitude:** Loudness is the sensation of different intensities of sound. **Larger amplitude = louder sound**.
- **Wave Energy:** Affected by both amplitude and frequency.
    - High amplitude = high energy.
    - Higher frequency transmits **more energy per second** because more waves pass by in a given period of time.

**5. Range of Audibility and Ultrasound**

- **Audible Frequency Range (Humans):** Approximately **20 Hz to 20,000 Hz (20 kHz)**.
- **Infrasound:** Sound with a frequency **below 20 Hz**.
- **Ultrasound:** Sound with a frequency **above 20,000 Hz** (audible to some animals like bats/dolphins, but inaudible to humans).
- **High-Yield Uses of Ultrasound:**
    - **Pre-natal scanning:** Uses echo sounding to safely image soft tissues.
    - **Sonar:** Reflects ultrasound off the sea floor to measure seabed depth.
    - **Medical therapy:** Breaking down kidney stones.
    - **Cleaning:** Vibrates dirt off objects without causing structural damage.

**6. Echoes, Echolocation, and Reverberation**

- **Echo:** Sound heard after the reflection of sound waves from hard, flat surfaces (e.g., a cliff or wall).
- **Echolocation:** Locating objects using reflected sound.
    - _Examples:_ Bats/dolphins finding prey; car reversing sensors beep faster as the bumper gets closer to an object.
- **Reverberation:** A prolonged sound caused by the merging of many echoes reflecting off the walls and ceilings of a large room like an auditorium.

**7. Essential Exam Math & Calculations**

- **Calculating Echo Distance:** $$\text{Total distance travelled } (2d) = \text{Speed } (v) \times \text{Time } (t)$$ $$\text{Distance to reflecting surface } (d) = \frac{v \times t}{2}$$
    - _Warning:_ Always divide by 2 when a sound travels to a wall/seabed and reflects back to the observer or receiver.
- **Wavelengths in a Pulse:** $$\text{Number of complete wavelengths} = \text{Frequency } (f) \times \text{Duration of Pulse } (t)$$
    - _Example:_ A pulse lasting $0.50\text{ s}$ at a frequency of $3600\text{ Hz}$ contains $3600 \times 0.50 = 1800$ complete wavelengths.
- **Consecutive Clapping Experiment:** If a person claps at the exact instant they hear an echo and repeats this $N$ times over a total time $t$ at a distance $d$ from a wall: $$\text{Speed of Sound } (v) = \frac{2 \times d \times N}{t}$$
