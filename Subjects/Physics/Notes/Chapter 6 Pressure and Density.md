

**1. Density (**ρ**)**

- **Definition:** Mass per unit volume.
- **Formula:** ρ=Vm​, where m is mass and V is volume.
- **Units:** SI unit is kg/m3; common unit is g/cm3.
- **Floating vs. Sinking:** Objects with a lower density than the liquid will float; those with a higher density will sink.
- **Key Relationships:**
    - **Temperature:** Density decreases as temperature increases because objects expand (increase in volume).
    - **Pressure:** Density increases as pressure increases.
- **Mixtures:** Combined density = Total VolumeTotal Mass​.

**2. Pressure in Solids (**P**)**

- **Definition:** Force acting per unit area.  
- **Formula:** <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">P=AF</mark>​.      P = pressure, A = area, F = force    [[Physics formulas]]
- **Units:** SI unit is **Pascal (**Pa**)** or N/m2.
- **Exam Relationships:**
    - **Maximum Pressure:** Occurs when the force is applied to the **minimum surface area**.
    - **Minimum Pressure:** Occurs when the force is applied to the **maximum surface area**.
    - **Relationship:** Pressure is directly proportional to force and inversely proportional to area.

**3. Pressure in Liquids**

- **Formula:** <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">P=hρg</mark>.  [[Physics formulas]]
    - h = vertical height of liquid column (m).
    - ρ = density of liquid (kg/m3).
    - g = gravitational field strength (10 N/kg on Earth).
- **Key Properties:**
    - **Independence:** Liquid pressure depends **only** on vertical depth (h) and density (ρ); it is independent of the container's shape, volume, or cross-sectional area.
    - **Equilibrium:** At the same vertical height within the same liquid, the pressure is identical.
    - **Direction:** Fluid pressure is directed in **all directions**.

**4. Pascal’s Principle and Hydraulics**

- **Principle:** Pressure applied to an enclosed incompressible liquid is transmitted **undiminished** to all parts of the liquid.

- **Hydraulic Press Formula:** A1​F1​​=A2​F2​​.
    - Allows a small effort (F1​) on a small area (A1​) to magnify into a large force (F2​) on a large area (A2​).
    ![[Pasted image 20260831215843.png]]
- **Conservation of Energy:** F1​×d1​=F2​×d2​.
    - Work done is the same on both sides; the smaller force must move through a larger distance.
- **Volume Displacement:** A1​d1​=A2​d2​ (the volume of liquid moved from one side equals the volume gained on the other).

**5. Atmospheric Pressure and Barometers**

- **Atmospheric Pressure (**P0​**):** Caused by the Earth's gravitational pull on air molecules; at sea level, it is approximately 1.013×105 Pa (or 1 atm).
- **Altitude:** Pressure decreases at higher altitudes due to reduced gravitational pull and lower air temperature.
- **Barometer:** An instrument measuring atmospheric pressure.
    - Mercury (Hg) is used because it is the densest liquid at room temperature, allowing for a manageable tube height.
    - Standard atmospheric pressure can support 760 mm Hg or 76 cm Hg.
- **Factors Affecting Barometric Height:** Altitude, temperature, humidity, and the presence of trapped air or moisture in the tube.
- **Design Independence:** Barometric height (h) remains constant even if the tube is tilted or has a different diameter.

**6. Manometers**

- **Definition:** An instrument used to measure the **difference** in pressure between two points.
- **Calculating Gas Pressure (**Pgas​**):**
    - If gas pushes the liquid down on its side: Pgas​=P0​+hρg.
    - If atmospheric pressure pushes the liquid down on the gas side: Pgas​=P0​−hρg.
- **Relationship:** If a denser liquid is used, the height difference (h) decreases.
