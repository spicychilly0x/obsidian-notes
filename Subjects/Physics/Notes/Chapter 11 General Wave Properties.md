
**1. Fundamental Concepts**

- **Wave Motion:** A disturbance or periodic motion that **transfers energy** from one point to another with **no net transport of matter**.
- **Energy Transfer Mechanism:**
    - **Electromagnetic Waves:** Vibrations of electric and magnetic fields.
    - **Sound Waves:** Vibrations of particles in a medium (solid, liquid, or gas).
    - **Water Waves:** Vibration of water particles.
    - **Energy Conversion:** In water waves, particles exchange **kinetic energy** for **potential energy** as they oscillate between rest positions and crests/troughs.

**2. Types of Waves**

- **Transverse Waves:** Direction of vibration is **perpendicular** to the direction of wave travel.
    - **Examples:** All Electromagnetic (EM) waves (Light, X-rays, Radio waves), water waves, waves on a rope.
- **Longitudinal Waves:** Direction of vibration is **parallel** to the direction of wave travel.
    - **Examples:** Sound waves, certain slinky spring waves.

**3. Key Wave Terms & Definitions**

- **Amplitude:** Maximum displacement of a particle from its rest position; determined by the energy of vibration.
- **Wavelength (**λ**):** Shortest distance between two consecutive points **in phase** (e.g., crest to crest, trough to trough, or compression to compression).
- **Period (**T**):** Time taken for one complete oscillation or for one complete waveform to be produced.
- **Frequency (**f**):** Number of complete oscillations/waveforms produced per second.
    - **Unit:** Hertz (Hz).
- **Wave Speed (**v**):** Distance travelled by a wave per second.
- **Crests/Troughs:** Highest and lowest points of a transverse wave, respectively.
- **Wavefront:** An imaginary line joining adjacent points that are in the same state of vibration (in phase).
- **Pitch:** Sensation of frequency in sound; high pitch = high frequency.

**4. Formulas and Relationships**
[[Physics formulas]]
- **Frequency-Period Relationship:** <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">f=1/T</mark>​.
- **The Wave Equation:** <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">v=fλ</mark>.
- **Must-Memorize Relationship:** When a wave passes from one medium to another (e.g., deep to shallow water), its **frequency remains constant**.
    - If speed (v) increases, wavelength (λ) must also increase.

**5. Phase and Particle Motion**

- **In Phase:** Points with the same displacement and moving in the same direction (0∘ or 360∘ difference).
- **Anti-Phase:** Points with the same displacement magnitude but moving in **opposite directions** (180∘ difference).
- **Predicting Motion:** To determine where a particle moves next, draw a "future" wave slightly shifted in the direction of travel. Observe if the particle must move up/down (transverse) or left/right (longitudinal) to meet the new line.

**6. Graphical Interpretation (Exam Focus)**

- **Displacement-Distance Graph:** Shows a snapshot of the entire wave at one instant; used to find **Amplitude** and **Wavelength**.
- **Displacement-Time Graph:** Shows the motion of a single point over time; used to find **Amplitude** and **Period**.
- **Refraction in Ripple Tanks:**
    - Water waves travel **slower** in **shallow water**.
    - When entering shallower water, wavelength **decreases** and the wave bends toward the normal.
