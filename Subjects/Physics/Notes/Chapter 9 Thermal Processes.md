
**1. Fundamental Concepts**

- **Thermal Energy:** The total kinetic energy of the atoms or molecules in a body.
- **Direction of Flow:** Energy always transfers from a region of **higher temperature** to a region of **lower temperature**.
- **Thermal Equilibrium:** A state where two or more objects in contact reach the **same temperature**, resulting in **no net transfer of energy**.

**2. Conduction**

- **Definition:** Transfer of thermal energy through a medium without any bulk flow of the medium.
- **Medium Requirements:** Occurs in solids (fastest), liquids, and gases (slowest).
- **Non-Metal Mechanism (Molecular Vibration):**
    - Particles at the hot end gain kinetic energy and vibrate more vigorously.
    - They collide with less energetic neighbors, transferring energy along the object.
- **Metal Mechanism (Electron Diffusion):**
    - Metals are far better conductors due to **free electrons**.
    - When heated, free electrons gain kinetic energy and move rapidly toward cooler regions.
    - Electrons collide with atoms in cooler parts, passing on energy much faster than vibration alone.
- **Factors Affecting Rate:** Material type, geometry (dimensions), and the temperature difference across the medium.

**3. Convection**

- **Definition:** Transfer of thermal energy by means of **convection currents** in a fluid (liquid or gas) due to **density differences**.
- **The Mechanism (Must Memorize):**
    1. Fluid near the heat source is **heated and expands**.
    2. Expanded fluid becomes **less dense** than the surrounding fluid and **rises**.
    3. Cooler, denser fluid sinks to take its place, creating a continuous loop (convection current).
- **Key Fact:** Convection **cannot** occur in solids because particles are not free to move.

**4. Radiation**

- **Definition:** Transfer of thermal energy via **electromagnetic waves** (infrared) without the need for a material medium. It can occur in a **vacuum**.
- **Emission vs. Absorption:** All objects emit and absorb infrared radiation.
- **Factors Affecting Rate:**
    - **Surface Color & Texture:** Dull, black, and rough surfaces are the **best emitters and absorbers**; shiny, white, and smooth surfaces are **poor emitters/absorbers** but **good reflectors**.
    - **Surface Temperature:** The higher the temperature relative to the surroundings, the higher the rate of emission.
    - **Surface Area:** Larger surface areas result in faster rates of emission and absorption.

**5. Exam Applications & Problem Solving**

- **Vacuum Flask:**
    - **Vacuum:** Prevents conduction and convection.
    - **Silvered Surfaces:** Minimizes radiation by reflecting it back.
    - **Plastic/Cork:** Good insulators to reduce conduction.
- **Insulation:** Trapped air (as in double-glazed windows or sponge cake) is a **poor thermal conductor**, reducing energy transfer.
- **Coastal Breezes:**
    - **Sea Breeze (Day):** Land heats faster than water; air over land rises (low pressure), and cool air from the sea moves in.
    - **Land Breeze (Night):** Land cools faster; air over the sea is warmer and rises; cool air from the land moves toward the sea.
- **Exam Logic:** To keep something cool in a hot climate, use a **bad conductor** (insulator) and a **light/shiny color** to reflect radiation.
