
**1. Force Fundamentals**

- **Definition:** A push or pull interaction that, when unopposed, changes the motion of an object.
- **Contact Forces:** Require physical contact (e.g., **Normal Reaction** perpendicular to the surface, **Friction** opposing motion, **Tension** in stretched strings/ropes).
- **Non-Contact Forces:** Influence objects from a distance (e.g., **Gravitational**, **Electric**, and **Magnetic** forces).
- **Inertia:** <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">The property of a body (due to its mass) that resists a change in its state of rest or motion.</mark>

**2. <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">Newton’s Three Laws of Motion**</mark>
[[Physics formulas]]
- **First Law:** An object stays at rest or in uniform motion in a straight line unless a **resultant force** acts on it.
    - **Implication:** If Fnet​=0, the object is stationary or moving at a constant velocity (a=0).
- **Second Law:** A resultant force (F) acting on a constant mass (m) causes acceleration (a) in the direction of the force.
    - **Key Formula:** F=ma.
- **Third Law (Action-Reaction):** If Body A exerts force FAB​ on Body B, Body B exerts an equal and opposite force FBA​ on Body A.
    - **Must-Memorize:** Action-reaction pairs act on **different** bodies, have equal magnitude, and opposite directions.
    - **Exam Trap:** The weight of a book and the normal force from the table are **not** an action-reaction pair because they act on the same object.

- **Free-Body Diagrams (FBD):** Used to visualize all forces acting **on** a specific object.
    - **Steps:** Represent object as a dot/block; draw vectors showing direction and relative magnitude; label all forces (e.g., Weight W, Tension T, Friction f).

**4. Terminal Velocity**

- **Definition:** The maximum constant velocity reached when the upward resistive force (drag/air resistance) equals the downward weight.
    - **At Terminal Velocity:** Resultant force is zero, and acceleration is zero.
- **Factors Affecting Terminal Velocity:**
    - **Weight:** Heavier objects reach higher terminal velocities.
    - **Surface Area:** Larger surface areas increase air resistance, resulting in a lower terminal velocity.
- **Parachute Descent Phases:**
    1. **Free Fall:** Immediate acceleration (W>Drag).
    2. **Terminal Velocity 1:** Drag increases until it equals weight (W=D).
    3. **Opening Parachute:** Massive increase in surface area leads to D≫W, causing rapid deceleration.
    4. **Terminal Velocity 2:** Speed decreases until drag again equals weight at a lower, safer velocity.

**5. Problem-Solving & Formulas**

- **Pulleys:** Apply F=ma to each mass individually to find system acceleration and string tension.
- **Inclined Planes:** The normal reaction (N) is usually **not** equal to weight (W); it acts perpendicular to the slope (N=Wcosθ).
- **Lift (Elevator) Problems:**
    - **Uniform Speed:** Tension equals weight (T=mg).
    - **Accelerating Up:** T=m(g+a).
    - **Accelerating Down:** T=m(g−a).
- **Connected Masses:** Treat the system as a "combined body" (macro) to find acceleration, then use individual "micro" FBDs to find internal forces (tensions).
