### **Chapter 1: Measurement**

**① CORE IDEA** Physics is the study of the physical world through **measurement**, where every physical quantity is expressed as a **numerical magnitude and a unit**. It involves identifying base and derived quantities while maintaining high precision and accuracy through the management of experimental errors.

**② KEY FACTS**

- **Physical Quantity:** A measurable property consisting of a magnitude and a unit.
- **Base vs. Derived:** There are seven base quantities (e.g., mass, time, length); all others are derived from them (e.g., speed, force).
- **Scalars vs. Vectors:** Scalars have magnitude only (e.g., mass, distance); vectors have both magnitude and direction (e.g., weight, displacement).
- **Significant Figures (s.f.):** Zeros between non-zero digits are significant; zeros before the first non-zero digit in a decimal are NOT significant.
- **Prefixes:** Used to indicate multiples or sub-multiples of SI units (e.g., nano (\(10^{-9}\)), micro (\(10^{-6}\)), kilo (\(10^{3}\)), mega (\(10^{6}\))).

**③ FORMULA / RELATIONSHIP** **Simple Pendulum Period:** \[T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}\]

- **Variables:** \(T\) = Period (s); \(l\) = length of string (m); \(g\) = gravitational field strength (\(\text{N kg}^{-1}\) or \(\text{m s}^{-2}\)).
- **Conditions:** The period \(T\) is **independent** of the mass of the bob and the angle of displacement (for small angles).
- **Constant:** Length (\(l\)) must be constant for a constant period in a fixed location (\(g\)).

**④ WHY / HOW**

- **Random Error:** Variations in conditions or human reaction time \(\rightarrow\) readings scattered about the true value \(\rightarrow\) reduced by taking averages.
- **Systematic Error:** Faulty equipment or zero errors \(\rightarrow\) readings deviate from the true value by a fixed pattern/magnitude \(\rightarrow\) eliminated by recalibration or accounting for zero error.

**⑤ DIAGRAM / GRAPH**

- **Random Error:** A distribution of measurements where points are scattered on both sides of the "true value".
- **Systematic Error:** A distribution where all measurements are shifted in one direction away from the "true value".
- **Simple Pendulum:** Diagram showing the pivot point, length \(l\), and the bob's swing amplitude.

**⑥ QUESTION TYPES**

- **Type 1: Precision Rules:** Calculating results using "least s.f." for multiplication/division and "least d.p." for addition/subtraction.
- **Type 2: Unit Conversions:** Converting complex units like density (\(\text{g cm}^{-3}\) to \(\text{kg m}^{-3}\)) or area (\(\text{cm}^2\) to \(\text{m}^2\)) using prefix powers.
- **Type 3: Instrument Selection:** Choosing between a meter rule, calipers, or micrometer based on the required precision (e.g., \(0.01\text{ mm}\) for micrometer).

**⑦ EXAMPLE** **Q:** Solve \(8.17 + 86.581 + 19.8\) and provide the answer to the correct precision. **Working:**

1. Identify decimal places: \(8.17\) (2 d.p.), \(86.581\) (3 d.p.), \(19.8\) (1 d.p.).
2. Calculation: \(8.17 + 86.581 + 19.8 = 114.551\).
3. Round to the least number of decimal places (1 d.p.). **Answer:** \(114.6\).

**⑧ COMMON TRAPS**

- ⚠️ **Mistake 1:** Applying significant figure rules to addition/subtraction (which use decimal places) or vice versa.
- ⚠️ **Mistake 2:** Assuming repeated measurements can fix systematic errors; they only reduce the effect of random errors.
- ⚠️ **Important Distinction:** **Accuracy** is how close a measurement is to the true value; **Precision** is how close a set of measurements are to each other.
