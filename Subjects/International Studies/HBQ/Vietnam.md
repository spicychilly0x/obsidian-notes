- **[THEME]** This essay will explore the relationship between Vietnam and China with regards to their military and diplomatic successes and challenges, evaluating their severe armed conflicts and territorial clashes alongside their extensive Cold War military alliances and peaceful border agreements.
- **[THESIS]** This essay believes that China-Vietnam relations have been hostile to a large extent.

**Part 3: Ultra-Minimalist PEEL Blocks**

**Paragraph 1: Hostile - The 1979 Sino-Vietnamese War (Military)**

- **Point (P):** Firstly, Vietnam's relationship with China has been hostile militarily as they have directly engaged in warfare with one another.
- **Evidence (Ev):**
    - **Ethnic Chinese Expulsion (Since 1975):** Vietnam undertook a campaign to punish and expel **1.5 million ethnic Chinese** residing in Vietnam.
    - **Soviet Alliance:** Vietnam was backed by the Soviet Union, which had a political fallout with China.
    - **Aid Suspension (1978):** In response to Vietnam's campaign, China suspended **21 development aid projects** in Vietnam.
    - **Cambodian Invasion:** Vietnam retaliated by invading Cambodia to overthrow the Chinese-backed Maoist leader Pol Pot.
    - **War of Attrition:** This escalated into a **10-year war of attrition** along the Sino-Vietnamese borders, resulting in thousands of deaths.
- **Core Explanation Logic (Ex):** Despite sharing a similar communist ideology and historical connections, their rapid political fallout and willingness to engage in a decade-long border war demonstrates that they lack mutual loyalty and will easily resort to direct military violence.
- **Link (L):** Hence, Vietnam and China have shown that they can easily enter into conflict despite their long history together.

**Paragraph 2: Hostile - South China Sea Disputes (Diplomatic/Territorial)**

- **Point (P):** Moreover, both Vietnam and China have failed to resolve territorial disputes diplomatically in the South China Seas leading to tension.
- **Evidence (Ev):**
    - **Territorial Overlaps:** Unresolvable claims over the Paracel and Spratly islands and overlapping maritime zones.
    - **Paracels Invasion (February 1974):** Chinese forces invaded the south-western group of the Paracel islands, which were held by South Vietnamese forces.
    - **Johnson South Reef Clash (14 March 1988):** Chinese warships opened fire on three Vietnamese naval vessels and a group of mostly unarmed combat engineers building structures.
    - **Casualties & Losses:** The clash claimed **64 Vietnamese lives**, sank **two Vietnamese ships**, and seriously damaged the third.
- **Core Explanation Logic (Ex):** Both nations' aggressive refusal to seek third-party mitigation, like the UN or ASEAN, and their willingness to engage in deadly clashes resulting in casualties proves they prioritize sovereignty and resources over diplomatic compromise.
- **Link (L):** Hence, their refusal to compromise clearly shows that Vietnam and China's relationship is very hostile.

**Paragraph 3: Friendly - Cold War Military Support (Counter-Point 1)**

- **Point (P):** However, there are also case-studies to show that Sino-Vietnamese relations have also been cordial/friendly through China's military assistance to Vietnam against the French and US armies.
- **Evidence (Ev):**
    - **First Indochina War:** China supported the Vietminh to establish a buffer zone and fulfill Mao's anti-colonial communist revolutionary ideals.
    - **AA Artillery Units:** Beijing dispatched **16 divisions of Chinese anti-aircraft artillery units** to protect strategic railways linking Hanoi.
    - **Troop Presence (1965–1969):** Over **320,000 Chinese engineering and anti-aircraft forces** were active on North Vietnamese soil.
- **Core Explanation Logic (Ex):** China’s willingness to deploy 320,000 of its own citizens and absorb massive financial losses to defend North Vietnam highlights a deep, historical, and ideologically unified alliance.
- **Link (L):** Hence, China's willingness to sacrifice its citizens' lives and its military weapons to help the Vietnamese show that they have a very good relationship.

**Paragraph 4: Friendly - Border Agreements (Counter-Point 2)**

- **Point (P):** Moreover, Vietnam and China have also successfully resolved territorial disputes between them diplomatically.
- **Evidence (Ev):**
    - **Shared Borderline:** The two countries share a land border of approximately **1,350 km**.
    - **Land Border Treaty (31 December 1999):** Signed to re-define the borderline utilizing the historical 1887-1895 Convention signed by France and the Qing Dynasty.
    - **Border Crossings (1992):** Opened **21 border trade points**, with four serving as official crossings.
    - **Tonkin Gulf Agreements (25 December 2000):** Successfully signed two treaties that demarcated territorial waters, established Exclusive Economic Zones (EEZs), and regulated fishing operations.
- **Core Explanation Logic (Ex):** Their success in peaceful border demarcation and trade coordination shows that when both sides choose to cooperate, they can resolve incredibly complex territorial contentions diplomatically.
- **Link (L):** Hence, the easy negotiations over land dispute show that China and Vietnam have a friendly relationship with one another.

**Part 4: Perfect 3-Mark Concluding Paragraph**

_To score a perfect 3/3 on your conclusion under the strict 2026 guidelines, write these exact four sentences. Each sentence is color-graded by the examiners to verify that you have covered the stand, criterion, elaboration, and future implications:_

- **[SENTENCE 1: THESIS]** In conclusion, this essay still believes that the Vietnam-China relations has been hostile to a large extent.
- **[SENTENCE 2: CRITERION]** This is because both governments show great actions that have an immense level of **intensity**.
- **[SENTENCE 3: ELABORATION]** These actions clearly show that both nations are willing to sacrifice thousands of lives and initiate direct military action—such as the 1979 border war over ethnic Chinese displacement and the 1988 Johnson South Reef clash that killed 64 Vietnamese personnel—to protect their sovereign national borders and maritime claims rather than seeking peaceful compromise.
- **[SENTENCE 4: IMPLICATIONS]** The implications of these actions, as such, clearly demonstrate that when it comes to disputes over resources and sovereignty that are integral to survival and identity, both nations will prioritize military deterrence over diplomatic agreements and are highly unlikely to engage in successful bilateral negotiations over territorial issues in the future.
