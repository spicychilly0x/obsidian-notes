The Mekong River is one of mainland Southeast Asia’s most vital transboundary waterways, supporting the livelihoods, food security, and ecosystems of over sixty million people across six countries since the past. **(Time/Context)**. **Argument 1**

**Firstly**, the Laos dams have created environmental insecurity in SEA. **(Topic Sentence)**.

**(P)** The **Xayaburi Dam**, the first completed mainstream dam on the Lower Mekong, illustrates how large-scale infrastructure can alter river dynamics with transboundary consequences.

**(Ev)** Research by the Mekong River Commission (MRC) estimates that mainstream dams such as Xayaburi could reduce sediment transport to downstream countries by **up to 50–60 per cent**, depriving Cambodia and Vietnam of the nutrient-rich deposits essential for floodplain fertility and delta stability. In addition, basin-wide studies project that hydropower development could lead to a **loss of approximately 725,000 tonnes of fish biomass annually**

**(Ex)** The building of the dams have weakened the river's resilience to provide nutrient-rich soil and fish to the farmers and fishermen dependent on the river for their livelihood. The reduction in the fertility of the farmland and the diversity of fish directly affects the income of this low wage farmers and fishermen. This makes it difficult for them to continue their traditional occupations because there is very less income for their family. In addition, because of the poor production of farm produce and fish, this also affects the GDP of Cambodia and Vietnam who are dependent on these products for export.

**(L)** Hence, the damming of the river has reduced its power to provide for the people living around it thus creating environmental insecurity.

 **Argument 2**

**(P) Moreover/In addition**, the Laos dams in the Mekong river also increase climate vulnerability.

**(Ev)**

The **cumulative impacts of multiple hydropower projects** have reduced the river basin’s ability to adapt to climate variability and long-term environmental change. The cumulative operation of mainstream and tributary dams could alter **up to 70 per cent of the Mekong’s natural flow variability**, significantly weakening the river’s capacity to buffer floods and droughts. the severe **2019–2020 Mekong drought**, water levels in parts of Cambodia and Vietnam fell to their lowest levels in decades. In Vietnam’s Mekong Delta, these compounded pressures have contributed to widespread saline intrusion, affecting over **1.5 million hectares of agricultural land** and threatening the livelihoods of millions of farmers.

**(Ex)** The dams have affected the natural flow of Mekong, making it less resilient to protect people from climate change. The slow flow has weakened the river's response to flood and droughts. The 2019 drought in Cambodia and Vietnam was one of the worst in their history. This led to starvation and migration of farmers and fishermen away from the Mekong river. In addition, the dumping of saline has destroyed  more than a million hectares of agricultural land making it impossible to plant anything for the farmers for revenue. Unable to work on their farms, These Cambodian and Vietnamese farmers are ill equipped to find alternative occupations because farming has been their way of life for many decades. This has brought great hardship to the Vietnamese and Cambodians who are dependent on the Mekong river for their livelihood. 

**(L)** Hence, the dams have destroyed the river's protective capabilities, harming millions of people along the river and creating a huge environmental security problem in mainland southeast asia.

**Counter 1**

**(P) However,** hydropower as a low-carbon energy source enhances regional environmental security

**(E)**

Laos’s hydropower strategy has enhanced environmental security in mainland Southeast Asia by contributing to low-carbon energy production and regional energy stability. By 2022, Laos had developed over **9,500 megawatts of installed hydropower capacity**, accounting for more than **90 per cent of its total electricity generation**, making it one of the most hydropower-dependent countries in the world. Laos has leveraged its hydropower potential to become a major exporter of renewable electricity, supplying energy to Thailand, Vietnam, and Cambodia

**(Ex)** Hydropower generated by the Laos dams have proven to be beneficial to the country and its neighbours. This green energy resource produces cheaper electricity and gives southeast Asia a sense of security because its source of supply is regional. This means that southeast Asia does not need to be overly worried to its energy supply because of external international problems like the Ukraine war and the Straits of Hormuz. The cleaner energy produced also makes the environment a more conducive place for southeast Asian people to live. 

**(L) Hence,** the Laos dams does have benefits that promote regional energy security

**Counter 2**

**(P) Moreover/In addition,** the Laos dams provide a strong opportunity for regional cooperation and strengthens ASEAN ties.

**(Ev)**

The **Mekong River Commission (MRC)** has expanded data-sharing platforms, introduced basin-wide modelling tools, and improved real-time hydrological monitoring, allowing downstream states to better anticipate and respond to flow variability.  Since 2016, Laos has participated in the **Joint Platform for Data and Information Sharing** between China and downstream countries like Vietnam, Cambodia and Thailand, improving transparency over upstream dam operations and dry-season water releases. These mechanisms have helped mitigate extreme low-water events and have enhanced regional capacity to manage environmental risks collectively, thereby contributing to environmental security rather than undermining it.

**(Ex)** The Mekong river dams have provided an excellent opportunity for China, Vietnam, Cambodia, Thailand and Laos to work together so that they can benefit best for the changes in the Mekong River. By sharing transparent data, the countries involved helps one another ensure that the Mekong River stays productive. This strengthens cooperation between these different countries and their diplomatic relations. Overall, these opportunities, provides an excellent platform to strengthen relationships and maintain peace in the region. 

**(L)** Hence, the Mekong river dams provide an excellent opportunity to promote Asian stability and harmony.
