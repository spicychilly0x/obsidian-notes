**Introduction HBQ**

Malaysia is trying to re-invent itself as the digital hub of SEA to take advantage of the profitable growth of the digital economy in the 21st century **(TIME).** The Malaysian government has prioritised creating a digitally ready workforce and the inclusion of the MSMEs in order to become successful. However, the country’s brain drain issue and the poor digital take up rate of the MSMEs may hinder its ambitions **(THEME).** The essay believes that Malaysia will succeed in its attempts to become a digital hub. **(THESIS).**

**Arg 1**

**(P)** Firstly, Malaysia has come up with plans to become a successful digital hub. Socially, it is educating its workforce to be future ready for the digital transformation.

**(EV)**

-       The eRezeki initiative was launched in 2015 to offer a means for urban B40 and M40 communities, especially gig workers, to supplement their income through digital platforms.

-       Since its inception, eRezeki has effectively trained a total of 312,735 participants to leverage digital platforms for online earnings, collectively generating RM2.51 billion in income as of December 2021

-       Operating from October 1 to December 31, 2022, the program aims to enhance the skills of over 8,000 gig workers, with a particular emphasis on engaging B40 and M40 households.

**(Exp)** The eRezeki programme targets low income communities who are not digitally literate. By tapping on this significant community, the government hopes to create a digitally ready work force that is capable of working in IT firms taking part in ecommerce. By making use of this community, Malaysia can raise its digital profile and also supply local and foreign digital firms with a steady supply of labour. Knowing that there is a digital ready workforce will also attract foreign investments into Malaysia’s ecommerce initiative

**(L)** Hence, by creating a steady supply of digital working for the Malaysian ecommerce platform and firms, Malaysia shows that it is ready to transform into a digital hub.

**Arg 2**

**(P)** Moreover, in terms of its economy, Malaysia is trying to include many MSMEs into its digital blueprint

**(Ev)**

-       SMEs numbered over 1.15 million in 2020, comprising 97.2% of all business establishments in the country. These enterprises collectively employed nearly half (48%) of the Malaysian workforce, providing 7.25 million jobs in 2020

-       in 2019, the Ministry of Entrepreneur Development (MED) introduced the National Entrepreneurship Policy (DKN 2030) incorporating Strategic Thrust 2 and National e-Commerce Strategic Roadmap (NeSR) 2.0.

-       Since November 2020, over 100 companies, including renowned brands like Yakult, Ogawa, and Nestle, have joined Subplace as Biz Partners. To date, approximately 8,000 subscribers have signed up since the official launch

**(Ex)** SMEs employing nearly half of the Malaysian workforce is a nescessary foundation for Malaysia's digital economy to succeed. The Malaysian government has created a digital platform for these companies to showcase and sell their companies online. By doing so, Malaysian government hopes there will be an increase in the number of digital commercial transactions that will boost its image in ASEAN. The malaysian government also hopes that these increased activities will also attract global firms and foreign direct investments into Malaysia's ecommerce platform, making it a significant presence in the global digital community. 
 
**(L)** Hence, the success of Malaysia's ecommerce platform by attracting SMEs will help boost its digital reputation and help it transform into a successful digital economy.
