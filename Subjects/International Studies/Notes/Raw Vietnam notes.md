**Introduction HBQ**

From WWII to the 21st century, Vietnam’s relationship with China has been characterized by successes and failures despite both countries sharing a common cultural, historical and political experience **(Time/Context)**. This essay will explore the relationship between Vietnam and China with regards to their military and diplomatic successes and challenges **(Explain terms/Arguments)**. This essay believes that China-Vietnam relations have been hostile to a large extent.  **(Thesis statement - follow terms used in hypothesis).**

**Argument 1**

**Firstly**, Vietnams’ relationship with China has been hostile militarily **(Topic Sentence)**.

**(P)** Vietnam and China have directly engaged in warfare with one another during the 1979 Sino-Vietnamese War.

**(Ev)** Since 1975, Vietnam had engaged in a huge campaign to punish and chase out the 1.5 million ethnic Chinese residing in Vietnam, many of them occupying Vietnamese land and jobs. Ho Chi Minh was confident of success as he was backed by the Soviet Union which had a political fallout out with China. China warned Vietnam to cease the operation and in 1978, suspended 21 projects that was to aid the development of Vietnam. In retaliation, Vietnam decided to invade Cambodia which was controlled by the Chinese-backed Maoist leader, Pol Pot. This led to a 10-year war of attrition along the Sino-Vietnamese borders that resulted in thousands of deaths

**(Ex)** Early in the 1970s, despite sharing a similar communist ideology and shared history, China and Vietnam did not hesitate to attack one another during their fallout. Given their long history together, Vietnam and China could have resolved this issue diplomatically without resulting to violence but this engagement clearly shows that despite their connections, they do not share loyalty with one another so they are willing to engage in military conflict easily.

**(L)** Hence, Vietnam and China have shown that they can easily enter into conflict despite their long history together.

 **Argument 2**

**(P) Moreover/In addition**, both Vietnam and China have failed to resolve territorial disputes diplomatically in the South China Seas leading to tension.

**(Ev)**

•       China and Vietnam having seemingly unresolvable disputes over <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">Paracel and Spratly islands </mark>and overlapping claims of maritime areas.  

•       In February 1974 China invaded the south-western group of the Paracel then held by South Vietnamese forces.

•       On 14 March 1988, Chinese warships opened fire on three Vietnamese naval ships and a group of mostly unarmed combat engineers who were trying to build new structures on the Johnson South Reef. The clash claimed 64 Vietnamese lives and resulted in the sinking of two Vietnamese ships and serious damage to the third

**(Ex)** China and Vietnam have shown that they are not willing to compromise on issues regarding national interest. Both countries want these islands because of the national resources and also they are part of their official territories. They are not willing to negotiate or seek third party help like the UN or ASEAN to mitigate this issue. These two countries would rather engage in deadly conflict resulting in casualties.

**(L)** Hence, their refusal to compromise clearly shows that Vietnam and China's relationship is very hostile.

**Counter 1**

**(P) However,** there are also case-studies to show that Sino-Vietnamese relations have also been cordial/friendly. Firstly, China has assisted Vietnam in its military engagements with the French and the US armies during the Cold war**.**

**(E)**

During the <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">First Indochina War</mark>   between the French and Vietminh, China supported the Vietminh out of security and ideological considerations. It was imperative to make Indochina a buffer zone between China and the West. Ideologically,<mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;"> Mao felt strongly that China had a duty to help its fellow Communist country</mark> and that the Chinese model of revolution was universally applicable to worldwide anti-colonial struggles. This was especially so for Vietnam, which had close historic and cultural ties with China. Beijing sent a total of 16 divisions of Chinese anti-aircraft artillery units to North Vietnam, whose primary tasks were to defend strategically significant targets such as the railway lines linking Hanoi and other places. in all, with over <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">320,000 Chinese engineering and anti-aircraft forces were present on North Vietnamese soil</mark> from 1965 through 1969

**(Ex)** There is a strong friendly relationship between China and Vietnam due to their common cultural ancestry, historical ties and shared communist ideology. China was also willing to help Vietnam because they wanted a buffer zone between itself and democratic forces. China showed its commitment to Vietnam by sending 320000 soldiers in an active military engagement together with expensive combat weapons. This clearly shows that China was willing to sacrifice its own citizens and lose a large amount of money in the defence of Vietnam.

**Hence,** China's willingness to sacrifice its citizens lives and its military weapons to help the Vietnamese show that they have a very good relationship.

**Counter 2**

**(P) Moreover/In addition,** Vietnam and China have also successfully resolved territorial disputes between them diplomatically

**(Ev)**

Vietnam and China have a shared borderline of around 1,350 km. On 31 December 1999, China and Vietnam signed a border agreement based on China’s treaty with France. The two countries utilized the 1887-1895 Convention signed by France and the Qing Dynasty and relevant historical documents, planning and border marker placement maps as well as defined border marks to re-define the land borderline. According to Ministry of the Foreign Affairs of the PRC, the signing of the treaty meant that all outstanding issues relating to the land border between China and Vietnam were resolved. In 1992, 21 border trade points were opened of which four served as border crossings. On 25 December 2000, China and Vietnam signed two agreements regarding the Tonkin Gulf that demarcated the territorial waters and their respective Exclusive Economic Zones (EEZs) and they established regulations to manage fishing operations

**(Ex)** China and Vietnam show that they are willing to engage each other diplomatically when it comes to border issues. There were many points of contention along the 1350km of border they shared due to differences based on China's original treaty with France. Unlike their unresolvable dispute along the South China Seas, China and Vietnam, show that they are willing to set aside differences. This shows friendly intentions of the highest order because land border disputes are difficult to manage.

Hence, the easy negotiations over land dispute show that China and Vietnam have a friendly relationship with one another.

**Conclusion**

**In conclusion,** this essay **still** believes that the Vietnam-China relations has been hostile to a large extent. **(THESIS)**. This is because both governments show great intensities in their military engagements more than their diplomatic negotiations. These actions clearly show that they are willing to sacrifice lives to protect their national interest and sovereignty. They are not willing to compromise on issues pertaining to these matters. The implications of these actions, as such, clearly demonstrate that China and Vietnam have a hostile relationship when it comes to issues that are integral to the country's survival and identity and therefore they will not engaged in diplomatic negotiations in the future regarding such issues.
