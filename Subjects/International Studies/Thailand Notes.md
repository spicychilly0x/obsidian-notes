Introduction (HBQ)

During the mid-20th century, as Thailand underwent significant political changes in its progress to nationhood, the Karen minorities in the country experienced various levels of integration and marginalization. **(TIME)**. The Thai government provided education and cultural opportunities to integrate the Karens into Thai society. However, Karen indigenous cultural and land ownership practices have proved to be a hindrance. **(THEME)**. This essay believes that the Thai government has come up with significant policies that have led to the **success of integration** **(THESIS)**.
# point 1
(P)

**Firstly** _(language marker)_, the Thai government has made sincere attempts to **successfully integrate** the Karen minorities through educational policies that **will remove communications barriers to integration**.

(EV)

-          One such initiative is the Ban Huay Nam Yen School constructed in the Mae Hong Son province in 2012 at the cost of 1.5 million THB. The school is bilingual in both Karen and Thai **to foster integration** (eg 1)

-          Literacy rates among the Karen were significantly lower than the national average. Estimates indicated that less than 30% of Karen adults could read and write, primarily due to limited access to formal education. By 2023, the literacy rate among the Karen population in Thailand had risen to approximately 70-80% allowing for **easier communication** between the Karens and the people. (eg 2)

(EXP) To break the communication barrier, the Thai government has introduced the Thai language as the Lingua France. This allows the Karens to easily talk with the Thais in school and working environments. With a common language there is easier communications which allows for more common activities between diverse groups to take place. This not only forces friendship and harmony but it also helps the Karens fit into Thai society

(L) Hence, the government’s bilingual policy has broken down communication barriers and it has successfully integrated the Karens into Thai society.

# point 2

(P)

**Moreover** _(language marker),_ the Thai government also **successfully integrated** the Karen communities by giving them space to promote their unique culture through **ethnic tourism** which is seen as an **integral part of Thai multiculturalism**.

(EV)

-          One such Karen long-neck tourism village is located at Muang Pham in the Mae Hong Son province. Karen women, central to Muang Pham's tourism, can be seen weaving traditional products, which are also sold to visitors. This craft has been practiced for centuries, with techniques and patterns handed down through generations which is seen as a feature of **Thai multi-racial society**. (eg 1)

-          In 2001, another Karen tourist village in Mae Hong San generated tourism revenue of 966.43 million baht and it increased to 1,629.48 million baht in 2006. This village, established in 1961, is home to approximately 100 households and 500 residents, most of whom are Sgaw-Karen who were given Thai citizenship to recognize their efforts in strengthening the Thai economy and **Thai multiculturalism**. (eg 2)

(EXP) Karen culture is now seen as part of Thai culture. The Thai government extensively protects and promotes Karen culture which gives these communities a safe space. This reassuring from the Thai government has allowed the Karens to feel like they belong to Thai society. In addition, the Karens also feel pride as contributing citizens to the Thai economy through their ethnic tourism. Therefore, the Karens see themselves as part of Thai society.

(L) Hence, active Thai government promotion has made Karen society inclusive of Thailand and this has allowed a successful integration.

# counter 1

(TS)

**However** (language marker), some Thai government policies like the promotion of Buddhism may have led to **failure in integrating** with the Karens who might have felt that their **own indigenous religion was under threat**.

(EV)

-          The nature of Karen identity and nationalism revolves around syncretic beliefs in Christianity and animism (ancestral worship). The introduction or promotion of Buddhist temples in predominantly Karen areas like Mae Hong Son by Thai authorities or organizations can be seen as a **threat** to Karen cultural autonomy and **religious identity**. (eg 1)

-          One notable example occurred in 2018 when the Thai government proposed the construction of a Thai Buddhist temple in a Karen village called Baan Klong Jark, located in the Thong Pha Phum district of Kanchanaburi province. This proposal sparked significant opposition and protests from the Karen community who felt their **religious identity** was **under threat** from Thai actions. (eg 2)

(EXP) The Thai government building of temples in Karen majority provinces has put the fear into the Karen communities that the Thai government was going to impose Buddhism on them. The Karens are fiercely protective of their identity. Any threat to their culture is seen as an act of aggression. Even if the Thai government had no intention of imposing Buddhism on the Karens, the Karens’ failure to compromise is a huge barrier to integration.

(L) Hence, the strong Karen sense of identity makes integration difficult.
