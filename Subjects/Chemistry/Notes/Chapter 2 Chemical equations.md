**1. Classification of Matter**

- **Pure Substance:** Constant, definite composition.
    - **Element:** Atoms with the same atomic number; cannot be broken down by chemical processes (e.g., $\text{Na}$, $\text{Cl}_2$).
    - **Compound:** Atoms of different elements chemically combined in a fixed ratio (e.g., $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{NaCl}$).
- **Mixture:** Physical combination of two or more substances (not chemically joined).
    - **Homogeneous:** Uniform composition/properties throughout (e.g., alloys, saltwater).
    - **Heterogeneous:** Non-uniform composition (e.g., soil, paint).

**3. Writing & Balancing Chemical Equations**

- **State Symbols:** Must include solid $(\text{s})$, liquid $(\text{l})$, gas $(\text{g})$, and aqueous $(\text{aq})$.
- **Balancing Rule:** Number of atoms of each element must be equal on both sides. **Do not write coefficient '1'** (leave the space blank).

---

**4. Ionic Equations & Dissociation Rules**

An ionic equation only includes the species **directly participating** in the reaction; spectator ions (unchanged ions on both sides) are cancelled out.

**Dissociation Rules (What splits into ions in** $\text{aq}$ **state?):**

1. **Strong Acids:** All dilute/concentrated acids dissociate completely in water (e.g., $\text{HCl} \rightarrow \text{H}^+ + \text{Cl}^-$).
2. **Alkalis:** Group 1 hydroxides and $\text{Ca}(\text{OH})_2$ dissociate into cations and $\text{OH}^-$.
3. **Soluble Ionic Compounds:** Dissociate into aqueous ions.
4. **Insoluble Ionic Compounds:** Remain as solids $(\text{s})$, **do not** dissociate.
5. **Simple Molecular Substances:** (e.g., $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{CO}_2$, $\text{NH}_3$) exist as neutral molecules, **do not** dissociate.
6. **Metals:** Exist as solids $(\text{s})$ (except $\text{Hg}(\text{l})$), **do not** dissociate.

**How to Construct an Ionic Equation:**

- **Step 1:** Write the fully balanced molecular equation with state symbols.
- **Step 2:** Dissociate only the soluble, strong electrolytes (acids, alkalis, soluble salts) into their constituent ions.
- **Step 3:** Cancel spectator ions that appear identical on both the reactant and product sides.
- **Step 4:** Write the remaining reacting species.
    - _Example (Neutralization):_ $\text{H}^+ (\text{aq}) + \text{OH}^- (\text{aq}) \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{O} (\text{l})$

---

🔍 **Exam Tip:** Keep Roman numerals in mind for transition metals with variable valencies (like Iron(II) vs Iron(III)) to construct formulas accurately.
