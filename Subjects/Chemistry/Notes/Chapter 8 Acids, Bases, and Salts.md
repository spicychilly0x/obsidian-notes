
**1. Acids & Acidic Strength**

- **Definition (Arrhenius Theory):** An acid is a substance that dissociates in water to produce **hydrogen ions (**$H^+$**)** and anions1.
- **Role of Water:** Acids **only** show acidic properties when dissolved in water because water is required for the acid molecules to dissociate into mobile $H^+$ ions1. In organic solvents (e.g., alcohol), acids exist as neutral molecules and do not dissociate [Image 13].
- **Strong vs. Weak Acids:**
    - **Strong Acid:** Completely dissociates in aqueous solution to produce a **high concentration of** $H^+$ **ions**23 (e.g., Hydrochloric acid ($HCl$), Sulfuric acid ($H_2SO_4$), Nitric acid ($HNO_3$))4.
    - **Weak Acid:** Partially dissociates in aqueous solution to produce a **low concentration of** $H^+$ **ions**3 (e.g., Ethanoic acid ($CH_3COOH$), Carbonic acid ($H_2CO_3$), Phosphoric acid ($H_3PO_4$))45. Reversible arrows ($\rightleftharpoons$) must be used in weak acid dissociation equations5.
- **Basicity:** The number of ionisable hydrogen atoms per acid molecule67.
    - **Monobasic:** 1 H per molecule (e.g., $HCl, HNO_3, CH_3COOH$)48.
    - **Dibasic:** 2 H per molecule (e.g., $H_2SO_4, H_2CO_3$)48.
    - **Tribasic:** 3 H per molecule (e.g., $H_3PO_4$)48.
- **Reactivity Comparison:**
    - Weak acids undergo the **same** chemical reactions as strong acids (e.g., requiring the exact same volume of base for neutralisation in titration)5.
    - However, the **rate of reaction** for a weak acid is **slower** due to the lower concentration of $H^+$ ions available6.

**Chemical Reactions of Acids (Must Memorize):**

1. **Acid + Reactive Metal** $\rightarrow$ **Salt + Hydrogen gas (**$H_2$**)**8
    - _Observation:_ Effervescence of a colourless, odourless gas that extinguishes a burning splint with a **'pop' sound** .
    - _Exceptions:_ Unreactive metals (e.g., $Cu, Ag, Au$) do not react . Lead ($Pb$) reaction stops quickly due to the formation of an **insoluble salt coating** (e.g., $PbSO_4$ or $PbCl_2$) that shields the remaining metal9.
2. **Acid + Carbonate** $\rightarrow$ **Salt + Carbon dioxide (**$CO_2$**) + Water (**$H_2O$**)**9
    - _Observation:_ Effervescence of a colourless, odourless gas that forms a **white precipitate in limewater** .
3. **Acid + Base/Alkali** $\rightarrow$ **Salt + Water (Neutralisation)**10
    - _Net Ionic Equation:_ $$H^+ (aq) + OH^- (aq) \rightarrow H_2O (l) \quad$$211

**2. Bases & Alkalis**

- **Definitions:**
    - **Base:** A metal oxide ($O^{2-}$) or metal hydroxide ($OH^-$) that reacts with an acid to produce **only salt and water**12. Bases can be soluble or insoluble in water1012.
    - **Alkali:** A water-soluble base that dissolves in water to produce **hydroxide ions (**$OH^-$**)**12. _( Mnemonic: "All alkalis are bases, but not all bases are alkalis" [59, Image 24])._
- **Strong vs. Weak Alkalis:**
    - **Strong Alkali:** Completely dissociates in water to produce a high concentration of $OH^-$ ions (e.g., $NaOH, KOH, Ca(OH)_2, Ba(OH)_2$)3.
    - **Weak Alkali:** Partially dissociates in water to produce a low concentration of $OH^-$ ions (e.g., Aqueous ammonia ($NH_3$))3: $$NH_3 (aq) + H_2O (l) \rightleftharpoons NH_4^+ (aq) + OH^- (aq) \quad$$3

**Chemical Reactions of Alkalis (Must Memorize):**

1. **Alkali + Acid** $\rightarrow$ **Salt + Water**13
2. **Alkali + Ammonium Salt** $\xrightarrow{\text{warm}}$ **Salt + Water + Ammonia (**$NH_3$**)**11
    - _Observation:_ A pungent, colourless gas is evolved that turns **damp red litmus paper blue**1415.
    - _Ionic Equation:_ $$OH^- (aq) + NH_4^+ (aq) \rightarrow H_2O (l) + NH_3 (g) \quad$$14

**3. The pH Scale & Indicators**

- **pH Definition:** A measure of hydrogen ion concentration16: $$\text{pH} = -\log_{10}[H^+] \quad$$17
- **Mathematical Relationships:**
    - In a neutral solution at $25^\circ C$, $[H^+] = 10^{-7}\text{ mol dm}^{-3} \Rightarrow \text{pH} = 7$17.
    - An increase in $[H^+]$ concentration by a **factor of 10** decreases the pH by **exactly 1**17.
    - $$\text{pH} + \text{pOH} = 14 \quad [\text{Image } 28]$$
- **Factors Affecting pH:**
    1. **Concentration:** Higher acid concentration $\rightarrow$ lower pH; higher alkali concentration $\rightarrow$ higher pH18.
    2. **Strength:** For equal concentrations, a strong acid has a lower pH than a weak acid6.
- **Universal Indicator Colours:**
    - **Red:** Strongly acidic (pH 0-2) [Image 29]
    - **Orange / Yellow:** Weakly acidic (pH 3-6) [Image 29]
    - **Green:** Neutral (pH 7) [Image 29]
    - **Blue / Turquoise:** Weakly alkaline (pH 8-11) [Image 29]
    - **Violet / Purple:** Strongly alkaline (pH 12-14) [Image 29]
- **Soil pH Control:** Slaked lime (calcium hydroxide) or quicklime (calcium oxide) is added to agricultural soil to **neutralise excess acidity** and optimize plant growth216.

**4. Classification of Oxides (Highly Tested)**

Oxides are classified into four categories based on metallic character and chemical reactions2:

1. **Basic Oxides:** Metallic oxides that react with acids to form salt and water only1012. Insoluble in water except for Group 1 oxides and $CaO$ .
    - _Examples:_ $Na_2O$, $K_2O$, $CaO$, $MgO$, $CuO$ .
2. **Acidic Oxides:** Non-metallic oxides that react with bases/alkalis to form salt and water only . Most dissolve in water to produce acidic solutions .
    - _Examples:_ $CO_2$, $SO_2$, $SO_3$, $NO_2$, $P_4O_{10}$ .
3. **Amphoteric Oxides:** Metallic oxides that react with **both acids and bases** to form salt and water .
    - _Examples (ZAP):_ **Zinc oxide (**$ZnO$**), Aluminium oxide (**$Al_2O_3$**), and Lead(II) oxide (**$PbO$**)** .
    - _Reactions to memorize:_ $$ZnO(s) + 2HCl(aq) \rightarrow ZnCl_2(aq) + H_2O(l)$$ $$ZnO(s) + 2NaOH(aq) \rightarrow \text{Na}_2\text{ZnO}_2(aq) + H_2O(l) \quad \quad (\text{sodium zincate})$$
4. **Neutral Oxides:** Non-metallic oxides that **do not react** with either acids or bases .
    - _Examples:_ **Carbon monoxide (**$CO$**), Nitric oxide / Nitrogen monoxide (**$NO$**), and Dinitrogen monoxide (**$N_2O$**)** .

**5. Salt Solubility Rules**

To choose correct salt preparation methods, memorizing solubility rules is critical2:

- **SPA Salts (Sodium, Potassium, Ammonium):** All are **soluble** .
- **Nitrates (**$NO_3^-$**):** All are **soluble** .
- **Chlorides (**$Cl^-$**):** All are soluble **except** **Silver chloride (**$AgCl$**) and Lead(II) chloride (**$PbCl_2$**)** .
- **Sulfates (**$SO_4^{2-}$**):** All are soluble **except** **Lead(II) sulfate (**$PbSO_4$**), Barium sulfate (**$BaSO_4$**), and Calcium sulfate (**$CaSO_4$ **is slightly soluble)** .
- **Carbonates (**$CO_3^{2-}$**):** All are **insoluble** **except** SPA carbonates .
- **Hydroxides & Oxides:** All are **insoluble** **except** Group 1, Barium, and Calcium (slightly) hydroxides/oxides [Image 25].

**6. Methods of Salt Preparation**

Reactant and product solubility determine which of the three methods to use2:

**Method 1: Precipitation (For Insoluble Salts)**

- **Reactants:** Two **soluble** salt solutions (one providing the target cation, one providing the target anion) .
- **Process:** Mix $\rightarrow$ Filter to collect precipitate residue $\rightarrow$ Wash residue with distilled water $\rightarrow$ Dry between sheets of filter paper [251, Image 23].
- _Example (Silver Chloride):_ $$AgNO_3(aq) + NaCl(aq) \rightarrow AgCl(s) + NaNO_3(aq) \quad $$

**Method 2: Excess Method (For Soluble, Non-SPA Salts)**

- **Reactants:** Acid + excess **insoluble** reactant (metal, metal oxide, metal hydroxide, or metal carbonate) .
    - _Why excess?_ To ensure all acid is completely neutralised .
- **Process:** Add excess reactant to acid and warm $\rightarrow$ Filter to remove unreacted excess solid $\rightarrow$ Heat filtrate to **saturation point** (do _not_ evaporate to dryness) $\rightarrow$ Cool to crystallise $\rightarrow$ Filter crystals, wash with cold distilled water, and dry .
- _Example (Copper(II) Sulfate):_ $$CuO(s) + H_2SO_4(aq) \rightarrow CuSO_4(aq) + H_2O(l) \quad$$19

**Method 3: Titration (For Soluble, SPA Salts)**

- **Reactants:** Acid + soluble alkali or soluble carbonate .
- **Process:** Titrate acid against alkali with an indicator (e.g., methyl orange) to find the endpoint $\rightarrow$ Repeat experiment using the same volumes **without the indicator** $\rightarrow$ Heat to saturation, cool to crystallise, filter, wash, and dry .
- _Example (Sodium Sulfate):_ $$2NaOH(aq) + H_2SO_4(aq) \rightarrow Na_2SO_4(aq) + 2H_2O(l) \quad$$20

**7. Qualitative Analysis (QA) Tests**

Standard diagnostic tests and positive observations :

**Anion Tests :**

- **Carbonate (**$CO_3^{2-}$**):** Add dilute acid $\rightarrow$ Effervescence of $CO_2$ gas (turns limewater cloudy).
- **Sulfate (**$SO_4^{2-}$**):** Add dilute nitric acid (or $HCl$), then add aqueous barium nitrate (or $BaCl_2$) $\rightarrow$ **White precipitate** of barium sulfate ($BaSO_4$) forms.
- **Chloride (**$Cl^-$**):** Add dilute nitric acid, then add aqueous silver nitrate $\rightarrow$ **White precipitate** of silver chloride ($AgCl$) forms.
- **Iodide (**$I^-$**):** Add dilute nitric acid, then add aqueous silver nitrate $\rightarrow$ **Yellow precipitate** of silver iodide ($AgI$) forms.
- **Nitrate (**$NO_3^-$**):** Add aqueous sodium hydroxide, add aluminium foil/Devarda's alloy, and warm gently $\rightarrow$ Effervescence of $NH_3$ gas (pungent gas turns moist red litmus blue).

**QA Gas Tests :**

- **Ammonia (**$NH_3$**):** Colourless, pungent gas. Turns moist red litmus paper blue15.
- **Carbon dioxide (**$CO_2$**):** Colourless, odourless gas. Forms a white precipitate with limewater .
- **Hydrogen (**$H_2$**):** Colourless, odourless gas. Extinguishes a burning splint with a **'pop' sound** .
