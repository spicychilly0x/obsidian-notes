**1. Acids & Acidic Strength**

- **Definition (Arrhenius Theory):** An acid is a substance that ionises/dissociates in water to produce hydrogen ions, (\text{H}^+\text{ (aq)}).
- **Role of Water:** Acids **only** show acidic properties when dissolved in water. Water is required for the acid molecules to dissociate into mobile (\text{H}^+) ions.
    - In organic solvents (such as alcohol or methylbenzene), acids exist entirely as neutral, un-ionised covalent molecules and will not react with metals or carbonates, nor will they turn dry blue litmus paper red.
- **Strong vs. Weak Acids:**
    - **Strong Acid:** Completely dissociates in aqueous solution to produce a **high concentration of (\text{H}^+) ions** (e.g., hydrochloric acid (\text{HCl}), sulfuric acid (\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4), nitric acid (\text{HNO}_3)). $$\text{HCl (aq)} \rightarrow \text{H}^+\text{ (aq)} + \text{Cl}^-\text{ (aq)}$$
    - **Weak Acid:** Partially dissociates in aqueous solution to produce a **low concentration of (\text{H}^+) ions**. Dissociation is reversible (represented with a reversible arrow (\rightleftharpoons)) (e.g., ethanoic acid (\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}), carbonic acid (\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3), phosphoric acid (\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4)). $$\text{CH}_3\text{COOH (aq)} \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3\text{COO}^-\text{ (aq)} + \text{H}^+\text{ (aq)}$$
- **Basicity of Acids:** The maximum number of ionisable hydrogen atoms per acid molecule.
    - **Monobasic:** Produces 1 (\text{H}^+) ion per molecule (e.g., (\text{HCl}), (\text{HNO}_3), (\text{CH}_3\text{COOH})). _(Note: In ethanoic acid, only the terminal (\text{H}) in the carboxyl group is ionisable)._
    - **Dibasic:** Produces 2 (\text{H}^+) ions per molecule (e.g., (\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4), (\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3)). Dibasic acids can dissociate in stages to form two types of salts: acid salts (e.g., (\text{NaHSO}_4)) and normal salts (e.g., (\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4)).
    - **Tribasic:** Produces 3 (\text{H}^+) ions per molecule (e.g., (\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4)).
- **Reactivity Comparison:**
    - Under equal concentrations, a weak acid has a **slower rate of reaction** than a strong acid because it has a lower concentration of free-moving (\text{H}^+) ions.
    - However, both acids require the **exact same volume of base** for complete neutralisation because the total quantity of potential, reactable (\text{H}^+) ions is the same.

**2. Chemical Reactions of Acids (Must Memorize)**

1. **Acid + Reactive Metal (\rightarrow) Salt + Hydrogen gas ((\text{H}_2))**
    - _Observation:_ Effervescence of a colourless, odourless gas that **extinguishes a burning splint with a 'pop' sound**.
    - _Equation Example:_ (\text{Mg (s)} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4\text{ (aq)} \rightarrow \text{MgSO}_4\text{ (aq)} + \text{H}_2\text{ (g)})
    - _Exceptions:_ Unreactive metals below hydrogen in the reactivity series (such as (\text{Cu, Ag, Au, Pt})) do not react.
    - _The Lead ((\text{Pb})) Trap:_ Lead reaction stops almost immediately due to the formation of an **insoluble salt layer** (e.g., (\text{PbSO}_4) or (\text{PbCl}_2)) that coats and shields the underlying metal.
2. **Acid + Carbonate/Hydrogencarbonate (\rightarrow) Salt + Carbon dioxide ((\text{CO}_2)) + Water ((\text{H}_2\text{O}))**
    - _Observation:_ Effervescence of a colourless, odourless gas that forms a **white precipitate in limewater** (turns limewater cloudy).
    - _Equation Example:_ (\text{CaCO}_3\text{ (s)} + 2\text{HCl (aq)} \rightarrow \text{CaCl}_2\text{ (aq)} + \text{CO}_2\text{ (g)} + \text{H}_2\text{O (l)})
3. **Acid + Metal Oxide/Hydroxide (\rightarrow) Salt + Water (Neutralisation)**
    - _Net Ionic Equation:_ $$\text{H}^+\text{ (aq)} + \text{OH}^-\text{ (aq)} \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{O (l)}$$
4. **Acid + Ammonia (\rightarrow) Ammonium Salt**
    - _Net Ionic Equation:_ $$\text{NH}_3\text{ (aq)} + \text{H}^+\text{ (aq)} \rightarrow \text{NH}_4^+\text{ (aq)}$$

**3. Bases & Alkalis**

- **Base:** A metallic oxide ((\text{O}^{2-})) or hydroxide ((\text{OH}^-)) that reacts with an acid to produce **a salt and water only**.
- **Alkali:** A water-soluble base that dissolves in water to produce hydroxide ions, (\text{OH}^-\text{ (aq)}).
    - _Relationship:_ All alkalis are bases, but not all bases are alkalis (insoluble bases are not alkalis).
- **Strong vs. Weak Alkalis:**
    - **Strong Alkali:** Completely dissociates in water to produce a high concentration of (\text{OH}^-) ions (e.g., (\text{NaOH, KOH, Ca(OH)}_2, \text{Ba(OH)}_2)).
    - **Weak Alkali:** Partially dissociates in water to produce a low concentration of (\text{OH}^-) ions (e.g., aqueous ammonia). $$\text{NH}_3\text{ (aq)} + \text{H}_2\text{O (l)} \rightleftharpoons \text{NH}_4^+\text{ (aq)} + \text{OH}^-\text{ (aq)}$$

**Chemical Properties of Alkalis (Must Memorize):**

1. **Alkali + Acid (\rightarrow) Salt + Water (Neutralisation)**
2. **Alkali + Ammonium Salt (\xrightarrow{\text{warm}}) Salt + Water + Ammonia gas ((\text{NH}_3))**
    - _Observation:_ Pungent, colourless gas is evolved that turns **moist red litmus paper blue**.
    - _Equation Example:_ (\text{NaOH (aq)} + \text{NH}_4\text{Cl (aq)} \rightarrow \text{NaCl (aq)} + \text{H}_2\text{O (l)} + \text{NH}_3\text{ (g)})
    - _Net Ionic Equation:_ $$\text{OH}^-\text{ (aq)} + \text{NH}_4^+\text{ (aq)} \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{O (l)} + \text{NH}_3\text{ (g)}$$

**4. The pH Scale & Indicators**

- **Mathematical Formulas:** $$\text{pH} = -\log_{10}[\text{H}^+]$$ $$\text{pOH} = -\log_{10}[\text{OH}^-]$$
    - At room temperature and pressure (r.t.p., $25^\circ\text{C}$): $$\text{pH} + \text{pOH} = 14$$
- **Key Relationships:**
    - Neutral solution at r.t.p.: ([\text{H}^+] = 10^{-7}\text{ mol dm}^{-3} \Rightarrow \text{pH} = 7).
    - A change of **1 pH unit** corresponds to a **10-fold change** in hydrogen ion concentration.
- **Universal Indicator Colours:**
    - **Red:** Strongly acidic (pH 0-2)
    - **Orange / Yellow:** Weakly acidic (pH 3-6)
    - **Green:** Neutral (pH 7)
    - **Blue / Turquoise:** Weakly alkaline (pH 8-11)
    - **Violet / Purple:** Strongly alkaline (pH 12-14)
- **Soil pH Control:** Slaked lime (calcium hydroxide, (\text{Ca(OH)}_2)) or quicklime (calcium oxide, (\text{CaO})) is spread on farmland to **neutralise excess soil acidity** and optimize crop growth.

**5. Classification of Oxides (Highly Tested)**

Oxides are classified based on their chemical reactions and metallic/non-metallic character:

1. **Basic Oxides:** Metallic oxides that react only with acids to form salt and water. They are insoluble in water except for Group 1 oxides and (\text{CaO}) which react to form alkalis.
    - _Examples:_ (\text{Na}_2\text{O, K}_2\text{O, CaO, MgO, CuO})
2. **Acidic Oxides:** Non-metallic oxides that react only with bases/alkalis to form salt and water. Most dissolve in water to produce acidic solutions.
    - _Examples:_ (\text{CO}_2, \text{SO}_2, \text{SO}_3, \text{NO}_2, \text{P}_4\text{O}_{10})
    - _The (\text{SiO}_2) Exception:_ Silicon dioxide is insoluble in water but is acidic because it reacts with hot, concentrated sodium hydroxide.
3. **Amphoteric Oxides:** Metallic oxides that react with **both acids and bases** to form salt and water.
    - _The "ZAP" Triad (Must Memorize):_ **Zinc oxide ((\text{ZnO})), Aluminium oxide ((\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3)), and Lead(II) oxide ((\text{PbO}))**. Beryllium oxide ((\text{BeO})) is also amphoteric.
    - _Reactions to Memorize:_ $$\text{ZnO (s)} + 2\text{HCl (aq)} \rightarrow \text{ZnCl}_2\text{ (aq)} + \text{H}_2\text{O (l)}$$ $$\text{ZnO (s)} + 2\text{NaOH (aq)} \rightarrow \text{Na}_2\text{ZnO}_2\text{ (aq)} + \text{H}_2\text{O (l)} \quad (\text{sodium zincate})$$
4. **Neutral Oxides:** Non-metallic oxides that **do not react** with either acids or bases.
    - _Examples:_ **Carbon monoxide ((\text{CO})), Nitrogen monoxide ((\text{NO})), and Dinitrogen monoxide ((\text{N}_2\text{O}))**.

**6. Salt Solubility Rules (Must Memorize)**

Solubility rules must be memorized to determine the correct salt preparation methods:

- **SPA Salts (Sodium, Potassium, Ammonium):** All are **soluble**.
- **Nitrates ((\text{NO}_3^-)):** All are **soluble**.
- **Chlorides ((\text{Cl}^-)):** All are soluble **except** **Silver chloride ((\text{AgCl})) and Lead(II) chloride ((\text{PbCl}_2))**.
- **Sulfates ((\text{SO}_4^{2-})):** All are soluble **except** **Lead(II) sulfate ((\text{PbSO}_4)), Barium sulfate ((\text{BaSO}_4)), and Calcium sulfate ((\text{CaSO}_4) is slightly soluble)**.
- **Carbonates ((\text{CO}_3^{2-})):** All are **insoluble** **except** SPA carbonates.
- **Hydroxides & Oxides:** All are **insoluble** **except** Group 1, Barium, and Calcium (slightly) hydroxides/oxides.

**7. Methods of Salt Preparation**

**Method 1: Precipitation (For Insoluble Salts)**

- **Reactants:** Two **soluble** starting solutions (one providing the target cation, one providing the target anion).
- **Process:** Mix reactants (\rightarrow) Filter to collect precipitate residue (\rightarrow) Wash residue with distilled water to remove spectator ions (\rightarrow) Dry residue between sheets of filter paper.
- _Example (Silver Chloride):_ $$\text{AgNO}_3\text{ (aq)} + \text{NaCl (aq)} \rightarrow \text{AgCl (s)} + \text{NaNO}_3\text{ (aq)}$$

**Method 2: Excess Method (For Soluble, Non-SPA Salts)**

- **Reactants:** Acid + excess **insoluble** solid reactant (metal, metal oxide, metal hydroxide, or metal carbonate).
    - _Why excess?_ To ensure all limiting acid is completely neutralised.
- **Process:** Add excess solid to acid and warm gently (\rightarrow) Filter to remove unreacted excess solid residue (\rightarrow) Heat filtrate to **saturation point** (\rightarrow) Cool slowly to crystallise (\rightarrow) Filter crystals, wash with a small volume of cold distilled water, and dry.
- _Example (Copper(II) Sulfate):_ $$\text{CuO (s)} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4\text{ (aq)} \rightarrow \text{CuSO}_4\text{ (aq)} + \text{H}_2\text{O (l)}$$

**Method 3: Titration (For Soluble, SPA Salts)**

- **Reactants:** Soluble acid + soluble alkali/soluble carbonate.
- **Process:** Titrate acid against alkali with an indicator (e.g., methyl orange) to find the exact endpoint (\rightarrow) Repeat the titration with identical volumes **without the indicator** (\rightarrow) Heat to saturation, cool to crystallise, filter, wash, and dry.
- _Example (Sodium Chloride):_ $$\text{NaOH (aq)} + \text{HCl (aq)} \rightarrow \text{NaCl (aq)} + \text{H}_2\text{O (l)}$$

**8. Qualitative Analysis (QA) Tests**

**Anion Tests:**

- **Carbonate ((\text{CO}_3^{2-})):** Add dilute acid. _Positive Observation:_ Effervescence of (\text{CO}_2) gas (turns limewater cloudy).
- **Chloride ((\text{Cl}^-)):** Acidify with dilute nitric acid, then add aqueous silver nitrate. _Positive Observation:_ **White precipitate** of silver chloride ((\text{AgCl})) forms.
- **Iodide ((\text{I}^-)):** Acidify with dilute nitric acid, then add aqueous silver nitrate. _Positive Observation:_ **Yellow precipitate** of silver iodide ((\text{AgI})) forms.
- **Sulfate ((\text{SO}_4^{2-})):** Acidify with dilute nitric acid, then add aqueous barium nitrate. _Positive Observation:_ **White precipitate** of barium sulfate ((\text{BaSO}_4)) forms.
- **Nitrate ((\text{NO}_3^-)):** Add aqueous sodium hydroxide, add aluminium foil/Devarda's alloy, and warm gently. _Positive Observation:_ Effervescence of ammonia ((\text{NH}_3)) gas (pungent gas turns moist red litmus blue).

**QA Gas Tests:**

- **Ammonia ((\text{NH}_3)):** Colourless, pungent gas. Turns moist red litmus paper blue.
- **Carbon dioxide ((\text{CO}_2)):** Colourless, odourless gas. Forms a white precipitate with limewater.
- **Hydrogen ((\text{H}_2)):** Colourless, odourless gas. Extinguishes a burning splint with a **'pop' sound**.
- **Oxygen ((\text{O}_2)):** Colourless, odourless gas. Relights a glowing splint.
- **Chlorine ((\text{Cl}_2)):** Greenish-yellow, pungent, choking gas. Bleaches damp blue litmus paper.
- **Sulfur dioxide ((\text{SO}_2)):** Colourless, pungent, suffocating gas. Turns acidified potassium manganate(VII) from **purple to colourless**.

**⚠️ Critical Exam Pitfalls & Grading Standards (Must Memorize):**

- **The Reversible Arrow:** Weak acid dissociation (like (\text{CH}_3\text{COOH})) and weak alkali dissociation (like (\text{NH}_3)) **must** use the reversible arrow ((\rightleftharpoons)) in equations to avoid deduction of marks.
- **Crystallisation vs. Dryness:** In the Excess and Titration methods, never heat the solution to dryness. Always evaporate only until **saturation** (crystallisation point) to obtain hydrated salt crystals.
- **Lead Sulfate Insoluble Layer:** When asked why lead ((\text{Pb})) cannot be used to prepare soluble salts like lead(II) sulfate with sulfuric acid, explicitly state that _an insoluble layer of lead(II) sulfate forms on the surface of the metal, protecting it and preventing further reaction_.
