**1. Relative Masses (Ratios — No Units)**

- **Isotopes:** Atoms of the same element with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons (and thus different mass numbers).
- **Relative Isotopic Mass:** The ratio of the mass of one atom of a specific isotope to $\frac{1}{12}$ of the mass of an atom of the $^{12}\text{C}$ isotope on the $^{12}\text{C}$ scale.
- **Relative Atomic Mass (**$A_r$**):** The ratio of the average mass of one atom of an element to $\frac{1}{12}$ of the mass of an atom of the $^{12}\text{C}$ isotope on the $^{12}\text{C}$ scale. $$A_r = \sum \left( \text{isotopic mass} \times \text{isotopic abundance} \right)$$
- **Relative Molecular Mass (**$M_r$**):** The ratio of the average mass of one molecule of a covalent substance to $\frac{1}{12}$ of the mass of an atom of the $^{12}\text{C}$ isotope on the $^{12}\text{C}$ scale (sum of the $A_r$ of all constituent atoms in the molecule).
- **Relative Formula Mass (**$M_r$**):** The identical ratio used specifically for ionic compounds because they do not exist as discrete molecules.

---

**2. The Mole & Molar Mass**

- **The Mole:** The amount of substance containing $6.02 \times 10^{23}$ particles (Avogadro's Constant / Avogadro's Number). $$\text{Number of Moles } (n) = \frac{\text{Number of particles}}{6.02 \times 10^{23}}$$
- **Particle Specification:** You must specify the particle type. E.g., $1\text{ mole}$ of oxygen gas molecules ($O_2$) weighs $32.0\text{ g}$ and contains $6.02 \times 10^{23}$ molecules, but contains $2\text{ moles}$ of oxygen atoms ($1.20 \times 10^{24}$ atoms) weighing $16.0\text{ g}$ each.
- **Molar Mass:** The mass of one mole of a substance (expressed in $\text{g mol}^{-1}$).
    - Mass of $1\text{ mole}$ of atoms $= A_r\text{ g mol}^{-1}$.
    - Mass of $1\text{ mole}$ of molecules/formula units $= M_r\text{ g mol}^{-1}$. $$\text{Number of Moles } (n) = \frac{\text{Mass of sample (g)}}{\text{Molar mass } (\text{g mol}^{-1})}$$

---

**3. Molar Volume of Gas**

- **Avogadro's Law:** Equal volumes of all gases under identical conditions of temperature and pressure contain the same number of particles.
- **Molar Gas Volume:** One mole of any gas occupies a volume of $24.0\text{ dm}^3$ (or $24000\text{ cm}^3$) at room temperature and pressure (**r.t.p.**, $25^\circ\text{C}$ and $1\text{ atm}$). $$\text{Number of Moles } (n) = \frac{\text{Volume of gas in dm}^3}{24.0} = \frac{\text{Volume of gas in cm}^3}{24000}$$
- **Conversion:** $1\text{ dm}^3 = 1000\text{ cm}^3$.

---

**4. Percentage Composition & Hydrates**

- **Law of Constant Composition:** The percentage composition of a pure substance remains constant and does not change.
- **Percentage Mass of an Element:** $$\% \text{ of Element} = \frac{\text{Number of atoms of element in formula} \times A_r}{M_r \text{ of compound}} \times 100\%$$
- **Water of Crystallisation (Hydrated Salts):** Chemically bound water molecules inside a salt's crystal lattice (e.g., $\text{CoCl}_2 \cdot 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$). Calculate the water mass contribution in the total $M_r$ of the hydrate: $$\% \text{ of Water} = \frac{\text{Number of } \text{H}_2\text{O} \text{ molecules} \times M_r(\text{H}_2\text{O})}{M_r \text{ of hydrated salt}} \times 100\%$$

---

**5. Empirical & Molecular Formulae**

- **Empirical Formula:** The simplest chemical formula showing the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms of each element in a compound.
- **Molecular Formula:** Shows the actual type and exact number of each atom present in a molecule. $$\text{Molecular Formula} = (\text{Empirical Formula})_n$$ $$\text{where } n = \frac{\text{Molar mass / molecular mass of compound}}{M_r \text{ of empirical formula}}$$

**6. Solution Concentration**

- **Concentration in** $\text{g dm}^{-3}$**:** $$\text{Concentration } (\text{g dm}^{-3}) = \frac{\text{Mass of solute (g)}}{\text{Volume of solution (dm}^3)}$$
- **Molar Concentration (Molarity in** $\text{mol dm}^{-3}$**):** $$\text{Molar Concentration } (\text{mol dm}^{-3}) = \frac{\text{Number of moles of solute (mol)}}{\text{Volume of solution (dm}^3)}$$
- **Interconversion:** $$\text{Molar Concentration } (\text{mol dm}^{-3}) = \frac{\text{Concentration } (\text{g dm}^{-3})}{\text{Molar mass } (\text{g mol}^{-1})}$$

---

**7. Stoichiometry & Yield Calculations**

- **Limiting Reactant:** The reactant that is completely consumed first. It limits and determines the maximum theoretical yield of products. All other reactants are in **excess**.
- **Percentage Yield:** $$\% \text{ Yield} = \frac{\text{Actual (Experimental) Yield}}{\text{Theoretical Yield}} \times 100\%$$
- **Percentage Purity:** $$\% \text{ Purity} = \frac{\text{Mass of pure substance}}{\text{Mass of impure sample}} \times 100\%$$

**⚠️ Critical Exam Pitfalls & Grading Standards (Must Memorize):**

- $A_r$ **and** $M_r$ **Accuracy:** Calculate and state all intermediate $A_r$ and $M_r$ values strictly to **2 decimal places (2 d.p.)**.
- **Significant Figures:** All final answers must be expressed strictly to **3 significant figures (3 s.f.)** unless specified otherwise.
- **Deduction Penalties:** Marks are strictly deducted for missing, omitted, or incorrect units, as well as incorrect significant figures.
- **Mass Conversions:** Always convert masses from kilograms ($\text{kg}$) to grams ($\text{g}$) by multiplying by 1000 before calculating moles.
- **Working Statements:** Do not jump straight to calculations. Write explicit working statements identifying the species being calculated (e.g., write "_No. of moles of_ $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$ _= ..._", not just numbers).
- **Ore vs. Pure Substance:** Be mathematically careful to distinguish between an impure ore (e.g., haematite) and the pure chemical compound inside it (e.g., iron(III) oxide) in statements and mass calculations.
