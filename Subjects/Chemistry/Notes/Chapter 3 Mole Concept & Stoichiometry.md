
**1. Relative Masses & Isotopes**

- **Isotopes:** Atoms of the same element with the same number of protons but different number of neutrons12.
- **Relative Isotopic Mass:** The ratio of the mass of one atom of an isotope to $\frac{1}{12}$ of the mass of an atom of ${}^{12}\text{C}$ on the ${}^{12}\text{C}$ scale12.
- **Relative Atomic Mass (**$A_r$**):** The average mass of an atom of an element compared to $\frac{1}{12}$ of the mass of an atom of ${}^{12}\text{C}$ on the ${}^{12}\text{C}$ scale (no units). $$A_r = \sum \left( \text{isotopic mass} \times \text{isotopic abundance} \right)$$
- **Relative Molecular/Formula Mass (**$M_r$**):** The sum of the relative atomic masses ($A_r$) of all atoms in a chemical formula45.

---

**2. The Mole & Molar Mass**

- **The Mole:** The amount of substance containing $6.02 \times 10^{23}$ particles (Avogadro's Constant/Number)67. $$\text{Number of Moles } (n) = \frac{\text{Number of particles}}{6.02 \times 10^{23}}$$
- **Molar Mass:** Mass of $1\text{ mole}$ of a substance (expressed in $\text{g mol}^{-1}$)45.
    - For elements, molar mass $= A_r\text{ g mol}^{-1}$45.
    - For compounds, molar mass $= M_r\text{ g mol}^{-1}$45. $$\text{Formula: } \text{Number of moles } (n) = \frac{\text{Mass of sample (g)}}{\text{Molar mass } (\text{g mol}^{-1})}$$
- _Note:_ Always specify the type of particle (e.g., $1\text{ mole}$ of $\text{O}_2$ molecules weighs $32.0\text{ g}$ and contains $2\text{ moles}$ of oxygen atoms weighing $16.0\text{ g}$ each)6more_horiz.

---

**3. Molar Volume of Gas**

- **Avogadro's Law:** Equal volumes of all gases at the same temperature and pressure contain the same number of particles89.
- **Molar Gas Volume:** One mole of any gas occupies $24.0\text{ dm}^3$ (or $24000\text{ cm}^3$) at room temperature and pressure (**r.t.p.**, $25^\circ\text{C}$ and $1\text{ atm}$)8more_horiz. $$\text{Formula: } \text{Number of moles } (n) = \frac{\text{Volume of gas in dm}^3}{24.0\text{ dm}^3\text{ mol}^{-1}} = \frac{\text{Volume of gas in cm}^3}{24000\text{ cm}^3\text{ mol}^{-1}}$$

---

**4. Percentage Composition & Hydrates**

- **Law of Constant Composition:** The percentage composition of a pure substance does not change1112.
- **Percentage by Mass of an Element:** \[%\text{ of element} = \frac{(\text{Number of atoms of element in formula} \times A_r \text{ of element})}{M_r \text{ of compound}} \times 100% \text{1112}\]
- **Hydrates (Water of Crystallisation):** Refers to water molecules chemically bound within a salt's crystal lattice. \[%\text{ of water in hydrated salt} = \frac{(\text{Number of } \text{H}_2\text{O} \text{ molecules} \times M_r \text{ of } \text{H}_2\text{O})}{M_r \text{ of hydrated compound}} \times 100%\]

---

**5. Empirical & Molecular Formulae**

- **Empirical Formula:** The simplest formula of a compound showing the **simplest whole-number ratio** of atoms of each element1013.
- **Molecular Formula:** Shows the **exact number** of each type of atom present in one molecule1013. $$\text{Molecular Formula} = (\text{Empirical Formula})_n, \quad \text{where } n = \frac{\text{Molar mass of compound}}{\text{Empirical formula mass}}$$

---

**6. Concentration & Volumetric Analysis**

- **Conversion Factor:** $1\text{ dm}^3 = 1000\text{ cm}^3$1013.
- **Concentration Equations:** $$\text{Concentration in g dm}^{-3} = \frac{\text{Mass of solute (g)}}{\text{Volume of solution (dm}^3\text{)}}$$ $$\text{Molarity/Molar Concentration (mol dm}^{-3}\text{)} = \frac{\text{Number of moles of solute (mol)}}{\text{Volume of solution (dm}^3\text{)}}$$
- **Interconverting Concentrations:** $$\text{Molar Concentration (mol dm}^{-3}\text{)} = \frac{\text{Concentration (g dm}^{-3}\text{)}}{\text{Molar mass (g mol}^{-1}\text{)}}$$
- **Titration:** Technique used to find the concentration of an unknown solution (e.g., acid-base neutralisation), using indicators like **methyl orange** to find the exact endpoint14.

---

**7. Stoichiometry & Yield Calculations**

- **Mole Ratio:** Derived directly from the coefficients of balanced equations1516.
- **Gaseous Volume Ratio:** For gaseous reactions, the volume ratio is proportional to the mole ratio at the same temperature and pressure.
- **Limiting Reactant:** The reactant that is completely consumed first. It determines the maximum theoretical yield of product. Reactants left over are in **excess**.
- **Percentage Yield:** \[%\text{ Yield} = \frac{\text{Experimental/Actual Yield}}{\text{Theoretical Yield}} \times 100% \text{17}\]
- **Percentage Purity:** \[%\text{ Purity} = \frac{\text{Mass of pure substance}}{\text{Mass of impure sample}} \times 100% \text{1518}\]
