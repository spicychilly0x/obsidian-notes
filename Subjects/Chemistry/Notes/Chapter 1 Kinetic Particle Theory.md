
**1. Kinetic Particle Theory (KPT) Principles**

- **Definition:** All matter consists of tiny particles in **constant, random motion** (translational, vibrational, or rotational).
- **Energy Relationship:** As temperature increases, particles gain heat energy, which is converted into **average kinetic energy**, causing particles to move faster.

**2. States of Matter Comparison**

|Feature|**Solid**|**Liquid**|**Gas**|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Arrangement**|Closely packed; **orderly**|Closely packed; **disorderly**|**Far apart**; disorderly|
|**Movement**|**Vibrate** about fixed positions|**Slide and roll** past each other|Move **rapidly/randomly**|
|**Forces**|Very strong|Strong (weaker than solid)|**Very weak**|
|**Shape**|Fixed|No fixed shape|No fixed shape|
|**Volume**|Fixed|Fixed|**No fixed volume**|
|**Compressibility**|Incompressible|Slightly compressible|**Very compressible**|
|**Density**|High|High|Low|

**3. Inter-conversion of States**

- **Energy Changes:** Heat is **absorbed** during melting, boiling, evaporation, and sublimation to overcome forces of attraction. Heat is **released** during freezing and condensation as particles form attractions.
- **Sublimation:** Solid changes directly to gas (e.g., **dry ice (**CO2​**), iodine, naphthalene, ammonium chloride**).
- **Evaporation vs. Boiling:**
    - **Boiling:** Occurs only at **fixed boiling point**; throughout the liquid; fast; temperature remains constant.
    - **Evaporation:** Occurs at **any temperature below boiling point**; only at the surface; slow; produces a **cooling effect** (absorbs latent heat from surroundings).

**4. Heating and Cooling Curves**

- **Constant Temperature Points:** During melting or boiling, temperature remains constant because the heat energy is used to **overcome forces of attraction** between particles, not to increase kinetic energy.
- **Cooling Curve Plateau:** During freezing, temperature remains constant because heat energy is **released** as particles move closer to form attractions, which compensates for the heat loss to the surroundings.
- **Average KE:** At any plateau (phase change), the average kinetic energy of the particles remains **constant**.

**5. Factors Affecting State Changes**

- **Purity:** Pure substances have **fixed, sharp** melting and boiling points.
- **Impurities:** These **lower the melting point** and **increase the boiling point**.
- **Pressure:** As atmospheric pressure increases, the **boiling point increases**.
- **Phase Diagram Terms:**
    - **Triple Point:** Temperature/pressure where solid, liquid, and gas coexist.
    - **Critical Point:** Point where liquid and gas are indistinguishable.

**6. Diffusion**

- **Definition:** The random movement of particles from a region of **higher concentration** to **lower concentration**.
- **Factor 1: Temperature:** Higher temperatures increase average kinetic energy, causing particles to move faster and **increasing the rate of diffusion**.
- **Factor 2: Relative Molecular Mass (**Mr​**):** Particles with a **lower** Mr​ travel at greater average speeds and diffuse **faster** than those with a higher Mr​.
- **Key Example:** In the reaction between NH3​ (Mr​=17) and HCl (Mr​=36.46), the white fumes of NH4​Cl form **closer to the** HCl **source** because NH3​ is lighter and diffuses faster.
- **Porous Pot Experiment:** If a gas outside the pot has a lower Mr​ than the gas inside, it enters the pot faster than the internal gas leaves, **increasing internal pressure** (causing liquid levels in a connected U-tube to shift).
