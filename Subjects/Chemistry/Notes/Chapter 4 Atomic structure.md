**2. Properties and Behavior of Sub-Atomic Particles**

- **Fundamental Particles Properties:**

|Sub-Atomic Particle|Relative Charge|Relative Mass|Absolute Mass (g)|Location|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**Proton (**$p$**)**|$+1$|$1$|$1.673 \times 10^{-24}$|Inside Nucleus|
|**Neutron (**$n$**)**|$0$|$1$|$1.675 \times 10^{-24}$|Inside Nucleus|
|**Electron (**$e^-$**)**|(-1)|(\frac{1}{1824}) (or $0.0005$)|$9.109 \times 10^{-28}$|Outside Nucleus (Shells)|

- **Behavior in an Electric Field (Deflection):**
    - **Protons:** Deflect toward the negative plate with a small angle of deflection.
    - **Electrons:** Deflect toward the positive plate with a large angle of deflection.
    - **Neutrons:** Travel straight without deflection because they carry no electric charge.
    - **Key Mathematical Relationship:** $$\text{Angle of Deflection } (\theta) \propto \frac{\text{Charge of particle}}{\text{Mass of particle}}$$ Because electrons have a significantly smaller mass than protons, they undergo a **much larger deflection** than protons under identical electric field strengths.
- **Nuclear Force:** Protons inside the nucleus experience electrostatic repulsion from each other due to like positive charges. However, they are held together tightly in the nucleus alongside neutrons by a short-range, highly powerful **strong nuclear force** which overcomes this electrostatic repulsion.

**3. Atomic Notation, Isotopes, and Isobars**

- **Nuclide Notation:** Represented as ${}^{A}_{Z}\text{X}^{c\pm}$
    - $Z$ **(Atomic/Proton Number):** The total number of protons in the nucleus of an atom. It uniquely defines the identity of the element.
    - $A$ **(Mass/Nucleon Number):** The total sum of protons and neutrons in the nucleus ($A = p + n$).
    - $c\pm$ **(Ionic Charge):** Represents electron gain (anion, (-)) or loss (cation, $+$). In a neutral atom, there is no charge, meaning the number of protons equals the number of electrons ($p = e$).
- **Isotopes:** Atoms of the **same element** containing the **same atomic number** (protons) but **different mass numbers** (due to a different number of neutrons).
    - **Chemical Properties:** **Identical** because they have the same number of valence electrons and identical electronic configurations.
    - **Physical Properties:** **Different** (e.g., density, melting point, boiling point, rate of diffusion) because their masses are different.
    - _Examples to memorize:_ Hydrogen isotopes (Protium ${}^{1}_{1}\text{H}$, Deuterium ${}^{2}_{1}\text{H}$, Tritium ${}^{3}_{1}\text{H}$) and Chlorine isotopes (Chlorine-35 and Chlorine-37).
- **Isobars:** Atoms of **different elements** that share the **same mass number (**$A$**)** but have different atomic numbers ($Z$) (e.g., Potassium-40 ${}^{40}_{19}\text{K}$ and Calcium-40 ${}^{40}_{20}\text{Ca}$). They have completely different chemical and physical properties.

**4. Energy Shells, Subshells, and Orbitals**

- **Principal Quantum Number (**$n$**):** Defines the main energy level or shell. Shells are numbered $n = 1, 2, 3...$ starting closest to the nucleus (lowest energy).
- **Subshells:** Main shells are split into subshells designated as $s, p, d, f$. The number of subshells in any main shell is equal to its principal quantum number $n$:
    - $n = 1$ contains: $1s$
    - $n = 2$ contains: $2s, 2p$
    - $n = 3$ contains: $3s, 3p, 3d$
    - $n = 4$ contains: $4s, 4p, 4d, 4f$
- **Atomic Orbital:** A regional space around the nucleus where there is a high probability (usually (\ge 90%)) of finding an electron.
    - **Maximum Capacity:** Any single orbital can hold a maximum of **2 electrons**.
    - **s orbital:** Spherical in shape. Size and energy increase as $n$ increases ($1s < 2s < 3s$).
    - **p orbital:** Dumbbell-shaped. There are three degenerate (equal energy) p-orbitals ($p_x, p_y, p_z$) oriented perpendicularly along the x, y, and z axes.
- **Crucial Structural Formulas:**
    - **Number of orbitals in shell** $n$ $= n^2$
    - **Maximum number of electrons in shell** $n$ $= 2n^2$
- **Relative Orbital Energies:** Orbitals are filled in order of increasing energy level: $$1s < 2s < 2p < 3s < 3p < 4s < 3d$$ _Note for Exams:_ Due to orbital shielding effects, the $4s$ **subshell is lower in energy than the** $3d$ **subshell** and must be filled first.

**5. Rules for Writing Electronic Configurations**

To write or draw a ground-state electronic configuration of an atom or ion, three rules must be strictly followed:

1. **Aufbau Principle:** Electrons must fill the lowest-energy orbitals available first before occupying higher-energy orbitals (e.g., filling $4s$ before $3d$).
2. **Hund’s Rule of Multiplicity:** When filling degenerate orbitals (orbitals of the same subshell, such as the three $2p$ orbitals), electrons must occupy the orbitals **singly with parallel spins first** before pairing up. This minimizes electrostatic repulsion between electrons.
3. **Pauli Exclusion Principle:** An orbital can hold a maximum of 2 electrons, and these electrons must have **opposite (paired) spins** (represented as (\uparrow\downarrow) in box diagrams).

**6. Writing ground-state electronic configurations (Up to** $Z = 20$**)**

You are required to write electronic configurations in both spdf format and box orbital diagrams up to Calcium ($Z = 20$):

- **Atomic Configurations Examples:**
    - **Carbon (**$Z=6$**):** $1s^2 2s^2 2p^2$ (Condensed: ([\text{He}] 2s^2 2p^2))
    - **Oxygen (**$Z=8$**):** $1s^2 2s^2 2p^4$ (Condensed: ([\text{He}] 2s^2 2p^4))
    - **Sodium (**$Z=11$**):** $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^1$ (Condensed: ([\text{Ne}] 3s^1))
    - **Argon (**$Z=18$**):** $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^6$ (Condensed: ([\text{Ar}]))
    - **Potassium (**$Z=19$**):** $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^6 4s^1$ (Condensed: ([\text{Ar}] 4s^1))
    - **Calcium (**$Z=20$**):** $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^6 4s^2$ (Condensed: ([\text{Ar}] 4s^2))
- **Ion Electronic Configurations:**
    - **Cation (+) Formation (Electron Loss):** Electrons are removed from the valence shell of the **highest principal quantum number (**$n$**)** first.
        - _Example:_ (\text{Na}^+) ion ($10e^-$) loses its $3s^1$ electron: $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6$.
    - **Anion (-) Formation (Electron Gain):** Incoming electrons are added to the vacant orbital of the **lowest energy level available**.
        - _Example:_ (\text{O}^{2-}) ion ($10e^-$) gains two electrons in its $2p$ subshell: $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6$.
- **Isoelectronic Species:** Different atoms, cations, or anions that possess the **identical electronic configuration** (e.g., (\text{O}^{2-}), (\text{F}^-), (\text{Na}^+), and (\text{Mg}^{2+}) are all isoelectronic with Neon, sharing the configuration $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6$).
