
**1. Development of Atomic Theories**

- **Dalton’s Atomic Theory (1800s):** Proposed that all matter is made of indivisible atoms1. Atoms of the same element are identical, atoms of different elements are different, and chemical reactions involve the rearrangement of atoms to form compounds in fixed, simple whole-number ratios1.
- **Thomson’s Plum Pudding Model (1897):** Discovered the electron1. Pictured the atom as a sphere of positive charge with negatively charged electrons embedded within it1.
- **Rutherford’s Nuclear Model (1911):** Based on the alpha-particle scattering experiment23. Replaced the plum pudding model with a nuclear model where the atom is mostly empty space with a tiny, dense, positively charged nucleus at the core containing almost all the mass, and light electrons circulating at some distance34.
- **Bohr’s Model:** Niels Bohr further developed the nuclear model, using experimental evidence to show that electrons occupy particular circular orbits (energy levels or shells) at fixed distances from the nucleus35.

**2. Sub-Atomic Particles**

- **Properties of Fundamental Particles:**
    - **Proton (**$p$**):** Located in the nucleus; relative charge of $+1$; relative mass of $1$6. Absolute mass: $1.673 \times 10^{-24}\text{ g}$4.
    - **Neutron (**$n$**):** Located in the nucleus; relative charge of $0$; relative mass of $1$6. Absolute mass: $1.675 \times 10^{-24}\text{ g}$4.
    - **Electron (**$e^-$**):** Located outside the nucleus in the electron cloud; relative charge of (-1); relative mass of (\frac{1}{1824}) (or $0.0005$)6. Absolute mass: $9.109 \times 10^{-28}\text{ g}$4.
- **Behavior in an Electric Field:**
    - When passed through an electric field, a beam of charged particles deflects6.
    - Positively charged protons deflect toward the negative plate6.
    - Negatively charged electrons deflect toward the positive plate6.
    - Neutrons are neutral and do not deflect6.
    - **Relationship to Memorize:** Electrons undergo a **larger deflection** than protons because their mass is significantly smaller4.
- **Nuclear Force:** While protons in the nucleus repel each other due to electrostatic repulsion, they attract each other and neutrons through a strong **nuclear force** that holds the nucleus together4.

**3. Atomic Notation, Isotopes, and Isobars**

- **Nuclide Notation:** Represented as ${}^{A}_{Z}\text{X}$78
    - **Atomic (Proton) Number (**$Z$**):** The number of protons in the nucleus of an atom (defines the identity of the element)89.
    - **Mass (Nucleon) Number (**$A$**):** The total sum of protons and neutrons in the nucleus ($A = p + n$)9.
    - **Neutral Atom:** Atom is electrically neutral, meaning the number of electrons equals the number of protons ($e = p$)8.
- **Isotopes:** Atoms of the same element with the same atomic (proton) number but different mass (nucleon) numbers (due to a different number of neutrons)10more_horiz.
    - **Chemical Properties:** **Identical** because they have the same number and configuration of electrons14.
    - **Physical Properties:** **Different** (e.g., melting points, densities, rates of diffusion) due to their different mass numbers14more_horiz.
    - _Examples to memorize:_ Hydrogen isotopes (Protium ${}^{1}_{1}\text{H}$, Deuterium ${}^{2}_{1}\text{H}$, Tritium ${}^{3}_{1}\text{H}$)14more_horiz and Chlorine isotopes (Chlorine-35 and Chlorine-37)17more_horiz.
- **Isobars:** Atoms of different elements that have the **same mass number** but different atomic numbers (e.g., Potassium-40 and Calcium-40)1011.

**4. Atomic Shells, Subshells, and Orbitals**

- **Principal Quantum Number (**$n$**):** Corresponds to main energy levels/shells numbered $1, 2, 3...$ with $n = 1$ nearest to the nucleus (lowest energy)5more_horiz.
- **Subshells:** Main shells are split into subshells23. The number of subshells in a shell equals its principal quantum number $n$23.
    - $n = 1$: $1s$2223.
    - $n = 2$: $2s, 2p$22.
    - $n = 3$: $3s, 3p, 3d$22.
    - $n = 4$: $4s, 4p, 4d, 4f$22.
- **Atomic Orbital:** A region in space where there is a high probability of finding an electron24.
    - An orbital can hold a **maximum of 2 electrons**2224.
    - **s orbital:** Spherical shape; size and energy increase as $n$ increases ($1s < 2s < 3s$)2425.
    - **p orbital:** Dumbbell-shaped; three degenerate orbitals ($p_x, p_y, p_z$) oriented perpendicularly to each other2425.
    - **d orbital:** Set of five degenerate orbitals available starting from shell $n = 3$2426.
- **Key Formulas:**
    - **Number of orbitals in shell** $n$ $= n^2$22.
    - **Maximum number of electrons in shell** $n$ $= 2n^2$122.
- **Orbital Energy Ordering:** Orbitals are filled in order of increasing energy2728: $$1s < 2s < 2p < 3s < 3p < 4s < 3d$$ _Note for Exams:_ The $4s$ orbital occupies a lower energy level than $3d$ and is filled first2728.

**5. Rules for Writing Electronic Configurations**

- **Aufbau Principle:** Electrons fill the lowest-energy orbital that is available first2728.
- **Hund’s Rule:** When filling degenerate orbitals (e.g., $2p$ or $3p$), electrons fill the orbitals **singly with the same spin first** before pairing up to minimize electrostatic repulsion29.
- **Pauli Exclusion Principle:** An orbital can hold a maximum of 2 electrons, and they must have **opposite spins**1.
- **Deducing Configurations (Up to** $Z = 20$**):**
    - _Full notation:_ Sodium (${}_{11}\text{Na}$) is $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^1$30.
    - _Condensed notation:_ Uses noble gas cores, e.g., Sodium is ([\text{Ne}] 3s^1)2930; Potassium ($Z=19$) is ([\text{Ar}] 4s^1)30.
    - _Orbital diagram representation:_ Drawn as boxes or lines with arrows ((\uparrow\downarrow))2931.
- **Ion Formation:**
    - **Cation (+) formation:** Formed when neutral atoms lose electrons29. Valence electrons are removed from the shell with the **highest principal quantum number (**$n$**)** first29.
    - **Anion (-) formation:** Formed when neutral atoms gain electrons29. Added to the vacant orbital of **lowest energy**29.
    - _Examples:_ (\text{Li}^+ = 1s^2); (\text{O}^{2-} = 1s^2 2s^2 2p^6); (\text{Al}^{3+} = 1s^2 2s^2 2p^6)32.
- **Isoelectronic Particles:** Different species (atoms or ions) that possess the **same electronic configuration** (e.g., (\text{O}^{2-}), (\text{Al}^{3+}), and (\text{F}^-) all have 10 electrons with the configuration $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6$)3233.
