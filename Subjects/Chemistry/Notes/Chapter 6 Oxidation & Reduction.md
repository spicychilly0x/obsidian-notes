
## 1. Definitions of Redox

Oxidation and reduction occur simultaneously in a chemical reaction. A **redox reaction** can be defined in four ways:

|Concept / Aspect|Oxidation|Reduction|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Oxygen Transfer**|**Gain** of Oxygen|**Loss** of Oxygen|
|**Hydrogen Transfer**|**Loss** of Hydrogen|**Gain** of Hydrogen|
|**Electron Transfer**|**Loss** of Electrons (**OIL**)|**Gain** of Electrons (**RIG**)|
|**Oxidation State (O.S.)**|**Increase** in Oxidation State|**Decrease** in Oxidation State|

---

### 2. Balanced Examples for Definitions

#### **A. Oxygen Gain/Loss**

\[2\text{Mg (s)} + \text{O}_2\text{ (g)} \rightarrow 2\text{MgO (s)}\]

- Magnesium is **oxidised** because it gains oxygen to form $\text{MgO}$.
- Oxygen is **reduced**.

#### **B. Hydrogen Loss/Gain**

$\[\text{H}_2\text{S (g)} + \text{Cl}_2\text{ (g)} \rightarrow 2\text{HCl (g)} + \text{S (s)}\]$

- $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ is **oxidised** because it loses hydrogen to form sulfur ($\text{S}$).
- $\text{Cl}_2$ is **reduced** because it gains hydrogen to form $\text{HCl}$.

#### **C. Electron Loss/Gain (OIL RIG)**

$\[\text{Mg (s)} + \text{Cu}^{2+}\text{ (aq)} \rightarrow \text{Mg}^{2+}\text{ (aq)} + \text{Cu (s)}\]$

- **Oxidation Half-Equation:** $\text{Mg (s)} \rightarrow \text{Mg}^{2+}\text{ (aq)} + 2\text{e}^-$ (Magnesium loses electrons).
- **Reduction Half-Equation:** $\text{Cu}^{2+}\text{ (aq)} + 2\text{e}^- \rightarrow \text{Cu (s)}$ (Copper(II) ions gain electrons).

#### **D. Disproportionation Reactions**

A reaction where the **same reactant** is simultaneously **oxidised and reduced**.

- _Example (Decomposition of Hydrogen Peroxide):_ $\[2\text{H}_2\text{O}_2\text{ (l)} \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O (l)} + \text{O}_2\text{ (g)}\]$
    - In $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$, the oxidation state of Oxygen is $-1$.
    - In $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, the oxidation state of Oxygen decreases to $-2$ (**Reduction**).
    - In $\text{O}_2$, the oxidation state of Oxygen increases to $0$ (**Oxidation**).

---

### 3. Rules for Assigning Oxidation States (O.S.)

Oxidation state (or oxidation number) represents the charge an atom would have if all bonds were purely ionic.

1. **Free Elements:** The O.S. of any uncombined element is exactly **$0$** (e.g., $\text{Na}, \text{H}_2, \text{O}_2, \text{Cl}_2, \text{S}_8 = 0$).
2. **Simple Monatomic Ions:** The O.S. is equal to the ionic charge (e.g., $\text{Na}^+ = +1$, $\text{Cl}^- = -1$, $\text{Fe}^{3+} = +3$, $\text{S}^{2-} = -2$).
3. **Oxygen in Compounds:** Always **$-2$**, with two exceptions:
    - In **peroxides** (e.g., $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$), Oxygen is **$-1$**.
    - In fluorine compounds (e.g., $\text{OF}_2$), Oxygen is **$+2$**.
4. **Hydrogen in Compounds:** Always **$+1$** when bonded to non-metals (e.g., $\text{H}_2\text{O}, \text{HCl}$), but is **$-1$** in metal hydrides (e.g., $\text{NaH}, \text{CaH}_2$).
5. **Neutral Compounds:** The sum of all oxidation states of atoms in a neutral compound must equal **$0$**.
    - _Example (Finding C in $\text{CaCO}_3$):_ $\[(+2) [\text{Ca}] + (\text{O.S. of C}) + 3(-2) [\text{O}] = 0\] \[+2 + \text{C} - 6 = 0 \Rightarrow \text{C} = +4\]$
6. **Polyatomic Ions:** The sum of all oxidation states equals the charge on the ion.
    - _Example (Finding S in $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$):_ $\[(\text{O.S. of S}) + 4(-2) [\text{O}] = -2\] \[\text{S} - 8 = -2 \Rightarrow \text{S} = +6\]$

⚠️ **Important Exam Notation Rules:**

- Always write the **sign ($+$ or $-$) in front of the number** (e.g., write **$+2$**, NOT $2+$).
- Specify the O.S. of a **single, individual atom**, not the overall group or compound.

### 4. Oxidising and Reducing Agents

- **Oxidising Agent:** A substance that oxidises another substance and is itself **reduced** (gains electrons, loses oxygen, gains hydrogen, or decreases in oxidation state).
- **Reducing Agent:** A substance that reduces another substance and is itself **oxidised** (loses electrons, gains oxygen, loses hydrogen, or increases in oxidation state).

#### **Tests for Oxidising and Reducing Agents (Must Memorize):**

|Test Reagent|Type|Observable Observation|Chemical Explanation|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Acidified Potassium Manganate(VII)** $(\text{KMnO}_4)$|Strong **Oxidising Agent** (used to detect a reducing agent)|Colour change from **purple** to **colourless** (decolourised).|Purple manganate(VII) ions $(\text{MnO}_4^-)$ are reduced by the analyte to colourless manganese(II) ions $(\text{Mn}^{2+})$. _Example:_ $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ is oxidised to $\text{Fe}^{3+}$.|
|**Aqueous Potassium Iodide** $(\text{KI})$|Strong **Reducing Agent** (used to detect an oxidising agent)|Colour change from **colourless** to **yellow-brown** solution.|Colourless iodide ions $(\text{I}^-)$ are oxidised by the analyte to brown iodine $(\text{I}_2)$. _Example:_ $\text{Cl}_2$ gas is reduced to $\text{Cl}^-$.|
