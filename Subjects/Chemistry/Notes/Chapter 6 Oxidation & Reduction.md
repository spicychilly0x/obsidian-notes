**1. Definitions of Oxidation and Reduction**

Oxidation and reduction always occur simultaneously; a reaction in which both take place is a **redox reaction**.

|Definition|**Oxidation**|**Reduction**|
|---|---|---|
|**Oxygen**|**Gain** of oxygen|**Loss** of oxygen|
|**Hydrogen**|**Loss** of hydrogen|**Gain** of hydrogen|
|**Electrons**|**Loss** of electrons (**OIL**)|**Gain** of electrons (**RIG**)|
|**Oxidation State (O.S.)**|**Increase** in oxidation state|**Decrease** in oxidation state|

---

**2. Chemical Examples of Redox Reactions**

**A. In Terms of Oxygen Transfer**

- **Reaction:** $$\text{PbO (s)} + \text{C (s)} \rightarrow \text{Pb (s)} + \text{CO (g)}$$
    - **Oxidised:** $\text{C}$ because it gains oxygen to form $\text{CO}$.
    - **Reduced:** $\text{PbO}$ because it loses oxygen to form $\text{Pb}$.

**B. In Terms of Hydrogen Transfer**

- **Reaction:** $$\text{H}_2\text{S (g)} + \text{Cl}_2\text{ (g)} \rightarrow 2\text{HCl (g)} + \text{S (s)}$$
    - **Oxidised:** $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ because it loses hydrogen to form $\text{S}$.
    - **Reduced:** $\text{Cl}_2$ because it gains hydrogen to form $\text{HCl}$.
- **Methane Combustion:** $$\text{CH}_4\text{ (g)} + 2\text{O}_2\text{ (g)} \rightarrow \text{CO}_2\text{ (g)} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O (l)}$$
    - $\text{CH}_4$ is oxidised (loses hydrogen and gains oxygen).
    - $\text{O}_2$ is reduced (gains hydrogen).

**C. In Terms of Electron Transfer (OIL RIG)**

- **Half-Equations:** Write separate equations to show electron loss or gain. Charges must balance on both sides.
    - **Oxidation (Loss):** $$\text{Mg (s)} \rightarrow \text{Mg}^{2+}\text{ (aq)} + 2\text{e}^-$$
    - **Reduction (Gain):** $$2\text{H}^+\text{ (aq)} + 2\text{e}^- \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{ (g)}$$
- **Spectator Ions:** Ions present in the reaction mixture that do not undergo any chemical change (e.g., $\text{K}^+$ in halogen displacement).
- **Net Ionic Equation Example (Halogen Displacement):** $$2\text{KI (aq)} + \text{Cl}_2\text{ (aq)} \rightarrow 2\text{KCl (aq)} + \text{I}_2\text{ (aq)}$$
    - _Oxidation half-equation:_ $2\text{I}^-\text{ (aq)} \rightarrow \text{I}_2\text{ (aq)} + 2\text{e}^-$
    - _Reduction half-equation:_ $\text{Cl}_2\text{ (aq)} + 2\text{e}^- \rightarrow 2\text{Cl}^-\text{ (aq)}$
    - _Overall ionic equation:_ $2\text{I}^-\text{ (aq)} + \text{Cl}_2\text{ (aq)} \rightarrow 2\text{Cl}^-\text{ (aq)} + \text{I}_2\text{ (aq)}$

**D. Disproportionation Reactions**

- **Definition:** A specific type of redox reaction where the **same reactant** is simultaneously **oxidised and reduced** to form different products.
- **Example (Decomposition of Hydrogen Peroxide):** $$2\text{H}_2\text{O}_2\text{ (l)} \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O (l)} + \text{O}_2\text{ (g)}$$
    - Oxygen in $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ has an O.S. of $-1$.
    - It is **reduced** to $-2$ in $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and **oxidised** to $0$ in $\text{O}_2$.

**3. Rules for Assigning Oxidation States (O.S.)**

The oxidation state is the charge an atom of an element would have if it existed as an ion in a compound.

1. **Uncombined Elements:** O.S. is always **0** (e.g., $\text{Na}$, $\text{Cu}$, $\text{Ar}$, $\text{H}_2$, $\text{O}_2$, $\text{Cl}_2$, $\text{S}_8 = 0$).
2. **Monatomic Ions:** O.S. is equal to the charge of the ion (e.g., $\text{Fe}^{2+} = +2$, $\text{O}^{2-} = -2$).
3. **Oxygen:** O.S. is always $-2$ in compounds, with two exceptions:
    - In **peroxides** (e.g., $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$, $\text{Na}_2\text{O}_2$), O.S. is $-1$.
    - In $\text{OF}_2$, O.S. is $+2$.
4. **Hydrogen:** O.S. is always $+1$ when bonded to non-metals (e.g., $\text{H}_2\text{O}$), but is $-1$ in reactive metal hydrides (e.g., $\text{NaH}$, $\text{CaH}_2$).
5. **Fluorine:** O.S. is always $-1$ in all its compounds.
6. **Neutral Compounds:** The sum of the O.S. of all atoms must equal **0** (e.g., in $\text{CaCO}_3$: $+2 [\text{Ca}] + \text{C} + 3(-2) [\text{O}] = 0 \Rightarrow \text{C} = +4$).
7. **Polyatomic Ions:** The sum of the O.S. of all atoms must equal the overall charge of the ion (e.g., in $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$: $\text{S} + 4(-2) [\text{O}] = -2 \Rightarrow \text{S} = +6$).

---

**4. Elements with Fixed vs. Variable Oxidation States**

**Fixed O.S. in Compounds:**

- **Group 1 metals:** Always $+1$
- **Group 2 metals:** Always $+2$
- **Group 13 metals (e.g., Al):** Always $+3$

**Variable O.S. in Compounds:**

- **Group 17 (Halogens):** Normally $-1$, but can have positive O.S. (up to $+7$) when bonded to more electronegative elements like Oxygen or Fluorine (e.g., $\text{Cl}$ in $\text{IO}_4^-$).
- **Group 16 (excluding O):** Can be $-2$, but also positive, e.g., sulfur is $+4$ in $\text{SO}_2$ and $+6$ in $\text{SO}_3$ and $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$.
- **Group 15 (Nitrogen):** Ranges from $-3$ (in $\text{NH}_3$) to $+3$ (in $\text{NO}_2^-$) and $+5$ (in $\text{NO}_3^-$).
- **Group 14 (Carbon):** Ranges from $-4$ (in $\text{CH}_4$) to $+4$ (in $\text{CO}_2$, $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$).
- **Transition Metals:** Variable O.S. (e.g., $\text{Fe}$ is $+2$ or $+3$; $\text{Mn}$ can be $+2, +4, +7$).

---

**5. Identifying Non-Redox Reactions**

If there is **no change in the oxidation state** of any element in a reaction, it is **not** a redox reaction.

- **Precipitation Reactions:** $$\text{Ag}^+\text{ (aq)} + \text{Cl}^-\text{ (aq)} \rightarrow \text{AgCl (s)}\quad (\text{All O.S. remain: } \text{Ag}=+1, \text{Cl}=-1)$$
- **Neutralisation Reactions:** $$\text{NaOH (aq)} + \text{HCl (aq)} \rightarrow \text{NaCl (aq)} + \text{H}_2\text{O (l)}\quad (\text{All O.S. remain: } \text{Na}=+1, \text{O}=-2, \text{H}=+1, \text{Cl}=-1)$$

---

**6. Oxidising and Reducing Agents**

- **Oxidising Agent (Oxidant):** A reactant that oxidises another substance and is itself **reduced** (O.S. decreases, gains electrons, loses oxygen, or gains hydrogen).
- **Reducing Agent (Reductant):** A reactant that reduces another substance and is itself **oxidised** (O.S. increases, loses electrons, gains oxygen, or loses hydrogen).

**⚠️ Critical Exam Pitfalls & Grading Standards (Must Memorize):**

- **O.S. Sign Placement:** Always write the positive or negative sign **directly in front** of the oxidation state number (e.g., $+2$, $-1$). Writing it after (e.g., $2+$, $1-$) represents ionic charge, not oxidation state, and will result in a loss of marks.
- **O.S. Element Specification:** When describing an oxidation state change, specify the O.S. of a **single atom/element**, not the whole ion or compound (e.g., write "_the oxidation state of_ $\text{Fe}$ _increases from_ $+2$ _to_ $+3$", NOT "_the oxidation state of_ $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ _increases_").
- **Explaining Redox in Exams:** To score full marks on "Explain why this is a redox reaction" questions, you must:
    1. State which element is **oxidised** and refer to its specific **initial and final oxidation states**.
    2. State which element is **reduced** and refer to its specific **initial and final oxidation states**.
    3. Conclude by stating that oxidation and reduction occurred simultaneously.
