
**1. Principles of Homeostasis**

- **Definition**: The maintenance of a relatively **constant internal environment** within a **narrow range**, regardless of significant external changes.
- **Variables Regulated**:
    - **Body Temperature**: ~37°C (optimal for enzyme function).
    - **Water Potential**: In body fluids.
    - **Blood Glucose Levels**.
    - **Body Fluid pH**.
- **Negative Feedback Mechanism**: The primary regulatory process where a change in a physiological variable triggers a response that **counteracts** the initial change to return the system to normal.
- **Regulatory Components**: **Stimulus** (the change) → **Receptor/Sensor** (detects change) → **Control Centre** → **Effector** → **Corrective Mechanism**.

**2. Regulation of Blood Glucose**

- **Glucose Rise (e.g., after a meal)**:
    - **Receptor**: Islets of Langerhans in the **pancreas** are stimulated.
    - **Corrective Mechanism**: Secretion of **more insulin** into the blood.
    - **Effect**: Increased permeability of cell membranes to glucose; **liver and muscles convert excess glucose to glycogen** for storage.
- **Glucose Fall (e.g., exercise or fasting)**:
    - **Receptor**: Islets of Langerhans in the pancreas stimulated.
    - **Corrective Mechanism**: Secretion of **more glucagon**.
    - **Effect**: Stimulates the **conversion of stored glycogen back to glucose**, which enters the bloodstream.

**3. Osmoregulation (Water Potential)**

- **Water Potential Fall (e.g., profuse sweating)**:
    - **Receptor**: **Osmoreceptors in the hypothalamus** (brain) stimulated.
    - **Corrective Mechanism**: **Posterior pituitary gland** secretes **more ADH**.
    - **Effect**: **Collecting ducts** in kidneys become **more permeable**; more water is reabsorbed into blood; result is a **small volume of concentrated urine**.
- **Water Potential Rise (e.g., excess drinking)**:
    - **Receptor**: Osmoreceptors in the hypothalamus stimulated.
    - **Corrective Mechanism**: Posterior pituitary secretes **less ADH**.
    - **Effect**: Collecting ducts become **less permeable**; less water reabsorbed; result is a **large volume of dilute urine**.

**4. Comparative Osmoregulation in Fish**

- **Marine (Bony Fish)**: Environment is **hypertonic** (dehydrating).
    - **Challenge**: Constant water loss through gills/skin.
    - **Solution**: Drink large amounts of seawater; little urine production; **active transport** at gills to remove excess salt.
- **Freshwater Fish**: Environment is **hypotonic**.
    - **Challenge**: Taking in too much water and losing salt.
    - **Solution**: Produce lots of dilute urine; use **active transport** to take in salt at the gills.

**5. Structure of Mammalian Skin**

- **Epidermis**:
    - **Outer Cornified Layer**: Dead, dry cells; contains **keratin**; water-resistant; prevents mechanical injury and microbe entry.
    - **Granular Layer**: Living cells moving upward to replace the cornified layer.
    - **Malpighian Layer**: Continual cell division; contains **pigment** to protect against UV rays.
- **Dermis**:
    - **Arterioles**: Controlled by nerves for **vasodilation/vasoconstriction**.
    - **Sweat Glands**: Coiled tubes; remove urea and regulate temperature via evaporation.
    - **Sensory Receptors**: Thermoreceptors (heat), touch, and pressure receptors.
    - **Adipose Tissue**: Sub-cutaneous fat; provides insulation to prevent heat loss.

**6. Thermoregulation (Temperature Control)**

- **Heat Gain Sources**: Respiration (liver/muscles), exercise, sun radiation, warm air, and **shivering**.
- **Heat Loss Mechanisms**: **Radiation**, **Convection**, **Conduction**, and **Evaporation** of sweat (removes latent heat).
- **Response to a Hot Day (Temperature Rise)**:
    - **Vasodilation**: Arterioles in skin **dilate**; **shunt vessels constrict**; more blood flows to skin capillaries for greater heat loss.
    - **Sweating**: Sweat glands more active; more latent heat lost as sweat evaporates.
    - **Muscles**: **Hair erector muscles relax** (hair lies flat); metabolic rate decreases.
- **Response to a Cold Day (Temperature Fall)**:
    - **Vasoconstriction**: Arterioles in skin **constrict**; **shunt vessels dilate**; less blood flows to skin capillaries to reduce heat loss.
    - **Sweating**: Sweat glands less active.
    - **Muscles**: **Hair erector muscles contract** (hairs stand up to trap an insulating layer of air); **shivering** (reflex skeletal muscle contraction to generate heat); metabolic rate increases.

**7. Heat Source Terminology**

- **Ectotherm**: Derives body temperature from **external** heat sources.
- **Endotherm**: Derives body temperature from **internal** heat production.
- **Poikilotherm**: Body temperature approximates ambient (external) temperature.
- **Homoiotherm**: Controls body temperature to keep it relatively **constant**.
