**1. Diffusion**

- **Definition**: The **passive movement** of molecules or ions from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration.
- **Concentration Gradient**: The difference in concentration between two regions.
- **Factors Increasing Rate**:
    - **Steeper concentration gradient**.
    - **Smaller particle size**.
    - **Higher temperature**: Increases kinetic energy of particles.
    - **Thinner membrane**: Creates a shorter diffusion pathway.
    - **Larger surface area**.
    - **Shorter distance** between regions.

**2. Osmosis**

- **Definition**: The passive movement of **water molecules** from a region of higher water potential to a region of lower water potential across a **partially-permeable membrane**.
- **Water Potential**: A measure of the tendency of water to move.
- **Relationship**: Dilute solutions have **higher water potential** (more water molecules per unit volume) than concentrated solutions.
- **Mechanism**: Water molecules actually move in **both directions** across the membrane, but the **net movement** is toward the region of lower water potential.

**3. Tonicity and its Effects on Cells**

|Term|Definition|Net Water Movement|
|---|---|---|
|**Isotonic**|Solute concentration is equal to cell cytoplasm.|No net movement; cell size remains unchanged.|
|**Hypertonic**|Higher solute concentration (lower water potential) than cell.|**Out of the cell**.|
|**Hypotonic**|Lower solute concentration (higher water potential) than cell.|**Into the cell**.|

- **Animal Cells (e.g., Red Blood Cells)**:
    - **In Hypotonic**: Cell swells and eventually **bursts (lysis)** because it lacks a protective cell wall.
    - **In Hypertonic**: Cell shrinks and develops spikes, a process called **crenation**.
- **Plant Cells**:
    - **In Hypotonic**: Vacuole enlarges, pushing contents against the wall. The cell becomes **turgid**. The strong, relatively inelastic **cell wall** exerts **turgor pressure** to prevent bursting.
    - **In Hypertonic**: Vacuole shrinks and the cytoplasm/cell membrane pull away from the cell wall, a process called **plasmolysis**.

**4. Facilitated Diffusion**

- **Definition**: Passive transport of molecules/ions across a membrane assisted by specific **protein channels or carriers**.
- **Reason**: Large or strongly charged molecules cannot dissolve in the non-polar phospholipid bilayer.
- **Channel Proteins**: Form pores lined with **polar groups** to allow charged ions (K⁺, Na⁺, Ca²⁺, Cl⁻) to pass through.
- **Carrier Proteins**: Bind specific larger polar molecules (e.g., **sugars, amino acids**), causing the protein to **change shape** to deliver the molecule to the other side.

**5. Active Transport**

- **Definition**: Movement of materials through a plasma membrane **against a concentration gradient** (low to high concentration).
- **Key Requirements**: Requires **respiratory energy (ATP)** and specific carrier protein "pumps".
- **Cell Characteristics**: Cells performing active transport have many **mitochondria** and a high rate of respiration.
- **Factors Affecting Rate**: Temperature, oxygen concentration, and metabolic poisons (e.g., cyanide).
- **Examples to Memorize**:
    - Absorption of **mineral salts** by plant root hair cells.
    - Absorption of **glucose and amino acids** in the villi of the small intestine.
    - Nerve impulse transmission and muscle contraction.
    - Excretion of urea by the kidney.

**6. Bulk Transport (Active Processes)**

- **Endocytosis**: Cellular uptake of large quantities of material by wrapping the plasma membrane around it to form a **vesicle**.
    - **Phagocytosis**: "Cell eating" of solid materials (e.g., white blood cells engulfing bacteria).
    - **Pinocytosis**: "Cell drinking" of liquid materials.
- **Exocytosis**: Secretion of materials (e.g., **digestive enzymes, hormones**, or mucus) by fusing internal vesicles with the plasma membrane.

**7. Surface Area-to-Volume Ratio (SA:V)**

- **Key Concept**: As cell size increases, its **volume increases faster than its surface area**, leading to a **decrease** in the SA:V ratio.
- **Efficiency**: A larger SA:V ratio allows a cell to obtain nutrients and remove wastes more efficiently.
- **Limits on Cell Size**:
    - Too large: Cannot exchange materials fast enough to sustain life.
    - Too small: Cannot fit necessary organelles to function correctly.
