
**1. Definitions and Foundations**

- **Hormone**: A chemical substance produced in minute quantities by an endocrine gland, transported by the blood to one or more specific target organs, where it alters their activity.
- **Lifecycle**: After performing its function, a hormone is **destroyed by the liver**.
- **Endocrine Glands (Ductless)**: Secrete hormones **directly into the bloodstream** (e.g., Pituitary, Adrenal, Pancreas).
- **Exocrine Glands**: Secrete products (e.g., sweat, saliva, digestive juices) through **ducts** to the external environment or organ cavities.

**2. Key Glands and Their Functions**

- **Pituitary Gland ("Master Gland")**: Secretes several hormones that control other endocrine glands; also secretes **Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH)**.
- **Hypothalamus**: Regulates the secretion of pituitary hormones and coordinates the adrenal response to stress.
- **Thyroid Gland**: Secretes **thyroxine**, which controls metabolic rate and physical development.
- **Adrenal Gland (Medulla)**: Secretes **adrenaline** for "fight or flight" situations.
- **Pancreas (Islets of Langerhans)**: Secretes **insulin** and **glucagon** to regulate blood glucose concentration.
- **Gonads**: **Ovaries** (oestrogen and progesterone) and **Testes** (testosterone).

**3. Blood Glucose Regulation**

- **Insulin (Decreases Blood Glucose)**:
    - Increases cell membrane permeability to glucose, enhancing uptake.
    - Stimulates liver and muscle cells to convert **glucose into glycogen** for storage.
    - Increases glucose oxidation during tissue respiration.
    - **Deficiency**: Leads to Diabetes Mellitus.
    - **Over-secretion**: Causes low blood glucose, leading to **shock, coma, or death**.
- **Glucagon (Increases Blood Glucose)**:
    - Main target organ is the **liver**.
    - Stimulates conversion of **glycogen into glucose**.
    - Stimulates conversion of fats, amino acids, and lactic acid into glucose.

**4. Diabetes Mellitus**

- **Definition**: A condition where the body cannot sufficiently control blood glucose levels due to a lack of insulin.
- **Signs to Memorize**:
    - Persistently high blood glucose concentration.
    - Presence of **glucose in urine** (exceeds kidney's reabsorption capacity).
    - Slow and difficult healing of wounds.
    - Weakness and weight loss; production of poisonous **ketones** due to fat oxidation.
- **Type 1 (Juvenile/Early-onset)**: Islets of Langerhans fail to produce enough insulin. **Treatment**: Insulin injections.
- **Type 2 (Late-onset)**: Target cells fail to respond to insulin. **Treatment**: Dietary control, exercise, and lifestyle changes.

**5. Adrenaline ("Fight or Flight")**

- **Secretion Pathway**: Stimuli (fear, stress) → Hypothalamus → Spinal cord → Adrenal medulla → Adrenaline released into blood.
- **Key Effects to Memorize**:
    - **Energy**: Increases blood glucose (glycogen → glucose) and metabolic rate.
    - **Circulation**: Increases heart rate/blood pressure; constricts arterioles to divert blood to muscles; increases blood coagulation rate.
    - **Respiration**: Increases rate and depth of ventilation.
    - **Vision**: Dilates pupils to enhance sight.
    - **Other**: Contracts hair muscles ("goose pimples").

**6. Hormonal vs. Nervous Systems**

|Feature|**Nervous System**|**Hormonal System**|
|---|---|---|
|**Signal Type**|Electrical (nerve impulses)|Chemical (hormones)|
|**Transmission**|Via neurones|Via blood|
|**Speed of Response**|Usually quick|Usually slow|
|**Duration**|Short-lived|Short-lived or long-lived|
|**Scope of Effect**|Localised (specific part)|Widespread (multiple organs)|
|**Control**|Voluntary or involuntary|**Always involuntary**|
