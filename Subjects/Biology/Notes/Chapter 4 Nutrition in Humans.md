
**1. Core Digestive Processes**

- **Ingestion**: Taking food into the body.
- **Digestion**: Breaking food into small, soluble molecules.
- **Absorption**: Uptake of soluble molecules into cells.
- **Assimilation**: Use of absorbed food to build new protoplasm or provide energy.
- **Egestion**: Passing out undigested materials (faeces).

**2. Physical vs. Chemical Digestion**

- **Physical Digestion**: Mechanical breakdown (chewing, churning, **emulsification**).
    - **Goal**: Increases **surface area to volume (SA:V) ratio** so enzymes act more efficiently.
- **Chemical Digestion**: Breaking down large molecules (starch, proteins, fats) into small soluble ones.
    - **Mechanism**: **Hydrolytic reactions** catalyzed by digestive enzymes.

**3. Movement of Food: Peristalsis**

- **Definition**: Rhythmic, wave-like contractions of gut walls to mix and move food.
- **Muscle Layers**: **Circular muscles** (inside) and **longitudinal muscles** (outside).
- **Antagonistic Action**:
    - **To push food forward**: Circular muscles contract, longitudinal muscles relax → gut wall constricts (narrower and longer).
    - **To allow food entry**: Circular muscles relax, longitudinal muscles contract → gut wall dilates (wider and shorter).

**5. Absorption Adaptations (Small Intestine)**

- **Length**: Longest part of the gut to maximize time for absorption.
- **Surface Area**: Folded internal walls, **villi**, and **microvilli** (on epithelial cells).
- **Efficiency**:
    - **One-cell thick epithelium**: Short diffusion pathway.
    - **Capillary network**: Rapidly carries away sugars/amino acids to maintain concentration gradient.
    - **Lacteals**: Absorbs fatty acids and glycerol.

**6. Transport and the Liver**

- **Hepatic Portal Vein**: Transports nutrient-rich, deoxygenated blood from the ileum to the liver.
- **Liver Functions (Must Memorize)**:
    1. **Glucose Regulation**: **Insulin** stimulates conversion of glucose to **glycogen** (storage); **Glucagon** converts it back when blood sugar is low.
    2. **Bile Production**: Emulsifies fats.
    3. **Deamination**: Removal of amino groups (−NH2​) from excess amino acids to form **urea** (excreted in urine).
    4. **Protein Synthesis**: Makes blood proteins like **fibrinogen**.
    5. **Detoxification**: Converts harmful substances (e.g., alcohol) into harmless ones.
    6. **Iron Storage**: Stores iron from destroyed red blood cells.

**7. Large Intestine and Fibre**

- **Function**: Absorbs water and mineral salts from undigested material.
- **Dietary Fibre**: Provides bulk for muscles to push against; prevents **constipation** by speeding up movement and preventing over-absorption of water.
