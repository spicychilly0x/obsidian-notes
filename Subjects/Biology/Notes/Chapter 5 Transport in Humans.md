
**1. The Circulatory System Basics**

- **Need for Transport**: Multicellular organisms are too large for diffusion to be efficient; they require special systems to transport materials faster to sustain life.
- **Closed System**: Blood is contained within a continuous network of vessels.
- **Three Components**:
    1. **Pump**: The heart.
    2. **Tubes**: Blood vessels (arteries, arterioles, capillaries, venules, veins).
    3. **Medium**: Blood.

**2. Double Circulation (Mammals)**

- **Definition**: Blood passes through the heart **twice** for every one complete circuit of the body.
- **Pulmonary Circulation**: Heart → Pulmonary arteries → Lungs (for gas exchange) → Pulmonary veins → Heart.
    - **Pressure**: Lower pressure to allow time for blood to be well-oxygenated.
- **Systemic Circulation**: Heart → Aorta/Arteries → Body tissues → Veins/Venae Cavae → Heart.
    - **Pressure**: Higher pressure to deliver oxygenated blood quickly to tissues.
- **Advantages**: Optimized pressure for different functions; maintains high metabolic rates; prevents mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood.

**3. The Human Heart: Structure & Function**

- **Cardiac Muscle**: Heart is made of specialized muscles that contract to pump blood.
- **Chambers**:
    - **Atria (Upper)**: Thinner walls; force blood into ventricles.
    - **Ventricles (Lower)**: Thicker walls to pump blood out at high pressure.
    - **Left Ventricle**: Thickest wall because it pumps blood to the **entire body**.
- **Median Septum**: Muscular wall preventing the mixing of oxygenated (left) and deoxygenated (right) blood.
- **Valves (Ensures one-way flow)**:
    - **Atrioventricular (AV) Valves**: Tricuspid (right) and Bicuspid/Mitral (left). They close during ventricular contraction to prevent backflow into atria (**"Lub"** sound).
    - **Semilunar Valves**: Located at the start of the aorta and pulmonary artery. They close during ventricular relaxation to prevent backflow into ventricles (**"Dub"** sound).
- **Coronary Arteries**: Small arteries emerging from the aorta that supply the heart muscle itself with nutrients and oxygen.

**4. The Cardiac Cycle**

- **Definition**: Sequence of events in one heartbeat (approx. 0.8 seconds).
- **Phases**:
    1. **Atrial and Ventricular Diastole**: All chambers relax and fill passively.
    2. **Atrial Systole**: Atria contract, adding the final 20% of blood to the ventricles.
    3. **Ventricular Systole**: Ventricles contract; AV valves close ("Lub"); pressure opens semilunar valves; blood is forced into arteries.
    4. **Ventricular Diastole**: Ventricles relax; semilunar valves close ("Dub") as arterial pressure exceeds ventricular pressure; AV valves reopen for refilling.

**5. Control of Heart Rate**

- **SA Node (Pacemaker)**: Located in the right atrium; initiates electrical signals for atrial contraction.
- **Nervous Control**: Medulla in the brainstem senses high CO2​.
    - **Cardiac Nerve**: Increases heart rate during exercise.
    - **Vagus Nerve**: Decreases heart rate back to normal.
- **Hormonal Control**: **Adrenaline** causes the SA node to fire more frequently, increasing heart rate during stress/excitement.

**6. Blood Vessels Comparison**

|Feature|**Artery**|**Vein**|**Capillary**|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Function**|Away from heart|Towards heart|Exchange of materials|
|**Wall**|Thick, muscular, elastic|Thin, less muscular/elastic|**One-cell thick** (endothelium)|
|**Pressure**|High (pulsatile)|Low (smooth flow)|Very low|
|**Lumen**|Small|Large|Tiny (size of one RBC)|
|**Valves**|Absent (except semilunar)|**Present**|Absent|

**7. Blood Composition & Functions**

- **Plasma (55%)**: Pale yellow liquid (90% water). Transports nutrients, ions, CO2​, urea, hormones, and proteins (fibrinogen, antibodies).
- **Red Blood Cells (Erythrocytes)**: Biconcave shape (high SA:V), **no nucleus** (more space for Hb), elastic (can turn bell-shaped in capillaries).
- **White Blood Cells (Leucocytes)**:
    - **Lymphocytes**: Large round nucleus; produce **antibodies** to clump/destroy bacteria or neutralize toxins.
    - **Phagocytes**: Lobed nucleus; perform **phagocytosis** (ingesting/digesting foreign particles).
- **Platelets**: Membrane-bound cytoplasm fragments essential for clotting.

**8. Blood Clotting Mechanism**

1. **Thrombokinase**: Enzyme released by damaged tissue and platelets.
2. **Prothrombin** → **Thrombin**: Thrombokinase (with **Calcium ions**) converts inactive prothrombin into active thrombin.
3. **Fibrinogen** → **Fibrin**: Thrombin converts soluble fibrinogen into **insoluble fibrin threads**.
4. **Clot Formation**: Fibrin mesh entangles blood cells to seal the wound.

**9. ABO Blood Groups**

- **Antigens**: Special proteins on RBC surfaces (A and B).
- **Antibodies**: Natural proteins in plasma (a and b).
- **Agglutination**: Dangerous clumping that occurs if recipient's antibodies match donor's antigens (e.g., Antibody a with Antigen A).
- **Universal Donor**: **Type O** (no antigens on RBCs).
- **Universal Recipient**: **Type AB** (no antibodies in plasma).

**10. Lymphatic System**

- **Functions**:
    1. Returns excess tissue fluid (10%) to blood circulation.
    2. Transports **fats** from the small intestine.
    3. Filters foreign particles in **lymph nodes**.
- **Flow**: Very slow; no pump; depends on skeletal muscle contraction and valves to maintain one-way flow towards the heart.
