
**1. Definitions and Importance**

- **Excretion**: The process by which **metabolic waste products** and **toxic materials** (e.g., urea, excess salts) are removed from an organism’s body.
- **Urea**: A nitrogenous waste produced in the **liver** from the **deamination of excess amino acids**.
- **Importance**: Prevents the accumulation of wastes that may become toxic to the body.

**2. The Mammalian Urinary System**

- **Renal Artery**: Supplies the kidney with oxygenated blood containing nitrogenous wastes.
- **Renal Vein**: Drains filtered (clean) blood from the kidney.
- **Kidney**: Filters blood to form urine.
- **Ureter**: A tube that carries urine from the renal pelvis to the urinary bladder.
- **Urinary Bladder**: A muscular sac that temporarily stores urine.
- **Urethra**: The tube through which urine is expelled from the body.

**3. Kidney Anatomy**

- **Renal Cortex**: The outer region of the kidney.
- **Renal Medulla**: The inner region, containing renal pyramids.
- **Renal Pelvis**: The central cavity where urine collects before entering the ureter.
- **Nephron**: The functional unit of the kidney, spanning the cortex and medulla.

**4. Structure and Function of the Nephron**

- **Malpighian (Renal) Corpuscle**: Site of **ultrafiltration**; consists of the **Glomerulus** (capillary knot) and the **Bowman’s Capsule** (renal capsule).
- **Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT)**: Primary site for **selective reabsorption**.
- **Loop of Henle**: A U-shaped portion that extends into the medulla; involved in water reabsorption.
- **Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT)**: Involved in the reabsorption of specific salts (e.g., Na+) and water.
- **Collecting Duct**: Collects urine from several nephrons and carries it to the renal pelvis; site of final water reabsorption under the influence of **ADH**.

**5. Urine Formation**

**Phase 1: Ultrafiltration**

- **Location**: Malpighian corpuscle.
- **Mechanism**: The **afferent arteriole** (bringing blood in) is **wider** than the **efferent arteriole** (taking blood out), creating high hydrostatic blood pressure in the glomerulus.
- **Filtration**: This pressure forces water and small molecules (glucose, amino acids, mineral salts, urea) through the **partially permeable basement membrane** into the Bowman’s capsule.
- **Remaining in Blood**: Large molecules (proteins, fats), blood cells, and platelets are too large to pass through and remain in the blood.

**Phase 2: Selective Reabsorption**

- **Location**: Starts in the **PCT**.
- **PCT Adaptations**: Cells have many **mitochondria** (to provide ATP for active transport) and **microvilli** (to increase surface area for absorption).
- **Reabsorbed Substances**:
    - **100% of Glucose and Amino Acids**: Reabsorbed by **active transport**.
    - **85% of Mineral Ions**: Reabsorbed by active transport.
    - **80% of Water**: Reabsorbed by **osmosis**.
- **Note on Diabetes**: In untreated diabetes, blood glucose exceeds the **renal threshold**, and active transport cannot "keep up," resulting in glucose being excreted in the urine.

**6. Osmoregulation**

- **Definition**: The control of water potential and solute concentration in the blood.
- **Control Center**: The **hypothalamus** detects changes in blood water potential and "instructs" the **pituitary gland**.
- **Effector**: **ADH (Antidiuretic Hormone)** controls the permeability of the collecting ducts.
- **Key Relationships**:
    - **Dehydration (Low Water Potential)**: Pituitary secretes **more ADH** → collecting ducts become more permeable → **more water is reabsorbed** into the blood → **Small volume of concentrated urine** is produced.
    - **Excess Water (High Water Potential)**: Pituitary secretes **less ADH** → collecting ducts become less permeable → **less water is reabsorbed** → **Large volume of dilute urine** is produced.

**7. Kidney Dialysis**

- **Purpose**: To clean the blood of patients with kidney failure by removing toxins (urea) and excess fluids.
- **Dialysis Machine Components**:
    - **Partially Permeable Tubing**: Long and narrow (high SA:V ratio) to increase diffusion of waste products.
    - **Dialysis Fluid**: Contains **essential salts and glucose** at the same concentration as healthy blood (prevents loss of these substances) but **contains no urea** (to maintain a steep concentration gradient for waste removal).
    - **Counter-current Flow**: Blood and dialysis fluid flow in **opposite directions** to maintain the concentration gradient for the entire length of the tubing.
- **Fistula**: A surgical connection between an artery and a vein in the forearm. The increased pressure makes the vein stronger and larger, allowing for faster blood flow and more efficient filtration.
