# 1. THE SPEAKER (Main Narrator)

### Point 1 — The speaker is a detached, critical outsider who moves through the suburb as a deliberate observer, offended by its artificial order.

> _"Cruising these residential Sunday / streets in dry August sunlight"_ _"what offends us is / the sanities"_

The **diction** of **"cruising"** suggests a slow, surveying passage — the speaker is passing through, not belonging. The **irony** is immediate and jarring: what should comfort (**"sanities"**) is what disturbs. The plural pronoun **"us"** establishes the speaker as the voice of a collective consciousness that rejects the suburb's values. The impression is of an alienated witness who sees through the neighborhood's performance of perfection.

### Point 2 — The speaker is a perceptive truth-teller who detects hidden sickness and violence beneath the suburb's clean surface.

> _"the smell of spilled oil a faint / sickness lingering in the garages"_ _"a plastic hose poised in a vicious / coil"_

The **olfactory imagery** of **"sickness lingering"** reveals the speaker's sensitivity to rot that others ignore, while the **visual imagery** of the hose in a **"vicious coil"** transforms a domestic object into a predatory threat. The **diction** of **"vicious"** and **"sickness"** casts the suburb as diseased and dangerous. The impression is of a forensic observer who autopsies the neighborhood and finds it rotten inside.

### Point 3 — The speaker is prophetic, envisioning the inevitable collapse of suburban order under the slow force of nature.

> _"the future cracks in the plaster"_ _"when the houses, capsized, will slide / obliquely into the clay seas, gradual as glaciers"_

The **foreshadowing** embedded in **"future cracks"** shows the speaker can read decay already written into the present. The **metaphor** of houses as **"capsized"** ships and the earth as **"clay seas"** frames nature as an ocean that will swallow human construction. The **simile** **"gradual as glaciers"** emphasizes unstoppable geological time. The impression is of a seer who looks past the suburb's temporary gleam to its destined ruin.

# 2. THE CITY PLANNERS

### Point 1 — They are individually megalomaniacal madmen: power-crazed conspirators whose professional rationality masks a cold, dangerous insanity.

> _"with the insane faces of political conspirators"_ _"in a bland madness of snows"_

The **metaphor** comparing each planner to a **"political conspirator"** individualizes him as a secretive, scheming figure who wields urban planning as a tool of covert control. The **oxymoron** **"bland madness"** captures a uniquely dull, bureaucratic lunacy — not foaming rage but a numb, pervasive insanity that makes him more sinister. The impression is of an individual who is not merely misguided but quietly, methodically dangerous.

### Point 2 — They are individually lost and disoriented — impostors fumbling in private chaos, lacking the expertise their title demands.

> _"each in his own private blizzard"_ _"guessing directions, they sketch"_

The **metaphor** of **"his own private blizzard"** presents each man as individually trapped in a personal storm of cold confusion and blindness, unable to see clearly yet presuming to design the world for others. The emphatic **diction** of **"guessing"** utterly demolishes his authority — he is not calculating but gambling. The impression is of a solitary fraud, lost in a self-created fog while pretending to be a rational architect.

### Point 3 — They are individually paranoid and fragmented — isolated solitaries concealed from one another in secrecy and fear.

> _"concealed from each other"_ _"scattered over unsurveyed / territories"_

The **diction** of **"concealed"** implies deliberate hiding — each man is too frightened or ashamed to be seen by his peers. The **visual imagery** of being **"scattered"** across **"unsurveyed territories"** is deeply ironic: these are professional surveyors who are themselves individually stranded in unmapped chaos. The impression is of a trembling, lonely figure who is professionally lost and existentially alone.

### Point 4 — They are individually futile creators of meaningless, vanishing boundaries.

> _"transitory lines rigid as wooden borders"_ _"on a wall in the white vanishing air"_

The **simile** **"rigid as wooden borders"** compares his plans to temporary, artificial markings drawn on a surface. The **juxtaposition** of **"transitory"** (temporary) with **"rigid"** (permanent) exposes the contradiction at the heart of his work. The **symbolism** of the **"white vanishing air"** suggests whatever he sketches dissolves into nothingness. The impression is of an impotent individual drawing boundaries that hold no real permanence.

# 3. THE HOUSES

### Point 1 — The houses are individually presented as authoritarian enforcers of conformity — pedantic, judgmental structures that demand human submission.

> _"the houses in pedantic rows"_ _"assert / levelness of surface like a rebuke"_

The **personification** of houses as **"pedantic"** — a word describing a fussy, rule-obsessed person — suggests the architecture itself polices behavior. The **personification** continues as the houses **"assert"** their flatness **"like a rebuke,"** actively shaming the imperfect "dent in our car door." The impression is not of welcoming homes but of stern schoolmasters or prison wardens.

### Point 2 — The houses are individually presented as fragile, doomed vessels masquerading as permanent — destined to be swallowed by nature.

> _"when the houses, capsized, will slide / obliquely into the clay seas"_ _"the future cracks in the plaster"_

The **metaphor** transforms houses into **"capsized"** ships sinking into **"clay seas,"** framing them as vessels that have lost their mooring. The **foreshadowing** in **"future cracks in the plaster"** shows that their destruction is already seeded in the present. The impression is of structures pretending to be eternal while quietly coming apart.

### Point 3 — The houses are individually presented as lifeless, surveillant shells with a frozen, deathlike gaze.

> _"the too-fixed stare of the wide windows"_ _"the roofs all display / the same slant of avoidance to the hot sky"_

The **personification** of windows having a **"too-fixed stare"** suggests rigor mortis or a mannequin's frozen expression — these buildings seem dead yet watchful. The **personification** of roofs showing **"avoidance"** suggests a collective turning away from truth and nature. The impression is of houses that are not alive but embalmed, watching their inhabitants with a cold, unblinking eye.

# 4. THE SUBURB / RESIDENTIAL PLACE

### Point 1 — The place is individually presented as a sterile, clinically pure landscape that judges and rejects human imperfection.

> _"the planted / sanitary trees"_ _"assert / levelness of surface like a rebuke / to the dent in our car door"_

The **diction** of **"sanitary"** suggests medical cleanliness — nature has been disinfected and sterilized. The **personification** of the landscape delivering a **"rebuke"** transforms the ground itself into a moral judge that attacks human fallibility. The impression is of a neighborhood that functions like a hospital ward or laboratory, hostile to anything organic or flawed.

### Point 2 — The place is individually presented as an acoustically dead zone that has violently suppressed all human passion and noise.

> _"No shouting here, or / shatter of glass"_ _"nothing more abrupt / than the rational whine of a power mower"_

The **auditory imagery** establishes absence — the silence of "no shouting," "no shatter." The **juxtaposition** of human chaos (shouting, breaking) with the mechanical **"rational whine"** reveals that human vitality has been replaced by machine noise. The impression is of a vacuum where life should be — a place that has murdered its own acoustics.

### Point 3 — The place is individually presented as a site of hidden rot — pristine on the surface but diseased underneath.

> _"the smell of spilled oil a faint / sickness lingering in the garages"_ _"a splash of paint on brick surprising as a bruise"_

The **olfactory imagery** of **"sickness lingering"** transforms the garage from a functional space into a site of hidden disease. The **simile** **"surprising as a bruise"** compares a random paint mark to bodily injury, revealing that the suburb's "perfect" skin is as fragile as human flesh. The impression is of a place with a dual identity: clean on the outside, wounded and sick within.

### Point 4 — The place is individually presented as a doomed, temporary construct that nature will inevitably reclaim.

> _"the future cracks in the plaster"_ _"when the houses, capsized, will slide / obliquely into the clay seas, gradual as glaciers"_

The **visual imagery** of **"cracks in the plaster"** uses **foreshadowing** — entropy is already at work. The **metaphor** of houses sliding into **"clay seas"** and the **simile** **"gradual as glaciers"** emphasize that nature's reclamation is slow but absolute. The impression is of a place whose destruction is written into its very foundation.

# 5. THE POWER MOWER

### Point 1 — The power mower is individually presented as an oppressive instrument of violent control that beats nature into geometric submission.

> _"the rational whine of a power mower"_ _"cutting a straight swath in the discouraged grass"_

The **oxymoron** of **"rational whine"** pairs logic with an unpleasant, complaining sound, suggesting the suburb's order is actually aggressive. The **personification** of grass as **"discouraged"** gives nature human despair — it has been beaten down so repeatedly that it has lost hope. The impression is of a weaponized tool that enforces conformity through repeated ecological violence.

### Point 2 — The power mower is individually presented as the dominant sound that has murdered all human expression and replaced it with mechanical noise.

> _"No shouting here, or / shatter of glass"_ _"nothing more abrupt / than the rational whine of a power mower"_

The **auditory imagery** establishes what is missing — human shouting, the breaking of glass, the sounds of passion and accident. The **juxtaposition** of this silence against the mower's **"rational whine"** reveals that the machine has become the suburb's only voice. The impression is of a device that has not just cut grass but silenced life itself.

### Point 3 — The power mower is individually presented as a symbol of mechanized madness — logic weaponized against organic life.

> _"the rational whine of a power mower"_ _"cutting a straight swath in the discouraged grass"_

The **diction** of **"rational"** ironically describes a machine that enforces conformity through brute force. The **symbolism** of the **"straight swath"** represents the suburban compulsion to impose geometry on chaos. The impression is of an object that embodies the poem's central paradox: the suburb's logic is itself a form of insanity, and its primary instrument is this whining, relentless blade.
