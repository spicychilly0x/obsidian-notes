# 1. THE SPEAKER (Older Self)

### Point 1 — She is guilt-ravaged and penitent, presenting herself as a vandal who has desecrated their shared physical inheritance.

> _"You must forgive me"_ _"I have spoiled this body we once shared"_

The opening **apostrophe** immediately establishes a confessional tone — the speaker begs pardon from her younger self as if addressing a wounded victim. The **diction** of **"spoiled"** carries connotations of rotting food or ruined goods, transforming the body from a living vessel into something corrupted and wasted. The **metaphor** of the body as a shared possession ("we once shared") frames the speaker as a traitor who has betrayed a joint trust. The impression is of a woman consumed by remorse for what time and experience have done to her physical form.

### Point 2 — She is physically decrepit and fearful, a cautious shadow of her former fearlessness.

> _"Look at the scars, and watch the way I move"_ _"careful of a bad back or a bruised foot"_

The **imperative verbs** ("Look," "watch") force the younger self — and the reader — to witness her physical deterioration. The **visual imagery** of **"scars"** suggests permanent damage, while the **alliteration** of **"bad back"** and **"bruised foot"** creates a rhythmic, painful litany of ailments. The **diction** of **"careful"** is devastating in its smallness — this is a woman who now calculates every step. The impression is of a body that has become an enemy, a traitor to its own former vitality.

### Point 3 — She is fiercely protective yet quietly resigned, choosing to bear the weight of adult fear alone.

> _"I shan't cloud your morning"_ _"I have fears enough for us both"_

The **metaphor** of **"cloud"** transforms fear into weather — the speaker refuses to darken the child's bright world with her own storms. The **hyperbole** **"fears enough for us both"** suggests she carries a crushing surplus of anxiety, enough to cover two lifetimes. The archaic **diction** of **"shan't"** lends a formal, almost maternal protectiveness to the line. The impression is of a woman who acts as a human shield, absorbing all the darkness so her younger self can remain in light.

### Point 4 — She is brutally honest about alienation, acknowledging that she and her younger self are now strangers.

> _"the truth is we have nothing in common"_ _"I won't keep you then"_

The **blunt diction** of **"nothing in common"** delivers the emotional climax with surgical cruelty — the speaker refuses to sentimentalize the bond. The **metaphor** of **"keeping"** suggests the younger self is a possession or a guest who has overstayed her welcome. The impression is of a woman who has made her peace with loss: she recognizes that the child she was is irretrievable, and she releases her with a kind of mournful clarity.

# 2. THE NINE-YEAR-OLD SELF

### Point 1 — She is feral and kinetic, defined by an insatiable hunger for motion and risk.

> _"You would rather run than walk, rather climb than run / rather leap from a height than anything"_ _"balancing on your hands or on the tightrope"_

The **anaphora** of **"rather... rather... rather"** builds a crescendo of escalating recklessness, as if the child cannot exist without accelerating. The **tricolon** creates a rhythmic, breathless energy that mirrors the child's physical urgency. The **visual imagery** of **"balancing on your hands"** and the **metaphor** of the **"tightrope"** (even if imaginary) present her as a circus performer, a creature of impossible equilibrium and danger. The impression is of a wild, untamed body that treats the world as an obstacle course.

### Point 2 — She is impulsive and death-defying, acting without calculation or caution.

> _"three minutes after waking / we'd jump straight out of the ground floor window / into the summer morning"_ _"eager to be gone"_

The **hyperbole** of **"three minutes after waking"** compresses time to emphasize the child's absolute impatience with stillness. The **kinaesthetic imagery** of **"jump straight out"** captures a body that moves before the mind has fully engaged. The **diction** of **"eager to be gone"** suggests a creature who cannot bear containment, who must always be escaping into the next moment. The impression is of a child for whom danger is not a concept — it is simply irrelevant.

### Point 3 — She is innocent and unburdened, existing in a world before adult fear took root.

> _"Don't look so surprised, / perplexed"_ _"I shan't cloud your morning"_

The **visual imagery** of the child's face — **"surprised, / perplexed"** — captures an expression of pure, open innocence, untroubled by the knowledge the speaker carries. The second quote (from the speaker's perspective) reinforces this innocence by contrast: the adult explicitly refuses to contaminate the child's **"morning"** (a **metaphor** for the dawn of her life) with adult darkness. The impression is of a creature who has not yet learned to be afraid.

### Point 4 — She is viscerally transgressive, finding rapture in bodily experiences that adults find repulsive.

> _"an ecstasy of concentration"_ _"slowly peeling a ripe scab from your knee / to taste it on your tongue"_

The **oxymoron** of **"ecstasy"** paired with a disgusting act shatters adult sensibilities — for the child, there is no boundary between beauty and revulsion. The **gustatory imagery** of **"taste it on your tongue"** and the **synaesthesia** (touch transformed into taste) reveal a child who explores her world through uninhibited physical consumption. The **diction** of **"ripe"** (usually reserved for fruit) makes the scab sound almost delicious. The impression is of a creature who is completely, unashamedly embodied — she does not flinch from her own flesh.

# 3. THE BODY

### Point 1 — It is a shared vessel that has been corrupted and violated by time.

> _"this body we once shared"_ _"I have spoiled this body"_

The **metaphor** of the body as shared property ("we once shared") frames it as a communal inheritance that the adult speaker has squandered. The **diction** of **"spoiled"** transforms the body from a living organism into something rotted — meat left too long in the sun. The impression is of a body that was once a site of joy and is now a site of regret.

### Point 2 — It is a scarred, treacherous machine that betrays its former fearlessness.

> _"Look at the scars"_ _"careful of a bad back or a bruised foot"_

The **visual imagery** of **"scars"** suggests permanent, visible damage — the body now bears a map of every injury. The **alliteration** of **"bad back"** and **"bruised foot"** creates a rhythmic, painful drumbeat of physical failure. The impression is of a body that has become an adversary, something to be managed rather than celebrated.

### Point 3 — It is a contested battlefield between the fearless child and the cautious adult.

> _"we'd jump straight out of the ground floor window"_ _"watch the way I move, / careful"_

The **juxtaposition** between the child's reckless **"jump"** and the adult's measured **"careful"** movement turns the body into a site of civil war. The **pronoun shift** from **"we"** (the shared, leaping child) to **"I"** (the solitary, careful adult) marks the moment the body changed allegiance. The impression is of a body that has been colonized by fear — what was once an instrument of freedom is now a fragile cage.

# 4. THE SCAB

### Point 1 — It is grotesque yet sacramental, an object of adult revulsion and childish devotion.

> _"a ripe scab"_ _"to taste it on your tongue"_

The **diction** of **"ripe"** (usually describing fruit or readiness) applied to a scab creates a shocking **oxymoron** — something dead and dried is described as full of life. The **gustatory imagery** of **"taste it on your tongue"** transforms a wound into a meal, collapsing the boundary between injury and nourishment. The impression is of an object that defies adult categories of disgust.

### Point 2 — It is an object of rapturous, almost religious obsession.

> _"an ecstasy of concentration"_ _"slowly peeling"_

The **diction** of **"ecstasy"** carries religious and sexual connotations — it is a word for transcendent rapture, not for picking at a knee. Paired with **"concentration,"** it suggests the child is in a trance, utterly absorbed in the physical moment. The **kinetic imagery** of **"slowly peeling"** emphasizes deliberation and ritual. The impression is of a child who treats her own body as a sacred text to be read and consumed.

### Point 3 — It is a symbol of primal self-knowledge and the body's resilience.

> _"ripe scab from your knee"_ _"taste it on your tongue"_

The **symbolism** of the **scab** as healing and protection (the body's own bandage) is complicated by the child's urge to destroy it — she cannot leave well enough alone. The **synaesthesia** (touch to taste) suggests a child who knows her body through direct, unfiltered sensory experience. The impression is of an object that represents everything the adult has lost: the ability to be present in one's own skin without disgust.

# 5. THE CHILDHOOD LANDSCAPE / SUMMER MORNING

### Point 1 — It is boundlessly idyllic, a world of infinite creative potential.

> _"into the summer morning"_ _"as the white paper to write it on"_

The **visual imagery** of **"summer morning"** evokes warmth, light, and endless possibility. The **simile** comparing the dream to **"white paper"** transforms the landscape into a blank page — nothing has been written yet, and therefore nothing has been ruined. The **symbolism** of white paper suggests innocence, potential, and the absence of error. The impression is of a world that is still innocent, still waiting to be invented.

### Point 2 — It is a site of chaotic, grandiose creation where ambition outpaces execution.

> _"that summer of ambition"_ _"created an ice-lolly factory, a wasp trap / and a den by the cesspit"_

The **diction** of **"ambition"** is an adult word applied to childish projects, creating a charming **bathos** — the gap between the grand term and the humble reality. The **visual imagery** of the **"ice-lolly factory"** and **"wasp trap"** captures a mind that builds without blueprint, inventing purely for the joy of making. The **juxtaposition** of the sweet factory with the disgusting **"cesspit"** reveals a landscape where beauty and filth coexist naturally. The impression is of a world where creativity is wild, messy, and unconstrained.

### Point 3 — It is entombed by modernity, a lost Eden buried under concrete.

> _"long buried in housing"_ _"I shan't cloud your morning"_

The **metaphor** of the tree being **"buried in housing"** transforms development into an act of entombment — nature has been killed and covered over by human construction. The second quote reinforces this loss by contrast: the adult refuses to tell the child that her world will be destroyed, preserving the illusion. The **symbolism** of burial suggests not just loss but death — the landscape is a corpse under concrete. The impression is of a paradise that no longer exists, accessible only in memory.

# 6. THE DREAM / UNFULFILLED AMBITION

### Point 1 — It is pristinely preserved in the child's mind, as fresh and untouched as the day it was conceived.

> _"That dream we had, no doubt it's as fresh in your mind"_ _"as the white paper to write it on"_

The **simile** comparing the dream to **"white paper"** suggests it remains unmarked, unspoiled, and full of potential. The **diction** of **"fresh"** implies the dream has not decayed with time — in the child's mind, it is still alive. The impression is of an ambition that has been cryogenically frozen in childhood, preserved while the adult has moved on.

### Point 2 — It is derailed and abandoned, sacrificed on the altar of trivial distractions.

> _"We made a start, but something else came up"_ _"a baby vole, or a bag of sherbet lemons"_

The **bathos** is devastating — a grand dream is abandoned for a **"baby vole"** or a bag of sweets. The **enjambment** after **"came up"** creates a pause that mirrors the interruption itself: the dream is literally cut off by distraction. The **symbolism** of **"sherbet lemons"** represents the sweetness and triviality of childhood that derails ambition. The impression is of a dream that was never destroyed by tragedy but simply forgotten in favor of smaller, sweeter things.

### Point 3 — It is grandiose in conception yet doomed by the chaotic nature of childhood.

> _"that summer of ambition"_ _"created an ice-lolly factory, a wasp trap / and a den by the cesspit"_

The **diction** of **"ambition"** elevates childish play to the level of adult enterprise, creating a touching irony. The **visual imagery** of the makeshift inventions reveals a mind that dreams big but builds erratically. The **juxtaposition** of creative ambition with the disgusting **"cesspit"** suggests that even the child's highest aspirations are rooted in dirt and impermanence. The impression is of a dream that was magnificent in its innocence and impossible in its execution.
