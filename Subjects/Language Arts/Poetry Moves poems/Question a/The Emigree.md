# 1. THE SPEAKER (THE EMIGRÉE)

### Point 1 — She is permanently branded by an idealized childhood memory that resists every assault of adult knowledge and political reality.

> _"I am branded by an impression of sunlight"_ _"The worst news I receive of it cannot break / my original view, the bright, filled paperweight"_

The **metaphor** of being **"branded"** transforms memory into a permanent, physical scar — she has been seared by an impression that cannot be erased. The **visual imagery** of the **"bright, filled paperweight"** presents her memory as a small, perfect, self-contained world that outside news cannot shatter. The **diction** of **"worst news"** and **"cannot break"** establishes her as defiantly, almost stubbornly loyal to a vision that refuses to die. The impression is of a woman whose inner landscape is armored against truth.

### Point 2 — She is linguistically orphaned, carrying a banned, embodied language that is both her treasure and her burden.

> _"That child's vocabulary I carried here / like a hollow doll, opens and spills a grammar"_ _"It may by now be a lie, banned by the state / but I can't get it off my tongue. It tastes of sunlight"_

The **simile** comparing her vocabulary to a **"hollow doll"** suggests something precious but empty — a container from childhood that unexpectedly releases meaning. The **diction** of **"banned by the state"** politicizes her very speech, making her a criminal of the tongue. The **gustatory imagery** of **"tastes of sunlight"** transforms language into something sensory and alive, linking it to her idealized memory. The impression is of a woman who carries a dead language in her mouth like a secret sacrament.

### Point 3 — She is stateless and physically exiled, stripped of legal identity and the right to return.

> _"I have no passport, there's no way back at all"_ _"They accuse me of absence"_

The **diction** of **"no passport"** reduces her to a non-person in the eyes of the state — she has no document, no permission, no legal existence. The **hyperbole** of **"no way back at all"** emphasizes the absolute finality of her exile. The second quote reveals the cruelty of her situation: the **diction** of **"accuse"** suggests she is guilty of a crime simply for having left. The impression is of a woman who is homeless in the most profound sense — not just without a house, but without a country that will claim her.

### Point 4 — She is defiantly imaginative, conjuring an alternative reality where her city comes to her across impossible distance.

> _"my city comes to me in its own white plane"_ _"My city hides behind me"_

The **metaphor** of the city arriving **"in its own white plane"** transforms memory into an aircraft, a vehicle that defies borders and passports. The **personification** of the city as something that can **"hide behind me"** suggests a symbiotic, protective relationship — she shields her idealized memory from the political reality. The impression is of a woman who has become a magician of memory, summoning a homeland that no longer exists.

# 2. THE CITY (THE IDEALIZED MEMORY)

### Point 1 — It is frozen in perpetual, impossible summer, immune to the grey reality of November.

> _"my memory of it is sunlight-clear"_ _"for it seems I never saw it in that November / which, I am told, comes to the mildest city"_

The **diction** of **"sunlight-clear"** transforms memory into something transparent, pure, and luminous — a photograph overexposed with joy. The **juxtaposition** between her eternal summer and **"that November"** (which she never saw) reveals that her city exists outside real time. The **irony** is that even the **"mildest city"** has winter, but her child-self never witnessed it. The impression is of a place that has been cryogenically frozen in the amber of childhood perception.

### Point 2 — It is small, precious, and fragile — a jewel or toy that she holds in the palm of her mind.

> _"the bright, filled paperweight"_ _"It lies down in front of me, docile as paper"_

The **metaphor** of the **"paperweight"** suggests something miniature, decorative, and preserved under glass — beautiful but inert. The **simile** **"docile as paper"** presents the city as pliant, obedient, and thin — it will not resist her imagination. The **personification** of a city that **"lies down"** transforms architecture into a domestic pet or a sleeping child. The impression is of a place that has been miniaturized and tamed, made safe by distance and memory.

### Point 3 — It is personified as a beloved companion — almost a lover or a child — with whom she shares an intimate, private bond.

> _"I comb its hair and love its shining eyes"_ _"My city takes me dancing"_

The **personification** is taken to an extreme: the city has **"hair"** and **"shining eyes"** that she tends with maternal or romantic tenderness. The **diction** of **"love"** and the **kinaesthetic imagery** of **"dancing"** suggest a relationship of joy and mutuality. The impression is of a place that has become a living being — not a location but a companion who returns her affection.

# 3. THE COUNTRY (THE ACTUAL/POLITICAL REALITY)

### Point 1 — It is diseased, tyrannical, and consumed by war — a body politic rotting from within.

> _"It may be at war, it may be sick with tyrants"_ _"They mutter death"_

The **diction** of **"sick with tyrants"** uses disease imagery to suggest the country is infected by its rulers — tyranny is not a policy but a pathology. The **auditory imagery** of **"mutter death"** creates a whispered, conspiratorial atmosphere of violence. The impression is of a nation that has become a patient in a terminal ward, its government a malignant tumor.

### Point 2 — It is grey, walled, and militarized — the antithesis of the speaker's luminous memory.

> _"as time rolls its tanks"_ _"through the city / of walls"_

The **metaphor** of time **"rolling its tanks"** transforms the passage of years into a military invasion — even time has become weaponized. The **symbolism** of the **"city / of walls"** suggests a place that has turned inward, defensive, and paranoid. The impression is of a homeland that has become a fortress and a prison, its open streets replaced by barriers.

### Point 3 — It is exclusionary and accusatory, rejecting the speaker as "dark" and foreign.

> _"They accuse me of being dark in their free city"_ _"They accuse me of absence"_

The **diction** of **"dark"** suggests the speaker is racially or politically othered — she does not fit the ideology of the "free city." The **irony** of **"their free city"** is crushing: a place that claims liberty while accusing exiles of darkness is not free at all. The second quote reveals that her very existence as an emigrée is treated as a crime. The impression is of a country that has rewritten its history to exclude those who left.

# 4. THE LANGUAGE / CHILD'S VOCABULARY

### Point 1 — It is a hidden, embodied artifact that the speaker carries like a smuggled treasure.

> _"That child's vocabulary I carried here / like a hollow doll"_ _"Soon I shall have every coloured molecule of it"_

The **simile** comparing language to a **"hollow doll"** suggests something from childhood that appears empty but contains unexpected depth — it **"opens and spills a grammar."** The **hyperbole** of **"every coloured molecule"** transforms words into physical, chromatic particles that she is slowly reassembling. The impression is of a language that has been smuggled across borders in pieces, a contraband of identity.

### Point 2 — It is banned and criminalized, making the speaker's tongue an act of resistance.

> _"banned by the state"_ _"I can't get it off my tongue"_

The **diction** of **"banned by the state"** reveals that her native speech has been outlawed — to speak it is now an act of political defiance. The **metaphor** of being unable to **"get it off my tongue"** suggests the language has adhered to her body, becoming part of her physical self. The impression is of a tongue that has become a battlefield, where every word is a small revolution.

### Point 3 — It is sensory and synesthetic, tasting of the sunlight that defines her memory.

> _"It tastes of sunlight"_ _"spills a grammar"_

The **gustatory imagery** of **"tastes of sunlight"** collapses language, memory, and sensation into a single experience — her words are not just sounds but flavors of a lost world. The **diction** of **"spills"** suggests an overflow, an abundance that cannot be contained. The impression is of a language that is not dead but delicious, nourishing her in exile.

# 5. THE "THEY" (CURRENT INHABITANTS / ACCUSERS)

### Point 1 — They are hostile, circling predators who treat the speaker as a target.

> _"They accuse me of absence, they circle me"_ _"They mutter death"_

The **diction** of **"circle"** suggests vultures or wolves — a predatory, closing movement. The **auditory imagery** of **"mutter death"** transforms them into conspirators who whisper threats. The repetition of **"They accuse"** creates an **anaphora** that pounds like a drumbeat of condemnation. The impression is of a mob that has designated the speaker as its scapegoat.

### Point 2 — They are self-righteous hypocrites who claim freedom while practicing exclusion.

> _"They accuse me of being dark in their free city"_ _"the frontiers rise between us"_

The **irony** of **"their free city"** is devastating — freedom is their possession, not a universal right, and the speaker is too **"dark"** to share it. The **symbolism** of **"frontiers"** rising between them reveals that they are the ones building walls, not the exiled speaker. The impression is of citizens who have confused nationalism with virtue, patriotism with purity.

### Point 3 — They are paranoid and suspicious, seeing the speaker's very shadow as evidence.

> _"my shadow falls as evidence of sunlight"_ _"They accuse me of absence"_

The **metaphor** of her **"shadow"** as **"evidence"** suggests they have turned her existence into a trial, where even her silhouette is proof of something criminal. The **irony** is that her shadow — a thing of darkness — is read as **"evidence of sunlight,"** the very thing they resent her for carrying. The impression is of a people so consumed by ideology that they have lost the ability to distinguish memory from threat.

# 6. THE FRONTIERS / BORDERS

### Point 1 — They are aggressive, militarized barriers that multiply and advance over time.

> _"as time rolls its tanks"_ _"the frontiers rise between us"_

The **metaphor** of time **"rolling its tanks"** transforms temporal distance into a military invasion — the longer she is away, the more armed the border becomes. The **diction** of **"rise"** suggests frontiers that grow like walls or waves, becoming higher and more insurmountable. The impression is of borders that are not static lines but active, hostile forces.

### Point 2 — They are fluid yet impassable, like natural walls of water that close over the speaker.

> _"close like waves"_ _"I have no passport, there's no way back at all"_

The **simile** **"close like waves"** transforms political borders into a natural, oceanic force — beautiful but deadly. Waves close over a drowning person; the frontier closes over her homeland. The second quote reinforces the absolute nature of her exile. The impression is of a border that is both liquid and lethal, erasing her path home.

### Point 3 — They create a prison of walls that entraps the current inhabitants while permanently excluding the speaker.

> _"through the city / of walls"_ _"there's no way back at all"_

The **symbolism** of the **"city / of walls"** suggests a place that has turned inward, becoming a fortress that imprisons its own citizens. The **hyperbole** of **"no way back at all"** emphasizes the finality of her separation. The impression is of a border that functions as a one-way mirror — those inside are walled in, and she is forever walled out.
