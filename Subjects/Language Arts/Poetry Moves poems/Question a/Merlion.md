# 1. THE SPEAKER ("I")

### Point 1 — He is a culturally rooted intellectual sniper who dissects Westernized pretension with lethal, observational precision.

> _"Eyeing the blond highlights in your black hair / And your blue lenses the shadow of a foreign sky"_ _"in that acquired accent of yours"_

The **visual imagery** of **"blond highlights"** and **"blue lenses"** transforms the tourist's body into a map of foreign influence — every aesthetic choice advertises a rejected heritage. The **diction** of **"acquired"** is surgically contemptuous; it suggests not natural growth but deliberate purchase, a performance of identity bought rather than inherited. The **symbolism** of the **"shadow of a foreign sky"** implies that even the tourist's vision is colonized. The impression is of a speaker who sees through every layer of costume to the insecurity beneath.

### Point 2 — He is an allegorical decoder who reads the Merlion not as a statue but as a living text of postcolonial shame and self-denial.

> _"What a riddle, this lesser brother of the Sphinx"_ _"it tries to purge itself of its aquatic ancestry, / in this ceaseless torrent of denial"_

The **allusion** to the **Sphinx** elevates the Merlion from tourist kitsch to mythic tragedy, framing it as a creature with a philosophical problem rather than a marketing logo. The **metaphor** of a **"ceaseless torrent of denial"** transforms the Merlion's physical water-spouting into a psychological symptom — it is not performing but vomiting up its own shame. The impression is of a speaker who treats national symbols as serious literature, demanding they be read with critical depth.

### Point 3 — He is a master of ironic silence who weaponizes pauses to expose the tourist's intellectual emptiness.

> _"A pause."_ _"Another pause."_

The **diction** of the single-word stage directions **"A pause"** and **"Another pause"** functions as a **caesura** that fractures the conversational flow. These pauses are not neutral; they are the speaker's trapdoors, moments where the tourist's shallow commentary falls into silence and is judged. The **repetition** emphasizes a pattern — the tourist cannot sustain thought without stumbling. The impression is of a speaker who dominates the dialogue not by talking more, but by talking better, letting the tourist's silence indict itself.

# 2. "YOU" / THE TOURIST

### Point 1 — They are a physically Westernized mimic who has dyed, lensed, and accented themselves into a foreign ideal while judging their own cultural symbol.

> _"the blond highlights in your black hair / And your blue lenses the shadow of a foreign sky"_ _"in that acquired accent of yours"_

The **juxtaposition** of **"black hair"** with **"blond highlights"** creates a visual contradiction — the body fights the dye, just as the tourist's heritage fights their adopted aesthetics. The **symbolism** of **"blue lenses"** suggests not just colored contacts but a deliberate choice to see the world through a foreign filter. The **diction** of **"acquired"** frames their voice as stolen property. The impression is of a person who has performed self-erasure and now looks with disgust at a statue that has not done the same.

### Point 2 — They are an aesthetically reactionary purist, repulsed by hybridity and demanding the Merlion conform to a "real" lion's body.

> _"I wish it had paws"_ _"It's quite grotesque the way it is, / you know, limbless"_

The **repetition** of **"paws"** (bookending the poem) reveals an obsession with anatomical correctness that masks a deeper intolerance for mixed identity. The **diction** of **"grotesque"** is violent in its dismissal — the national symbol is not odd or unusual but monstrous. The **diction** of **"limbless"** emphasizes lack and deformity, as if the Merlion's fish tail is a missing limb rather than a legitimate feature. The impression is of a person who cannot tolerate impurity, who wants their symbols to be singular and whole.

### Point 3 — They are intellectually vacuous, stumbling through inarticulate speech and shallow, sensational comparisons.

> _"I mean, how does it move? Like a / torpedo? Or does it shoulder itself / against the currents... like some post-Chernobyl nightmare?"_ _"I mean, you know, I mean..."_

The **rhetorical questions** pile up without answer, revealing a mind that asks only to hear itself speak. The **simile** **"like some post-Chernobyl nightmare"** is **hyperbole** of the most hysterical kind — the Merlion is not odd but radioactive, not hybrid but mutated. The **repetition** of **"I mean"** and the **ellipsis** expose verbal bankruptcy; the tourist reaches for words and finds only filler. The impression is of a person whose critical vocabulary consists entirely of foreign clichés and pop-culture references.

### Point 4 — They are a consumer-tourist who treats the national symbol as a grotesque post-apocalyptic curiosity rather than a site of meaning.

> _"like some post-Chernobyl nightmare?"_ _"And why does it keep spewing that way?"_

The **allusion** to **Chernobyl** frames the Merlion as a disaster artifact, something to be gawked at rather than understood. The **diction** of **"spewing"** reduces the Merlion's iconic water feature to something vulgar and involuntary, like vomiting. The impression is of a person who approaches their own culture with the shallow gaze of a foreign tourist, demanding entertainment and disgusted by complexity.

# 3. THE MERLION

### Point 1 — It is a grotesque biological impossibility — limbless, writhing, and visually offensive to any purist gaze.

> _"It's quite grotesque the way it is, / you know, limbless"_ _"imagine it writhing in the water, / like some post-Chernobyl nightmare?"_

The **diction** of **"grotesque"** and **"limbless"** frames the Merlion as a body that has gone wrong, a creature missing the limbs that would make it a proper lion. The **simile** **"like some post-Chernobyl nightmare"** transforms it into a radiation victim, a mutant that should not exist. The **kinaesthetic imagery** of **"writhing"** suggests pain and unnatural movement. The impression is of a body that violates biological law — neither fully fish nor fully lion, and therefore monstrous.

### Point 2 — It is a marooned exile, forcibly terrestrial and deeply ashamed of its own aquatic bloodline.

> _"how it is poised so terrestrially, / marooned on this rough shore"_ _"Perhaps it is even ashamed / (But what a bold front!) / to have been a creature of the sea"_

The **metaphor** of **"marooned"** transforms the Merlion from a statue on a pedestal into a castaway, stranded on land by shipwreck or betrayal. The **juxtaposition** between **"ashamed"** and **"bold front"** creates an **oxymoron** — it projects confidence while inwardly cringing. The **personification** of shame gives the statue a psychology; it is not inert stone but a conscious being embarrassed by its own origin story. The impression is of a refugee from its own nature.

### Point 3 — It is a self-doubting lesser sibling to the Sphinx — a riddle that cannot solve itself and remains perpetually surprised by its own existence.

> _"What a riddle, this lesser brother of the Sphinx."_ _"how its sister's lips are sealed / with self-knowledge and how its own jaws / clamp open in self-doubt, still / surprised after all these years."_

The **allusion** to the **Sphinx** places the Merlion in the company of ancient myth, but the **diction** of **"lesser brother"** immediately demotes it. The **juxtaposition** between the sister's **"lips... sealed / with self-knowledge"** and the Merlion's **"jaws / clamp open in self-doubt"** creates a cruel contrast: where the Sphinx is mysterious and complete, the Merlion is gaping and confused. The **paradox** of being **"still / surprised after all these years"** suggests a permanent state of arrested development. The impression is of a creature that woke up in the wrong myth and has never recovered.

### Point 4 — It is compulsively self-erasing, spewing water to destroy the mirror of its own divided identity.

> _"It spews continually if only to ruffle / its own reflection in the water"_ _"such reminders / will only scare a creature so eager to reinvent itself."_

The **metaphor** transforms the Merlion's water feature from a cute tourist photo-op into a compulsive act of self-sabotage — it spits at its own reflection because it cannot bear to see itself. The **diction** of **"ruffle"** suggests a violent disturbance of surface calm. The **personification** of the creature as **"eager to reinvent itself"** frames its entire existence as a rejection of authenticity. The impression is of a being at war with its own image, trapped in an endless loop of self-avoidance.

### Point 5 — It is trapped in a ceaseless torrent of denial, draining its own body of historical and ancestral memory.

> _"in this ceaseless torrent of denial, draining / the body of rivers of histories, lymphatic memories."_ _"it tries to purge itself of its aquatic ancestry"_

The **metaphor** of a **"ceaseless torrent of denial"** transforms the Merlion's water into a flood of historical amnesia. The **diction** of **"purge"** suggests vomiting or exorcism — a violent expulsion of what is naturally part of it. The **metaphor** of **"lymphatic memories"** is extraordinary: memories are not mental but bodily, flowing through vessels like immune fluid. The impression is of a creature that is literally dehydrating itself of history, trying to become a blank slate.

# 4. THE SEA / AQUATIC ANCESTRY

### Point 1 — It is a haunted, primordial origin that stains the Merlion with iridescent, ineradicable traces of its true nature.

> _"the seaweed-haunted / depths"_ _"what brand new sun can dry / the iridescent slime from the scales"_

The **diction** of **"haunted"** personifies the sea as a ghost-filled graveyard, not a place of origin but of lingering trauma. The **rhetorical question** **"what brand new sun can dry"** insists that no amount of modernity or reinvention can erase the sea's mark. The **visual imagery** of **"iridescent slime"** is beautiful and repulsive at once — the sea leaves a shimmering, organic stain that refuses to be cleaned. The impression is of an ancestry that is not proud but haunting, and permanently visible.

### Point 2 — It is an ancestral bloodstream of "lymphatic memories" that flows through the Merlion's veins and resists surgical removal.

> _"draining / the body of rivers of histories, lymphatic memories."_ _"what fresh rain wash the sting of salt / from those chalk-blind eyes?"_

The **metaphor** of **"lymphatic memories"** transforms history into a bodily fluid — part of the immune system, necessary for survival, impossible to drain without killing the host. The **rhetorical question** about **"fresh rain"** washing away **"the sting of salt"** is deeply ironic: rain is freshwater, but it cannot desalinate the creature's very eyes. The **symbolism** of **"salt"** suggests both the sea and tears — the Merlion weeps its own origin. The impression is of a heritage that is biological, not optional.

### Point 3 — It is an inescapable physical reality that has bleached and branded the Merlion's body beyond cosmetic repair.

> _"its furious mane bleached / the colour of a drowned sun"_ _"what brand new sun can dry / the iridescent slime from the scales"_

The **visual imagery** of the mane **"bleached / the colour of a drowned sun"** suggests the sea has already altered the Merlion's appearance — the sun itself has drowned in the water, leaving only a pale, washed-out imitation. The **symbolism** of **"scales"** (fish-like) against the **"mane"** (lion-like) creates a permanent visual contradiction. The impression is of a body that has been chemically changed by its environment and can never be restored.

# 5. THE SPHINX (The Sister)

### Point 1 — She is an ancient, self-knowing monument whose sealed lips signal a perfect, confident identity that the Merlion desperately lacks.

> _"this lesser brother of the Sphinx"_ _"its sister's lips are sealed / with self-knowledge"_

The **allusion** to the **Sphinx** places her in the pantheon of eternal, mythic creatures — she is not a modern marketing gimmick but an ancient enigma. The **metaphor** of lips **"sealed / with self-knowledge"** suggests that her silence is not ignorance but supreme understanding; she has nothing to prove and no need to speak. The **diction** of **"self-knowledge"** is the ultimate compliment — she knows exactly what she is. The impression is of a creature who has achieved the wholeness that the Merlion can only dream of.

### Point 2 — She is the Merlion's cruel mirror — complete where he is fractured, silent where he is gaping in self-doubt.

> _"how its sister's lips are sealed / with self-knowledge and how its own jaws / clamp open in self-doubt"_

The **juxtaposition** is devastating: the sister's **"sealed"** lips versus the Merlion's **"clamp open"** jaws create a visual polarity. Sealed lips suggest control, mystery, and completion; clamped-open jaws suggest panic, exposure, and a body that cannot stop betraying itself. The **diction** of **"self-doubt"** versus **"self-knowledge"** frames the two as opposites in a moral allegory. The impression is of a sibling who makes the Merlion's inadequacy unbearably visible.

### Point 3 — She represents a civilization that never needed reinvention, making the Merlion's struggle look pathetic by comparison.

> _"What sibling polarity"_ _"this lesser brother of the Sphinx"_

The **diction** of **"polarity"** insists that the two are not merely different but diametrically opposed forces. The **metaphor** of **"lesser brother"** establishes a hierarchy of mythic status — the Sphinx is original, the Merlion is derivative. The Sphinx never had to **"reinvent itself"** or **"purge"** its ancestry because it was always enough. The impression is of an older, wiser sibling who watches the Merlion's frantic self-improvement with silent, ancient pity.

# 6. THE LAND / SHORE

### Point 1 — It is a rough, alien prison where the Merlion is marooned and perpetually unsure of its rightful harbor.

> _"marooned on this rough shore, / as if unsure of its rightful / harbour"_ _"how it is poised so terrestrially"_

The **metaphor** of **"marooned"** transforms the land from a homeland into a site of abandonment — the Merlion did not choose to arrive here but was left behind. The **diction** of **"rough shore"** suggests hostility and discomfort, not welcome. The **diction** of **"terrestrially"** is clinical and strange — it defines the Merlion by what it is not (aquatic), emphasizing its displacement. The impression is of a shore that functions as an open-air prison.

### Point 2 — It is a stage of "unaccustomed limpidity" — a clarity and exposure that terrifies a creature born in murky depths.

> _"having taken to this unaccustomed limpidity"_ _"But take a second look at it"_

The **diction** of **"unaccustomed limpidity"** is extraordinary — **"limpidity"** means clarity and transparency, but for a sea creature, clarity is terrifying. In the depths, things are hidden; on land, everything is visible. The **imperative** **"take a second look"** suggests the land demands scrutiny, exposing the Merlion to a gaze it cannot escape. The impression is of a place where there is nowhere to hide, no water to swim in, no darkness to dissolve into.

### Point 3 — It is the site of the Merlion's terrestrial masquerade, where it poses in shameful boldness while its true self drowns in denial.

> _"Perhaps it is even ashamed / (But what a bold front!)"_ _"poised so terrestrially"_

The **oxymoron** of **"ashamed"** yet displaying a **"bold front"** captures the land's cruel demand: the Merlion must perform confidence while inwardly crumbling. The **diction** of **"poised"** suggests a deliberate, studied posture — on land, the Merlion is always acting. The land requires a performance of belonging that the sea never demanded. The impression is of a stage where the Merlion plays the role of "land creature" badly, tragically, and permanently.
