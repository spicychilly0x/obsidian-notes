# 1. THE BULLY

### Point 1 — The bully is individually presented as a pseudo-scientific sadist who frames deliberate cruelty as biological curiosity.

> _"as children we were naturally / curious, biological"_ _"how we'd trample you / just to watch that guarded / returning spring"_

The **diction** of **"naturally"** and **"curious"** is deeply ironic — the bully uses the language of innocence to justify acts of domination. The **juxtaposition** between the clinical tone of **"biological"** and the brutal verb **"trample"** exposes a mind that has learned to intellectualize violence. The **visual imagery** of **"returning spring"** reveals the motive: the bully is not merely hurting but experimenting, treating the victim's defensive recovery as repeatable entertainment. The impression is of a predator who wears the mask of a student.

### Point 2 — The bully is individually presented as a voyeur who derives pleasure from witnessing another being's cycle of destruction and resurrection.

> _"just to watch"_ _"for years it seemed magic"_

The **diction** of **"just to watch"** reduces the bully's motive to pure spectatorship — the violence is committed for the pleasure of observation. The **diction** of **"magic"** reveals the bully's self-deception: they have reframed systematic abuse as wonder, turning another creature's trauma into their own private theatre. The **symbolism** of the returning spring becomes a grotesque magic trick performed on demand. The impression is of a tormentor who is addicted to the spectacle of survival.

### Point 3 — The bully is individually presented as a physically escalating threat, moving from casual touch to calculated assault.

> _"each time we ran / our fingers, the edge / of careless shoe / against trembling"_ _"kicking teeth"_

The **kinaesthetic imagery** traces a chilling escalation: first **"fingers"** (exploratory), then the **"edge / of careless shoe"** (harder, more violent), finally **"kicking teeth"** (full assault). The **diction** of **"careless"** is devastating — the bully does not even register their own brutality. The **metaphor** of **"kicking teeth"** (read across the twin columns) transforms the plant into a human victim, revealing that this is a rehearsal for interpersonal violence. The impression is of a body that moves through the world like a weapon, leaving damage without intention.

### Point 4 — The bully is individually presented as wilfully blind, taking years to admit that what they called shyness was actually terror.

> _"until we learnt / – it wasn't shyness"_ _"every false death was nothing / but defence"_

The **enjambment** after **"learnt"** creates a dramatic pause before the devastating correction. The **irony** is crushing: the bully misread the victim's survival mechanism as charming bashfulness. The **oxymoron** of **"false death"** exposes the biological reality — the victim was not performing but fighting for its life. The impression is of an adult looking back at their childhood self with horror, realizing they were not curious but cruel.

# 2. THE VICTIM (The Mimosa Plant)

### Point 1 — The victim is individually presented as a trembling, sentient being that reacts to violence with visible, bodily fear.

> _"the way your / leaves shuttered"_ _"against trembling"_

The **personification** of the plant as **"your"** gives it a voice and identity — it is not an object but a being with a body that responds to threat. The **diction** of **"shuttered"** (rather than "closed") suggests a protective, defensive action, like windows being slammed against a storm. The **kinaesthetic imagery** of **"trembling"** transforms the plant into a creature that shakes with fear. The impression is of a living thing that is not passive but actively suffering.

### Point 2 — The victim is individually presented as a survivor trapped in a cycle of violation and forced resurrection.

> _"just to watch that guarded / returning spring"_ _"every false death was nothing / but defence"_

The **metaphor** of **"returning spring"** frames the victim's reopening as a seasonal resurrection, a stubborn refusal to stay broken. The **oxymoron** of **"false death"** reveals the victim's only strategy: play dead to survive. The **diction** of **"guarded"** suggests the victim has learned to protect itself, becoming wary after repeated attacks. The impression is of a creature that is battered but not broken, developing survival mechanisms in response to cruelty.

### Point 3 — The victim is individually presented as voiceless and misread, unable to explain its own suffering.

> _"it wasn't shyness"_ _"voice shut / gate"_

The **irony** is devastating: the bully interpreted the victim's terror as endearing shyness. The **metaphor** of **"voice shut / gate"** transforms the victim's silence into a locked barrier — it has no language to protest with, no mouth to scream. The **personification** of the plant as having a voice (even a silenced one) makes its muteness more tragic. The impression is of a victim whose suffering is invisible because it cannot be articulated.

### Point 4 — The victim is individually presented as a creature dreaming of rooted belonging and identity beyond abuse.

> _"dead earth dreaming of / what roots could mean"_ _"allowed reanimation"_

The **personification** of **"dead earth dreaming"** gives even the soil a consciousness that yearns for meaning. The **symbolism** of **"roots"** suggests identity, belonging, and groundedness — everything the victim is denied as it is repeatedly uprooted by careless shoes. The **diction** of **"reanimation"** carries Frankensteinian connotations — the victim must be brought back to life after each "false death." The impression is of a being that longs for stability but is trapped in a cycle of destruction and recovery.

# 3. THE BYSTANDERS

### Point 1 — They are individually presented as morally absent, having retreated into themselves to avoid responsibility.

> _"out of ourselves"_ _"eyes closed, the path which / pasts ignored"_

The **diction** of **"out of ourselves"** suggests dissociation — the bystanders have left their own bodies to avoid witnessing pain. The **visual imagery** of **"eyes closed"** is a deliberate choice to un-see, while **"pasts ignored"** reveals a willful amnesia. The impression is of people who have chosen oblivion over engagement, making them accessories to cruelty.

### Point 2 — They are individually presented as self-censored, biting back their own voices to maintain safety.

> _"of bitten word"_ _"ears a wall, voice shut / gate"_

The **metaphor** of **"bitten word"** suggests words that have been chewed back, swallowed before they could become intervention. The **metaphor** of **"voice shut / gate"** transforms silence into a locked barrier — they have chosen to barricade their own mouths. The impression is of bystanders who are not voiceless by nature but have gagged themselves out of fear.

### Point 3 — They are individually presented as complicit architects of a wall that protects abusers and imprisons victims.

> _"complicit"_ _"ears a wall"_

The **diction** of **"complicit"** is a single-word confession that lands like a verdict. The **metaphor** of **"ears a wall"** transforms the bystanders' bodies into fortresses that block out the sounds of suffering. The **symbolism** of the wall suggests not just deafness but active construction — they have built their own ignorance. The impression is of people who have engineered their own deafness, making them as responsible as the bullies themselves.

# 4. THE SCHOOLYARD

### Point 1 — It is individually presented as a site of natural curiosity that has been corrupted into a theatre of cruelty.

> _"as children we were naturally / curious, biological"_ _"doors only half-open for us –"_

The **diction** of **"naturally"** and **"biological"** frames the schoolyard as a space where human instincts are tested and shaped. The **metaphor** of **"doors only half-open"** suggests the schoolyard is a threshold — a place of incomplete knowledge where children are exposed to life's harsh lessons before they are ready. The impression is of a liminal space that should nurture but instead wounds.

### Point 2 — It is individually presented as a place where the weak are publicly sacrificed for the entertainment of the strong.

> _"how we'd trample you / just to watch"_ _"kicking teeth"_

The **visual imagery** of **"trample"** and **"kicking teeth"** transforms the schoolyard into a colosseum where the powerful test their strength on the powerless. The **diction** of **"just to watch"** reveals the motive is not even malice but boredom and spectacle. The impression is of a place where violence is casual, public, and normalized.

### Point 3 — It is individually presented as a classroom of cruelty where the curriculum is survival.

> _"Schoolyard Lesson"_ _"cruel, which is to say / fact: that life holds / no excuses for"_

The **diction** of the title **"Schoolyard Lesson"** is deeply ironic — the lesson learned is not academic but existential. The **diction** of **"cruel, which is to say / fact"** presents cruelty not as an aberration but as a fundamental truth of existence. The **symbolism** of the schoolyard as a place of education is inverted: here, children learn not algebra but brutality. The impression is of a place that functions as a brutal finishing school, teaching that the world has no mercy.

The **diction** of **"each time"** reveals repetition — this is not a single incident but a habit, a ritual of cruelty. The impression is of an event that starts as exploration and degenerates into systematic abuse.

The **irony** is grotesque: the victim's resilience is what makes the abuse repeatable. The impression is of an event where survival is punished by further violence.

> _"for years it seemed magic"_ _"every false death was nothing / but defence"_

The **diction** of **"magic"** frames the event as enchanting rather than brutal, revealing the children's self-deception. The **oxymoron** of **"false death"** exposes the biological reality: the victim was not performing but fighting for its life. The **symbolism** of the "lesson" is inverted — what seemed like wonder was actually warfare. The impression is of an event that was misread for years, its true meaning buried under childish wonder.
