**12 Literary Devices (2 Quotes Each)**

**1. Juxtaposition / Direct Contrast**

- **Quote 1:** `"It may be at war, it may be sick with tyrants,"`
    - **Context:** Stanza 1, where the speaker acknowledges news of her country's current ruin while holding onto her bright childhood view.
    - **Theme Link:** Juxtaposes political collapse (`"war"`, `"tyrants"`) against her pure internal memories, showing that personal love for home defies external state destruction.
- **Quote 2:** `"They accuse me of being dark in their free city."`
    - **Context:** Stanza 3, where the speaker describes facing xenophobic hostility from local citizens in her new host country.
    - **Theme Link:** Juxtaposes the host city's self-proclaimed status as `"free"` against their prejudiced persecution of her as `"dark"`, exposing societal hypocrisy.

---

**2. Irony (Situational & Sinister Irony)**

- **Quote 1:** `"They accuse me of being dark in their free city."`
    - **Context:** Stanza 3, as the speaker reflects on the racial hostility she experiences in exile.
    - **Theme Link (Situational Irony):** It is deeply ironic that a city claiming to be `"free"` restricts and alienates an immigrant based on racial prejudice (`"being dark"`).
- **Quote 2:** `"but I am branded by an impression of sunlight."`
    - **Context:** Concluding line of Stanza 1, reflecting on her permanent connection to her home city.
    - **Theme Link (Sinister Irony):** While `"sunlight"` connotes warmth, using `"branded"` ironically turns memory into a permanent, painful mark that designates her as a perpetual outsider.

---

**3. Metaphor**

- **Quote 1:** `"my original view, the bright, filled paperweight."`
    - **Context:** Stanza 1, describing her childhood memory of her city kept preserved inside her mind.
    - **Theme Link:** Metaphorically compares her memory to a solid `"paperweight"`, framing her past as a heavy, pristine, unshakeable anchor preserving her identity.
- **Quote 2:** `"and my shadow falls as evidence of sunlight."`
    - **Context:** The final line of the poem, as the speaker stands firm against the hostile citizens of her host city.
    - **Theme Link:** Uses her dark `"shadow"` as a metaphor to prove that even in dark exile, her enduring love for her homeland provides indisputable `"evidence of sunlight"`.

---

**4. Personification**

- **Quote 1:** `"I comb its hair and love its shining eyes."`
    - **Context:** Stanza 3, where the speaker visualises her remembered city visiting her in exile like a living creature.
    - **Theme Link:** Personifies the city as a beloved child or pet, illustrating her deep nurture, tenderness, and refusal to abandon her heritage.
- **Quote 2:** `"It may be at war, it may be sick with tyrants,"`
    - **Context:** Stanza 1, acknowledging the political ruin of her native country.
    - **Theme Link:** Personifies the nation as being `"sick"`, framing tyranny as a disease infecting the land while her personal memory remains healthy.

---

**5. Symbolism & Central Motif**

- **Quote 1:** `"I have no passport, there’s no way back at all"`
    - **Context:** Opening line of Stanza 3, stating her legal and physical inability to return home.
    - **Theme Link:** The `"passport"` symbolises official state recognition and legal belonging; lacking one signifies her permanent physical exile.
- **Quote 2:** `"It tastes of sunlight."`
    - **Context:** Closing line of Stanza 2, describing her native language that she carries in exile.
    - **Theme Link:** `"Sunlight"` acts as the central motif for uncorrupted, warm memory that keeps her cultural identity alive despite political bans.

---

**6. Visual & Tactile Imagery**

- **Quote 1:** `"The white streets of that city, the graceful slopes"`
    - **Context:** Stanza 2, where the speaker visualises the landscape of her home city in pristine detail.
    - **Theme Link:** Vivid visual imagery (`"white streets"`, `"graceful slopes"`) captures the pure quality of her memories untouched by war.
- **Quote 2:** `"I comb its hair and love its shining eyes."`
    - **Context:** Stanza 3, describing her physical interaction with the personified memory of her city.
    - **Theme Link:** Tactile and visual imagery (`"comb its hair"`, `"shining eyes"`) gives physical warmth and life to an abstract memory.

---

**7. Synesthesia (Mixing Senses)**

- **Quote 1:** `"It tastes of sunlight."`
    - **Context:** Stanza 2, describing her mother tongue that she refuses to stop speaking.
    - **Theme Link:** Blends the sense of taste (`"tastes"`) with visual light (`"sunlight"`), showing that her language and culture are a visceral, nourishing part of her inner identity.
- **Quote 2:** `"Soon I shall have every coloured molecule of it."`
    - **Context:** Stanza 2, describing how her childhood grammar opens up and fills her mind in exile.
    - **Theme Link:** Blends visual color (`"coloured"`) with abstract language and science (`"molecule"`), illustrating how vibrant and complete her mental sanctuary is.

**8. Zoomorphism / Predatory Imagery**

- **Quote 1:** `"They accuse me of absence, they circle me."`
    - **Context:** Stanza 3, describing the hostile host citizens surrounding her in her new city.
    - **Theme Link:** Uses predatory zoomorphism (`"circle me"`) to portray the local population as wild beasts hunting down a helpless outsider.
- **Quote 2:** `"They mutter death, and my shadow falls as evidence of sunlight."`
    - **Context:** Stanza 3, facing the dangerous whispers and hostility of her host environment.
    - **Theme Link:** Captures the predatory, threatening atmosphere (`"mutter death"`) surrounding the exile, against which her memory stands as her sole defense.

**9. Status-Laden Diction / War vs. Authoritarian Diction**

- **Quote 1:** `"glow even clearer as time rolls its tanks"`
    - **Context:** Stanza 2, describing how time and political destruction fail to dim her memory.
    - **Theme Link:** Militarised diction (`"tanks"`) highlights state aggression and war, contrasting against the speaker's glowing internal sanctuary.
- **Quote 2:** `"It may by now be a lie, banned by the state"`
    - **Context:** Stanza 2, acknowledging that her native language has been outlawed by the new regime at home.
    - **Theme Link:** Authoritarian diction (`"banned by the state"`) underscores how oppressive governments attempt to destroy culture, which she resists in memory.

**10. Repetition / Anaphora**

- **Quote 1:** `"They accuse me of absence, they circle me."`
    - **Context:** Stanza 3, as the speaker describes being singled out by host citizens.
    - **Theme Link:** Repeating the hostile pronoun `"They"` emphasizes the overwhelming, collective threat of the host society against a solitary exile.
- **Quote 2:** `"They accuse me of being dark in their free city."`
    - **Context:** Stanza 3, directly following the previous line in the third stanza.
    - **Theme Link:** Anaphora of `"They accuse me of..."` stresses the relentless, repeated persecution she faces simply for retaining her distinct identity.

**11. Tone (Nostalgic/Idealised vs. Hostile/Persecuted)**

- **Quote 1:** `"my original view, the bright, filled paperweight."`
    - **Context:** Stanza 1, reflecting on her unshakeable early perception of her hometown.
    - **Theme Link (Idealised/Nostalgic Tone):** Warm, nostalgic tone reflects her total devotion to an unblemished past that sustains her in exile.
- **Quote 2:** `"They accuse me of being dark in their free city."`
    - **Context:** Stanza 3, confronting the discrimination of her host environment.
    - **Theme Link (Hostile/Persecuted Tone):** A dark, threatened tone conveys the fear, vulnerability, and bitterness of being racialised and ostracised.

**12. Alliteration & Sound Devices (Sibilance vs. Harsh Phonetics)**

- **Quote 1:** `"The white streets of that city, the graceful slopes"`
    - **Context:** Stanza 2, describing the pristine streets of her remembered home.
    - **Theme Link (Sibilance & Euphony):** Sibilance of the **'s'** sound (`"streets... slopes"`) combined with smooth liquid **'l'** sounds creates a peaceful cadence mirroring her mental sanctuary.
- **Quote 2:** `"They accuse me of being dark in their free city."`
    - **Context:** Stanza 3, conveying the sharp hostility of host citizens.
    - **Theme Link (Harsh Plosives & Dental Sounds):** Biting plosives and dental consonants (**'d'**, **'k'**, **'t'**, **'f'**: `"dark... free city"`) create a harsh, jarring rhythm reflecting the violence of xenophobia.
