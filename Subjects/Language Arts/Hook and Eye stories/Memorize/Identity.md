# **1.** **Identity** **(by Latha)**

### **1. Setting & Spatial Contrast**

- **Point 1: India as a Nostalgic, Vibrant Homeland Setting**
    - `"She thought about the weddings she had attended back home in India. What riotous fun! Everyone would be bubbling over with excitement and happiness."` (Page 138)
    - `"She recalled with longing the joy she had had with her friends, teasing the bride and groom... giggling like small girls. She had no such intimate friends here."` (Page 138)
- **Point 2: Singapore's Oppressive, Humid Domestic Setting**
    - `"It was a hot day and the humidity hung heavy in the room. She longed to slide into her favourite chair... But that wasn't possible... a whole load of housework awaited her."` (Page 130)
    - `"The day spent in the kitchen had taken its toll and her appetite had shrivelled."` (Page 133)

### **2. Situational & Character Irony**

- **Point 1: Situational Irony (Foreign Qualifications & Citizenship Invalidated)**
    - `"If she had pursued her MSc here in Singapore instead of in Tamil Nadu, her salary would have been thrice or quadruple what she was currently earning."` (Page 131)
    - `"Your father has only A-Levels. I have passed MSc Maths."` / `"You studied in India. You wouldn't understand a thing about Maths in Singapore."` (Page 135)
- **Point 2: Character Irony (Patriarchal Hypocrisy)**
    - `"Don't forget that I married a girl from India because I like my wife to be conservative and feminine."` (Page 136)
    - `"openly stating that her "India ways" were not suitable"` (Page 133)

### **3. Characterisation & Stereotyping**

- **Point 1: Characterising the Protagonist (Self-Sacrificing, Physical Erosion)**
    - `"With her dishevelled hair and face drenched in perspiration, she did look like a maid."` (Page 134)
    - `"Do I look like an Indian or Sri Lankan maid?"` (Page 134)
- **Point 2: Characterising the Antagonists (Prejudiced Gatekeepers)**
    - `"You come from India? Come to do housework?"` (Page 129)
    - `"If I were like you, people would also tease me as a country bumpkin from India."` (Page 136)

### **4. Juxtaposition & Direct Contrast**

- **Point 1: External Juxtaposition (Legal Claim vs. Social Gatekeeping)**
    - `"I am a Singaporean."` (Page 137)
    - `"You say you're a Singaporean. But you don't know Malay?"` (Page 129)
- **Point 2: Internal Juxtaposition (India's Communal Warmth vs. Singapore Isolation)**
    - `"She thought about the weddings she had attended back home in India. What riotous fun! Everyone would be bubbling over with excitement and happiness."` (Page 138)
    - `"She had no such intimate friends here. Anyway, weddings in Singapore were no fun."` (Page 138)

### **5. Dialogue & Diction**

- **Point 1: Status-Laden Local Dialogue**
    - `"From India means must be maid?"` (Page 129)
    - `"You come from India? Come to do housework?"` (Page 129)
- **Point 2: Imperative Domestic Speech**
    - `"Hey, I don't want your thosai... Make me some French toast."` (Page 132)
    - `"If I were like you, people would also tease me as a country bumpkin from India."` (Page 136)

### **6. Symbolism & Physical Motifs**

- **Point 1: State Symbol (The Pink NRIC)**
    - `"Her distracted eyes fell on the pink Singapore identity card placed neatly in the purse pocket. It seemed to smile at her smugly."` (Pages 137–138)
- **Point 2: Domestic & Reflection Symbols (Yellow Thaali & Bathroom Mirror)**
    - `"Apart from the yellow thread that was her thaali, the marital necklace... her only other jewellery was a thin chain"` (Page 134)
    - `"With her dishevelled hair and face drenched in perspiration, she did look like a maid."` (Page 134)

### **7. Narrative Perspective & Internal Monologue**

- **Point 1: Focalising Societal Exclusion & Tragic Self-Blame**
    - `"Is it me or them? Maybe I am not easy to get along with. Or have I not adapted to the ways of life here?"` (Page 137)
    - `"I am a Singaporean."` (Page 137)
- **Point 2: Focalising Domestic Exploitation & Trapped Helplessness**
    - `"After all, had they not brought her from India for this?"` (Page 132)
    - `"Anyway, do I have the luxury of quarrelling with my husband and running home to my mother?"` (Page 136)

### **8. Structural Climax & Unresolved Ending**

- **Point 1: Climax of Societal Rejection (Weeping with NRIC)**
    - `"I am a Singaporean! she reminded herself."` (Page 137)
    - `"But she could not stem the tears that coursed down her cheeks."` (Page 138)
- **Point 2: Unresolved Ending & Circular Trap**
    - `"She had to go to the salon... She needed to coach her son for his Maths test..."` (Page 138)
    - `"Dismissed her sentimentality for the happier times in India and just get on with her miserable life in Singapore."` (Page 138)

# **2.** **Margarine and the Syrian Refugee Project** **(by Leonora Liow)**

### **1. Setting & Spatial Contrast**

- **Point 1: Upper SES Privilege (Landed Mansion Estate)**
    - `"Jo shrank to a thin sliver as her three-storeyed mansion loomed larger and larger and enveloped her."` (Page 51)
    - `"drowned out by the heavy luxury of the estate. We drove past many other formidable mansions, but saw hardly any humans"` (Page 51)
- **Point 2: Heartland Working Class (HDB Flat Interior & Void Deck)**
    - `"Nabilah lived in a corner unit on the fourth floor of a HDB flat."` (Page 51)
    - `"We were on the fourth floor, so we could hear everything that was happening downstairs. School buses dropping off students, kids playing football in the void deck"` (Page 53)

### **2. Situational Irony**

- **Point 1: Global vs. Local Empathy Paradox**
    - `"You need some perspective Nabs. You keep forgetting about the Syrian refugees, how little they have, and how lucky you are."` (Page 55)
    - `"We stayed at the Four Seasons in Damascus... I guess your cousin wasn't staying there too?"` (Page 47)
- **Point 2: Institutional Irony (Education as a Class Divider)**
    - `"I thought you'd be able to step outside yourself and empathise with others—"` (Page 55)
    - `"To think we spent so much time working on our project... We didn't hear from her again."` (Page 55)
- **Point 3: Ideological Irony (The National Pledge Omission)**
    - `"As Nabilah had said before, Margarine belonged to a different world. Regardless of race, language or religion. But what about money?"` (Page 53)
    - `"I guess that's where people like you go to school," Nabilah said.` (Page 54)

### **3. Characterisation & Stereotyping**

- **Point 1: Upper-Class Unconscious Insularity**
    - `"To you guys ministers probably make a lot of money, but you know, there are many other jobs that give you more money."` (Page 49)
    - `"Well I think it's obvious that you're not as rich as my dad," Jo said matter-of-factly.` (Page 49)
- **Point 2: Working-Class Defensive Resentment**
    - `"Rich kid. Why did she transfer to Bukit Katak?"` (Page 43)
    - `"I don't think that anybody who goes to Bukit Katak now, can become like your dad in the future."` (Page 54)

### **4. Juxtaposition of Material Culture & Diction**

- **Point 1: Consumer Privilege Diction**
    - `"It smelled very posh, not like any of the normal deodorant sprays we used, the ones that you would find in Watsons."` (Page 43)
    - `"well-worn sneakers had the faded outline of an embroidered pattern... they didn't have any branches in Singapore."` (Page 43)
- **Point 2: Material Insecurity & Self-Consciousness**
    - `"I felt self-conscious of my clunky shoes, with their thick, ungainly soles."` (Page 43)
    - `"wore our school uniform better than us, I thought with envy and some resignation"` (Page 45)

### **5. Dialogue & Speech Patterns**

- **Point 1: Elite Detached Tone**
    - `"it's not my fault that you don't have what I do... You have to stop grudging others for what they have and you don't."` (Pages 54–55)
    - `"I don't fit in this education system, I have no choice," Margarine said.` (Page 54)
- **Point 2: Heartland Confrontational Rhetoric**
    - `"That's where people like you live," Nabilah told Margarine. "They put them in a different GRC too."` (Page 53)
    - `"Are you crazy? Do you know how many options you have that we don't?"` (Page 54)

### **6. Symbolism & Metaphor**

- **Point 1: The Spilt Fishball & Noodle Bowl**
    - `"The embarrassed clot of noodles lay helplessly on the ground... A fishball fell out of his clasp and dropped to the ground, rolling towards the overturned bowl."` (Pages 54–55)
    - `"Margarine picked up his bowl and the fishball. She didn't come to school the next day. We didn't hear from her again."` (Page 55)
- **Point 2: The Name "Margarine" as a Symbolic Substitute**
    - `"Oei, Margarine! Why, your mother don't like kaya issit?"` (Page 43)
    - `"It's okay if I call you Margarine? I think it's very cute. Your real name is too hard for me lah."` (Page 43)
- **Point 3: The Syrian Project & National Pledge Metaphor**
    - `"As Nabilah had said before, Margarine belonged to a different world. Regardless of race, language or religion. But what about money?"` (Page 53)
    - `"after all, how can somebody who doesn't care about her friends truly care about people she doesn't know?"` (Page 55)

### **7. First-Person Narrative Perspective & Focalisation**

- **Point 1: Narrative Mediation & Breakdown of Initial Prejudice**
    - `"I know you're looking," her slightly hostile smile seemed to say.` (Page 43)
    - `"I told Nabilah that at the end of the day, Margarine was our friend."` (Page 55)
- **Point 2: Retrospective Focalisation & Permanent Separation**
    - `"It was clear that Nabilah was really upset about everything, so I never brought Margarine up with her again."` (Page 55)
    - `"But every time I read about Syria in the news, I wonder about Margarine, and if she thinks about us."` (Page 55)

### **8. Structural Climax & Unresolved Ending**

- **Point 1: Climactic Canteen Explosion**
    - `"A small commotion ensued as an angry auntie emerged with a mop and a sweaty face."` (Page 54)
    - `"it's not my fault that you don't have what I do... You have to stop grudging others for what they have and you don't."` (Pages 54–55)
- **Point 2: Unresolved Departure**
    - `"Margarine picked up his bowl and the fishball. She didn't come to school the next day. We didn't hear from her again."` (Page 55)
    - `"Why should we care about her, if she doesn't care about us?"` (Page 55)

# **3.** **The Émigrée** **(by Carol Rumens)**

### **1. Structural Devices**

- **Point 1: Stanza Tag Word / Refrain ("Sunlight")**
    - `"It tastes of sunlight."` (Page 125)
    - `"and my shadow falls as evidence of sunlight."` (Page 125)
- **Point 2: Structural Volta / Shift in Stanza 3**
    - `"I have no passport, there’s no way back at all"` (Page 125)
    - `"They accuse me of being dark in their free city."` (Page 125)

### **2. Metaphor & Imagery**

- **Point 1: Paperweight & Memory Metaphors**
    - `"my original view, the bright, filled paperweight."` (Page 125)
    - `"That child’s vocabulary I carried here like a hollow doll, opens and spills a grammar."` (Page 125)
- **Point 2: Personification of the City**
    - `"I comb its hair and love its shining eyes."` (Page 125)
    - `"It may be at war, it may be sick with tyrants, but I am branded by an impression of sunlight."` (Page 125)

### **3. Paradox & Irony**

- **Point 1: The Shadow Paradox**
    - `"They accuse me of being dark in their free city."` (Page 125)
    - `"They mutter death, and my shadow falls as evidence of sunlight."` (Page 125)
