### **1. Setting & Spatial Contrast (Landed Estate vs. HDB Heartland)**

- **Quote 1:** `"Jo shrank to a thin sliver as her three-storeyed mansion loomed larger and larger and enveloped her."`
- **Quote 2:** `"drowned out by the heavy luxury of the estate. We drove past many other formidable mansions, but saw hardly any humans"`
- **Analysis & Link:** Personifying the mansion as something that "loomed larger" and "enveloped her" creates a physical fortress, while the phrase "heavy luxury" conveys a suffocating, artificial isolation where human presence is absent. **This reveals about upper-class privilege that spatial wealth acts as a physical barrier that cuts off the elite from ordinary human interactions, rendering them blind to the lived realities of lower socioeconomic classes.**
- **Quote 1:** `"Nabilah lived in a corner unit on the fourth floor of a HDB flat."`
- **Quote 2:** `"We were on the fourth floor, so we could hear everything that was happening downstairs. School buses dropping off students, kids playing football in the void deck"`
- **Analysis & Link:** The specific spatial detail of a "corner unit" combined with the sensory auditory imagery of "school buses" and "kids playing... downstairs" portrays a communal, unshielded living space where private life constantly overlaps with public activity. **This reveals about the heartland working class that lower socioeconomic existence is defined by shared, collective spaces, directly contrasting against the guarded, silent isolation of elite wealth.**
